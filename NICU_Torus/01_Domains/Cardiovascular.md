# Cardiovascular

## Overview

The Cardiovascular domain covers the hemodynamic architecture of the fetal and neonatal circulation, the dramatic vascular and structural transitions that occur at birth, and the diseases that arise when these transitions fail or cardiac anatomy is congenitally malformed. Core physiology (fetal circulation, cardiac output determinants, Frank-Starling, PVR) underpins all clinical management. The domain is deeply intertwined with Respiratory — PPHN, PDA, and transitional hemodynamics are joint territory. It is a high-yield board domain spanning basic physiology through complex congenital heart disease management.

## Cross-Domain Links

- [[Respiratory]] — [[Pulmonary Vascular Resistance]] and [[Transitional Circulation]] are shared territory: the PVR drop at birth is driven by oxygenation and lung inflation. See [[Extrauterine Pulmonary Transition]] and [[Pulmonary Hypertension]] in the Respiratory domain.
- [[Respiratory]] — [[PPHN Hemodynamics]] produces right-to-left ductal and atrial shunting that mimics and co-exists with respiratory failure. See [[Pulmonary Hypertension]] and [[RDS]] for the overlap.
- [[Respiratory]] — CDH creates both pulmonary hypoplasia (Respiratory) and severe pulmonary hypertension with RV failure (Cardiovascular). Management requires integrating both domains.
- [[Maternal-Fetal]] — Placental insufficiency, maternal diabetes, fetal arrhythmias, and hydrops all have primary cardiovascular manifestations.
- [[Pharmacology]] — Prostaglandin E1 pharmacology, vasoactive agent mechanisms (dopamine, dobutamine, milrinone, vasopressin), and iNO delivery are detailed in the Pharmacology domain.
- [[Endocrine-Metabolic-Thermal]] — Hypocalcemia and hypoglycemia both impair myocardial contractility; adrenal insufficiency causes vasopressor-resistant hypotension.
- [[Hematology-Bilirubin]] — Polycythemia raises PVR and can precipitate or worsen PPHN; anemia reduces oxygen delivery and increases cardiac output demand.

## Board Focus Areas

- Fetal circulation: parallel circuit anatomy, preferential streaming of oxygenated blood to brain and heart, role of ductus venosus / foramen ovale / ductus arteriosus
- Transitional circulation: what drives PVR drop (O2, lung inflation, loss of placental prostaglandins), how the series circuit is established
- Cardiac output determinants: preload, afterload, contractility, heart rate — neonatal specifics (rate-dependent CO, limited preload reserve)
- Frank-Starling: why volume loading has a ceiling in neonates; how the immature myocardium differs
- PPHN: definition, etiologies (maladaptation, maldevelopment, underdevelopment), echo criteria, pre/post-ductal SpO2 differential, iNO mechanism and indications, ECMO threshold
- PDA in the preterm: pathophysiology, criteria for hemodynamic significance, treatment options and their evidence base (TIPP trial, PDA-TOLERATE trial), risks of treatment vs. non-treatment
- Congenital heart disease approach: five-step framework (cyanosis, murmur, shock, differential sats, four-extremity BPs); hyperoxia test interpretation; ductal-dependent lesions and PGE1 dosing
- Vasoactive pharmacology: dopamine receptor pharmacology by dose range, dobutamine vs dopamine, milrinone mechanism, vasopressin role in refractory shock, hydrocortisone for vasopressor-resistant hypotension
- Chromosomal/syndrome associations with specific cardiac lesions
- Echo in the NICU: functional echocardiography vs structural echo; TR jet, septal position, ductal flow direction, LA:Ao ratio
