## 1. Identity

- Fetal circulation is a **parallel** circuit in which both ventricles eject blood into the systemic circulation simultaneously
- Three obligate shunts maintain this architecture: **ductus venosus**, **foramen ovale**, **ductus arteriosus**
- The **placenta** serves as the organ of gas exchange, not the lungs
- The right ventricle is the dominant pump, contributing ~65% of combined ventricular output (CVO)
- Lungs receive only ~10% of CVO due to high pulmonary vascular resistance

---

## 2. Taxonomy / Developmental Placement

- Active from ~8 weeks gestation through birth
- Maintained by: low fetal arterial PO₂, elevated PVR, PGE2, and prostacyclin from placenta
- Gestational axis: **fetal**
- Transitions abruptly at birth → see [[Transitional Circulation]]

---

## 3. Structure

**Three Shunts:**

- **Ductus venosus (DV)**
  - Connects umbilical vein → IVC, bypassing hepatic sinusoids
  - Delivers oxygenated umbilical venous blood (UV saturation ~80%) preferentially toward the foramen ovale
  - ~50% of umbilical venous return bypasses the liver via DV

- **Foramen ovale (FO)**
  - Interatrial communication allowing oxygenated IVC blood to cross from RA → LA
  - Flap-valve mechanism; remains patent by RA > LA pressure gradient in fetal life
  - Delivers relatively oxygenated blood to left heart and coronary/cerebral circulation

- **Ductus arteriosus (DA)**
  - Connects main pulmonary artery → descending aorta
  - Carries deoxygenated RV output away from high-resistance lungs into systemic circulation
  - Maintained patent by PGE2, low PO₂, and endogenous prostaglandins from placenta

**Parallel Circuit:**
- Both ventricles eject to body simultaneously (not in series as in postnatal life)
- RV → pulmonary artery → DA → descending aorta → placenta/body
- LV → ascending aorta → coronary, cerebral, upper body circulation

---

## 4. Function

**Oxygen Saturation at Key Points:**

| Location | Approximate SaO₂ |
|---|---|
| Umbilical vein (UV) | ~80% |
| Ductus venosus | ~80% |
| IVC (mixed) | ~67% |
| Ascending aorta / LV output | ~65% |
| Descending aorta | ~58% |
| Umbilical arteries | ~55% |

**Preferential Streaming:**
- Oxygenated UV blood → DV → IVC → FO → LA → LV → ascending aorta → brain and coronary arteries
- Deoxygenated SVC blood → RA → RV → PA → DA → descending aorta → placenta for re-oxygenation
- The brain and heart receive the most oxygenated blood in the fetal circuit (evolutionary advantage)

**Right Ventricular Dominance:**
- RV contributes ~65% of CVO; LV contributes ~35%
- RV wall thickness equals or exceeds LV in the fetus
- Reflected on neonatal ECG as right axis deviation and dominant R waves in right precordial leads

**Low-Resistance Placental Circuit:**
- Placenta acts as a low-impedance shunt for the fetal systemic circulation
- Cord clamping at birth removes this circuit → SVR rises acutely

---

## 5. Dysfunction / Pathophysiology

- **Loss of any shunt in utero** can be catastrophic (e.g., premature DA constriction from NSAIDs → fetal right heart failure)
- **Placental insufficiency** → reduced UV saturation → chronic fetal hypoxia, IUGR
- **Fetal arrhythmias** → impair the HR-dependent fetal CO
- **Hydrops fetalis** → failure of fetal circulatory adequacy (cardiac, lymphatic, or anemic etiology)
- **Congenital heart disease** may be well-compensated in fetal life but decompensates at transition → see [[Transitional Circulation]]

---

## 6. Clinical Phenotype

- Normal fetal circulation is not clinically visible; assessed by **fetal echocardiography** and **Doppler** studies
- Absent/reversed end-diastolic flow in umbilical artery → increased placental resistance, fetal compromise
- Elevated DV pulsatility index or reversed a-wave → impaired right heart function, severe fetal distress
- Fetal bradycardia or tachyarrhythmia → reduced CO → hydrops

---

## 7. Differential / Distinctions

| Feature | Fetal Circulation | Postnatal Circulation |
|---|---|---|
| Circuit type | Parallel | Series |
| Gas exchange organ | Placenta | Lungs |
| PVR | High | Low |
| Dominant ventricle | Right | Left |
| Shunts | 3 active | Closed |
| DA flow direction | Right → Left | Left → Right (if patent) |

---

## 8. Diagnosis

- **Fetal echocardiography**: structural assessment, shunt patency, ventricular function
- **Doppler velocimetry**: umbilical artery, MCA, DV — markers of fetal well-being
- **Fetal heart rate monitoring**: indirect marker of circulatory adequacy
- **Fetal MRI**: adjunct for complex anatomy (e.g., CDH, brain malformations)

---

## 9. Management

- No intervention required for normal fetal circulation
- **Fetal interventions** for selected lesions:
  - Balloon atrial septostomy for intact atrial septum with HLHS
  - Fetal cardiac interventions (aortic valvuloplasty) at specialized centers
- **Avoid maternal NSAIDs** after 32 weeks → risk of premature DA constriction
- Monitor high-risk fetuses (IUGR, twin-twin transfusion, CHD) with serial Doppler

---

## 10. Therapeutics

- **Fetal SVT**: maternal digoxin, flecainide, sotalol to restore fetal CO
- **Fetal anemia (alloimmune)**: intrauterine transfusion to restore oxygen-carrying capacity
- **TTTS**: laser ablation of placental anastomoses
- **Indomethacin tocolysis**: use with caution >32 weeks; monitor DA flow with Doppler

---

## 11. Complications / Outcomes

- **Premature DA closure**: right heart failure, pulmonary hypertension, hydrops → can be fatal
- **Fetal hypoxia from placental failure**: IUGR, neurologic injury, stillbirth
- **Hydrops fetalis**: high mortality without intervention; etiology-dependent management
- **Transition failure**: if fetal shunts do not close appropriately → PPHN, cyanosis, shock

---

## 12. Associations / Genetics / Maternal-Fetal Links

- **Maternal NSAID use** → premature ductal closure
- **Maternal SLE/anti-Ro antibodies** → congenital heart block (affects fetal HR and CO)
- **Maternal diabetes** → fetal hyperinsulinism → hypertrophic cardiomyopathy
- **Placental mosaicism** → IUGR with abnormal umbilical Doppler
- **Chromosomal abnormalities** (Trisomy 21, 18, 13, Turner) → structural CHD affecting fetal circulation

---

## 13. Context / Board Framing

- **"Which vessel carries the most oxygenated blood in the fetus?"** → Umbilical vein (~80% SaO₂)
- **"Which structure preferentially directs oxygenated blood to the brain?"** → Foramen ovale (IVC streaming through FO → LA → LV → ascending aorta)
- **"What maintains DA patency in the fetus?"** → PGE2, low PO₂
- **"What closes the foramen ovale at birth?"** → LA pressure exceeds RA pressure (increased pulmonary venous return + loss of placental venous return)
- **"Why does the fetal ECG show right ventricular dominance?"** → RV does more work in fetal life; wall thickness reflects workload

---

## 14. Edge Pearl

- The left hepatic lobe receives better-oxygenated blood than the right hepatic lobe in the fetus, because oxygenated UV blood preferentially streams through the left portal vein before entering the DV. This may explain why the left hepatic lobe is relatively larger in neonates.
- Fetal hemoglobin (HbF) has a **leftward O₂-dissociation curve** (higher O₂ affinity) — this allows HbF to extract O₂ from maternal HbA across the placenta even at low PO₂ gradients, enabling fetal survival with descending aortic PO₂ of only ~20-25 mmHg.
