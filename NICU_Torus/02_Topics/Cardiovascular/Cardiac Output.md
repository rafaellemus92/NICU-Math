## 1. Identity

- **Cardiac output (CO)** = Heart Rate × Stroke Volume (HR × SV)
- In neonates, CO is primarily **heart rate-dependent** due to limited stroke volume reserve
- Normal neonatal CO: **150-300 mL/kg/min** (significantly higher per kg than adults due to high metabolic demand)
- CO must be matched to tissue oxygen delivery (DO₂ = CO × CaO₂)
- Assessment is challenging at the bedside; echocardiography is the gold standard in the NICU

---

## 2. Taxonomy / Developmental Placement

- Relevant across all gestational ages in the postnatal period
- Preterm infants have additional constraints: immature myocardium, transitional shunts affecting flow distribution
- SVC flow is used as a surrogate for systemic CO in preterm infants (avoids confounding by DA shunt)
- Links to: [[Preload]], [[Afterload]], [[Frank-Starling Principle]]

---

## 3. Structure

**Determinants of Cardiac Output:**

1. **Heart Rate**
   - Primary determinant in neonates (SV reserve is limited)
   - Normal neonatal HR: 120-160 bpm
   - Bradycardia is poorly tolerated; tachycardia above ~220 bpm reduces diastolic filling time → reduced SV

2. **Stroke Volume (SV)**
   - Volume ejected per beat; determined by:
     - **Preload**: end-diastolic volume (Frank-Starling mechanism)
     - **Afterload**: resistance against which ventricle ejects
     - **Contractility**: intrinsic myocardial force generation
   - Neonatal SV: ~1.5-2.5 mL/kg

3. **Preload** → see [[Preload]]
   - Venous return, blood volume, venous tone, intrathoracic pressure

4. **Afterload** → see [[Afterload]]
   - SVR (LV afterload), PVR (RV afterload)
   - Immature myocardium is highly afterload-sensitive

5. **Contractility**
   - Intrinsic myocardial function independent of loading conditions
   - Impaired by: hypoxia, acidosis, hypoglycemia, hypocalcemia, sepsis, asphyxia

---

## 4. Function

- CO distributes oxygen to organs proportional to their metabolic demand
- Brain (~15-25% of CO), heart (~5%), gut, kidneys, skin
- Right heart CO = left heart CO in steady state (no shunts)
- **In preterm with significant PDA**: left heart CO is inflated by pulmonary recirculation; SVC flow more accurately reflects cerebral perfusion

**SVC Flow as Systemic CO Surrogate:**
- Normal SVC flow: 40-120 mL/kg/min
- Low SVC flow (<40 mL/kg/min) in the first 24h → associated with IVH, neurodevelopmental impairment
- Measured by echocardiography (Doppler at SVC-RA junction)

**Combined Ventricular Output (CVO):**
- In fetal/transitional period: RV + LV output combined
- Fetal CVO: ~450-500 mL/kg/min
- Postnatal: ~300-400 mL/kg/min (lower due to loss of placenta)

---

## 5. Dysfunction / Pathophysiology

- **Low CO** → inadequate tissue O₂ delivery → anaerobic metabolism → lactic acidosis → end-organ dysfunction
- **Causes of low CO in neonates:**
  - Hypovolemia (hemorrhage, third spacing)
  - Myocardial dysfunction (asphyxia, sepsis, metabolic derangements)
  - Arrhythmia (bradycardia reduces CO; SVT reduces SV by impairing filling)
  - High afterload (PPHN, severe PDA with pulmonary overcirculation)
  - Cardiac tamponade (pericardial effusion)
  - Tension pneumothorax (impedes venous return)

- **High CO states**: sepsis (early), arteriovenous malformations, severe anemia
- **Dissociation between BP and CO**: neonatal BP correlates poorly with CO; normal BP does not exclude low CO

---

## 6. Clinical Phenotype

**Signs of Low CO:**
- Pallor, mottling, prolonged capillary refill (>3 seconds)
- Cool extremities with warm core (temperature gradient)
- Weak pulses (low pulse pressure)
- Tachycardia (compensatory)
- Oliguria (<1 mL/kg/hr)
- Metabolic acidosis, elevated lactate
- Hypotension (late sign; may be absent early)

**Signs of High CO / Hyperdynamic State:**
- Bounding pulses, wide pulse pressure
- Tachycardia
- Warm extremities
- Seen in: PDA with large left-to-right shunt, sepsis, AVM

---

## 7. Differential / Distinctions

| Condition | CO | BP | SVR |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hypovolemia | Low | Low | High |
| Cardiogenic shock | Low | Low | High |
| Distributive (sepsis) | High early / Low late | Low | Low |
| PPHN | Variable | Normal/Low | Variable |
| Large PDA | LV high, SVC low | Wide pulse pressure | Low |

---

## 8. Diagnosis

**Echocardiography (Gold Standard):**
- LV output: calculated from LVOT VTI × CSA × HR
- RV output: calculated from RVOT VTI × CSA × HR
- SVC flow: Doppler-derived
- Fractional shortening (FS): normal 28-40%; rough contractility marker
- Ejection fraction (EF): normal >55%

**Near-Infrared Spectroscopy (NIRS):**
- Cerebral and somatic tissue oxygenation as surrogate for regional DO₂
- Low cerebral NIRS (<55-65%) → may indicate reduced cerebral CO

**Indirect Markers:**
- Lactate >4 mmol/L → tissue hypoperfusion
- Base excess < -8 → metabolic acidosis from inadequate DO₂
- Urine output, capillary refill, color, pulse quality

---

## 9. Management

**Treat Underlying Cause:**
- Volume: 10 mL/kg NS bolus if hypovolemic (assess response)
- Correct metabolic derangements: glucose, calcium, pH
- Treat infection
- Optimize ventilation (high MAP impedes venous return → reduces preload)

**Vasoactive Support:**
- Dopamine: first-line in many centers; increases HR and contractility at low-mid doses; increases SVR at high doses
- Dobutamine: increases contractility and HR; reduces afterload
- Epinephrine: for refractory shock; potent inotrope and vasopressor
- Norepinephrine: for vasodilatory shock (sepsis); increases SVR

---

## 10. Therapeutics

- **Volume**: isotonic crystalloid (NS or LR) 10 mL/kg; packed RBCs if anemic
- **Dopamine** 5-20 mcg/kg/min: DA1 receptors at low dose (renal vasodilation), β1 at mid dose (inotrope), α1 at high dose (vasopressor)
- **Dobutamine** 5-20 mcg/kg/min: β1/β2 inotrope; reduces afterload; useful in cardiogenic shock
- **Epinephrine** 0.01-1 mcg/kg/min: potent β1/β2/α1; used when dopamine/dobutamine insufficient
- **Milrinone** 0.25-0.75 mcg/kg/min: PDE3 inhibitor; lusitrope + inotrope; reduces PVR and SVR; useful in PPHN with poor RV function
- **Hydrocortisone**: for vasopressor-refractory hypotension (adrenal insufficiency or catecholamine sensitization)

---

## 11. Complications / Outcomes

- **Untreated low CO** → ischemic injury to brain (IVH, PVL), kidneys (AKI), gut (NEC), myocardium
- **Low SVC flow in first 24h** → independently associated with increased IVH and poor neurodevelopmental outcomes in VLBW infants
- **Excessive volume resuscitation** → pulmonary edema, worsening lung function, IVH
- **Vasoactive drug toxicity**: tachyarrhythmia, hypertension, peripheral ischemia at high doses

---

## 12. Associations / Genetics / Maternal-Fetal Links

- **Perinatal asphyxia**: myocardial ischemia → cardiogenic low CO; tricuspid regurgitation, right heart failure
- **Maternal diabetes**: hypertrophic cardiomyopathy → obstructive and diastolic dysfunction → low CO
- **Prematurity**: immature myocardium + transitional shunts complicate CO assessment and management
- **Twin-to-twin transfusion**: recipient twin → polycythemia, high CO state; donor → anemia, low CO

---

## 13. Context / Board Framing

- **"CO = HR × SV"** — know the formula and its components
- **"In neonates, CO is primarily HR-dependent"** — because SV reserve is limited
- **"Normal neonatal CO"** → 150-300 mL/kg/min
- **"Why does normal BP not exclude low CO in neonates?"** → High SVR can maintain BP despite low SV (e.g., cardiogenic shock with compensatory vasoconstriction)
- **"What is SVC flow used for?"** → Estimate of systemic CO in preterm with PDA (avoids confounding by left-to-right shunt inflating LVCO)
- **"Low SVC flow in first 24h predicts what outcome?"** → IVH and neurodevelopmental impairment in VLBW infants

---

## 14. Edge Pearl

- In preterm infants with a large PDA, **left ventricular output (LVO) overestimates true systemic blood flow** because it includes pulmonary recirculation. SVC flow is therefore a more reliable marker of actual cerebral and systemic perfusion. A neonate can have a "normal" LVO of 200 mL/kg/min while having a critically low SVC flow of 30 mL/kg/min — a scenario that is physiologically dangerous but clinically easy to miss without targeted echo assessment.
