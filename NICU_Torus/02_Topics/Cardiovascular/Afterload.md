## 1. Identity

- **Afterload** is the wall stress experienced by the ventricle during ejection — the resistance against which the myocardium must contract to eject blood
- Formally: wall stress = (pressure × radius) / (2 × wall thickness) — LaPlace's Law
- Clinically approximated by vascular resistance: **SVR** for the LV, **PVR** for the RV
- The neonatal myocardium is **highly afterload-sensitive** due to immaturity of contractile proteins and reduced myocardial reserve

---

## 2. Taxonomy / Developmental Placement

- One of four determinants of [[Cardiac Output]]
- Closely linked to [[Pulmonary Vascular Resistance]] for the right ventricle
- Afterload changes dramatically during [[Transitional Circulation]] (cord clamping → acute SVR rise)
- Gestational axis: preterm and term postnatal
- Board pearl: **immature myocardium is more afterload-sensitive than adult myocardium**

---

## 3. Structure

**Components of Afterload:**

1. **Systemic Vascular Resistance (SVR)** — LV afterload
   - SVR = (MAP - CVP) / CO × 80 (dynes·s/cm⁵)
   - Increased by: vasopressors (norepinephrine, dopamine at high dose), hypothermia, pain/stress
   - Decreased by: vasodilators (milrinone, nitroprusside), sepsis, fever

2. **Pulmonary Vascular Resistance (PVR)** — RV afterload
   - PVR = (mPAP - PCWP) / CO × 80
   - High in fetal life; falls dramatically at birth
   - Elevated in PPHN, CDH, RDS → right ventricular pressure overload
   - See [[Pulmonary Vascular Resistance]]

3. **Impedance** (more complete afterload descriptor)
   - Accounts for pulsatile flow resistance, not just mean resistance
   - Clinically less practical but more accurate

4. **Ventricular wall stress**
   - Afterload increases as ventricular radius increases (dilated ventricle faces higher wall stress per LaPlace)
   - Ventricular hypertrophy reduces wall stress by increasing wall thickness

---

## 4. Function

**Effect of Afterload on Stroke Volume:**
- Increased afterload → reduced SV (inverse relationship)
- Myocardium must generate higher pressure to eject → more energy used → less effective ejection
- In the **adult**: the myocardium has reserve to compensate for afterload increases
- In the **neonate** (especially preterm): less reserve → even modest afterload increases significantly reduce SV

**Transitional Afterload Changes:**
- **Cord clamping**: removes low-resistance placental circuit → SVR rises abruptly → LV afterload increases acutely
- **PVR fall at birth**: RV afterload decreases → RV output redistributed to lungs
- **PDA**: left-to-right shunt → pulmonary overcirculation → increased LV volume load; also lowers diastolic BP → reduced coronary perfusion pressure

**Vasopressor Effects on Afterload:**
- Dopamine (high dose) and norepinephrine: increase SVR → increase LV afterload
- Vasopressin: increases SVR; may selectively reduce PVR at low doses
- Milrinone: reduces SVR and PVR (lusitrope + vasodilator) → reduces both ventricular afterloads

---

## 5. Dysfunction / Pathophysiology

- **Elevated LV afterload**:
  - Critical CoA, AS → LV pressure overload → concentric hypertrophy → diastolic dysfunction → eventual failure
  - Vasopressor overdose → excessive SVR → reduced SV → paradoxical low CO despite treatment
  - Post-transition: SVR rise from cord clamping → duct-dependent systemic lesions decompensate

- **Elevated RV afterload**:
  - PPHN → elevated PVR → RV pressure overload → RV failure → right-to-left shunting → severe hypoxemia
  - CDH, pulmonary hypoplasia → chronic elevated PVR → RV hypertrophy → failure
  - RV is particularly poorly adapted to acute afterload stress (thin-walled, compliant structure)

- **Reduced afterload (vasodilatory states)**:
  - Septic shock → low SVR → hypotension despite adequate CO initially
  - Vasodilatory agents: milrinone, nitroprusside, iNO (selective pulmonary vasodilator)

---

## 6. Clinical Phenotype

**High LV Afterload (Elevated SVR):**
- Hypertension, strong pulses
- Reduced SV → narrow pulse pressure if CO falls
- Signs of LV failure if severe: hepatomegaly, respiratory distress, poor feeding

**High RV Afterload (Elevated PVR / PPHN):**
- Central cyanosis unresponsive to O₂
- Pre-post ductal SpO₂ gradient >10%
- Loud P2 on auscultation
- RV heave, tricuspid regurgitation murmur

**Low Afterload (Vasodilatory):**
- Warm, well-perfused periphery
- Wide pulse pressure, bounding pulses
- Low diastolic BP (diastolic runoff through PDA)
- Hypotension with high CO (early sepsis)

---

## 7. Differential / Distinctions

| Condition | Dominant Afterload Problem | Ventricular Impact |
|---|---|---|
| PPHN | High PVR (RV) | RV failure, R→L shunt |
| Critical CoA | High SVR (LV) | LV failure, shock |
| Septic shock | Low SVR (LV) | Distributive hypotension |
| Large PDA | Low SVR + high pulmonary flow | LV volume overload |
| Vasopressor excess | High SVR (LV) | Reduced SV, worsened CO |

---

## 8. Diagnosis

- **Echocardiography**:
  - TR jet velocity → estimate RV systolic pressure → infer PVR
  - Septal bowing toward LV → RV pressure > LV pressure (severe PPHN)
  - LV fractional shortening and EF → assess LV response to afterload
  - DA shunt direction → assess balance of PVR vs. SVR
- **Blood pressure** (4-limb BPs): upper-lower gradient >10-20 mmHg → CoA
- **Oxygenation Index (OI)**: MAP × FiO₂ × 100 / PaO₂ → quantify RV afterload burden in PPHN
- **Biomarkers**: BNP/NT-proBNP → elevated in ventricular pressure or volume overload

---

## 9. Management

**Reduce RV Afterload (High PVR):**
- Optimize lung recruitment (surfactant, ventilation)
- Supplemental oxygen (pulmonary vasodilator)
- Inhaled NO (iNO): selective pulmonary vasodilator
- Milrinone: PDE3 inhibitor; reduces PVR and SVR
- Sildenafil: PDE5 inhibitor; reduces PVR
- Correct acidosis and hypoxia (potent pulmonary vasoconstrictors)

**Reduce LV Afterload (High SVR):**
- Milrinone: reduces SVR + improves contractility
- Nitroprusside: potent vasodilator (short-acting, titrated)
- Treat underlying cause (e.g., CoA requires surgical/interventional repair)

**Increase SVR (Low Afterload / Vasodilatory Shock):**
- Norepinephrine: first-line vasopressor for vasodilatory shock
- Dopamine (high dose): α1-mediated vasoconstriction
- Vasopressin: non-adrenergic vasopressor; useful as adjunct

---

## 10. Therapeutics

- **iNO** 5-20 ppm: selective pulmonary vasodilator; activates sGC → cGMP → RV afterload reduction
- **Sildenafil** (PDE5 inhibitor): oral/IV; prevents cGMP breakdown → sustained PVR reduction
- **Milrinone** 0.25-0.75 mcg/kg/min: PDE3 inhibitor; inotrope + vasodilator; reduces both PVR and SVR
- **Norepinephrine** 0.01-1 mcg/kg/min: α1 + β1; raises SVR; for vasodilatory states
- **Vasopressin** 0.0001-0.002 units/kg/min: V1 receptor-mediated SVR increase; preserves pulmonary vasodilation

---

## 11. Complications / Outcomes

- **Unrecognized high RV afterload (PPHN)**: severe hypoxemia, right heart failure, ECMO requirement, death
- **Excessive vasopressor use → high SVR**: impairs LV ejection → worsens CO despite elevated BP
- **Milrinone hypotension**: systemic vasodilation can cause profound hypotension if preload is not maintained; risk in hypovolemic patients
- **iNO rebound**: abrupt discontinuation of iNO → rebound pulmonary vasoconstriction → acute hypoxemia

---

## 12. Associations / Genetics / Maternal-Fetal Links

- **CDH**: maldeveloped pulmonary vasculature → chronically elevated PVR → severe RV afterload stress at birth
- **Turner syndrome / Williams syndrome**: associated with CoA → LV afterload stress
- **22q11 deletion**: associated with aortic arch anomalies and pulmonary vascular abnormalities
- **Maternal SSRI use**: associated with PPHN → elevated neonatal RV afterload
- **Maternal diabetes**: hypertrophic cardiomyopathy → dynamic LV outflow obstruction → functional high afterload

---

## 13. Context / Board Framing

- **"Which ventricle is more sensitive to acute afterload stress?"** → Right ventricle (thin-walled, not adapted to pressure loads)
- **"Immature myocardium vs adult"**: more afterload-sensitive; less contractile reserve to compensate
- **"Cord clamping effect on afterload"**: removes placental low-resistance circuit → SVR rises → LV afterload increases acutely
- **"What is the role of milrinone in PPHN?"** → Reduces PVR (RV afterload) and SVR (LV afterload); also improves contractility
- **"PDA effects on afterload"**: left-to-right PDA → pulmonary overcirculation → diastolic runoff → low diastolic BP → reduces coronary perfusion (diastolic steal)

---

## 14. Edge Pearl

- The transition from fetal to postnatal life represents the most dramatic afterload change the human heart ever experiences: at cord clamping, SVR approximately **doubles** as the low-resistance placental circuit is removed. The LV — which was handling low afterload in fetal life — must immediately adapt. In most term infants this is well-tolerated. In preterm infants with immature LV function, or in infants with duct-dependent systemic lesions (e.g., critical CoA, HLHS), this acute afterload surge can precipitate cardiovascular collapse within hours.
