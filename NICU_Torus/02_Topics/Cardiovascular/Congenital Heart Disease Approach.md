## 1. Identity

- **Congenital heart disease (CHD)** affects ~8-10 per 1,000 live births; CHD is the most common major congenital anomaly
- Critical CHD (requiring intervention in the first year): ~25% of all CHD
- Neonatal approach requires: systematic categorization, recognition of duct-dependence, and knowledge of presentation timing
- Central tool: **hyperoxia test** — differentiates cardiac from pulmonary cause of cyanosis
- **PGE1** is the critical bridge therapy for duct-dependent lesions

---

## 2. Taxonomy / Developmental Placement

- Most CHD presents postnatally as fetal shunts close
- Duct-dependent lesions decompose within hours to days of DA closure
- Critical gestational axis: **term** (most critical CHD presents in term infants; preterm CHD presentation is confounded by prematurity physiology)
- Links: [[Fetal Circulation]] (fetal well-compensation), [[Transitional Circulation]] (unmasking), [[PDA Physiology]] (duct-dependence)

## 3. Structure

**Primary Classification:**

**Cyanotic CHD (Right-to-Left or Obligate Mixing):**
- The "5 Ts" plus additions:
  1. **Tetralogy of Fallot (ToF)**: VSD + RVOTO + overriding aorta + RVH; degree of cyanosis depends on RVOTO
  2. **Transposition of Great Arteries (TGA)**: parallel circuits; most critical cyanotic lesion; duct-dependent mixing
  3. **Tricuspid Atresia**: no RA-RV connection; requires ASD and often VSD for survival; duct-dependent
  4. **Truncus Arteriosus**: single outflow tract; mixing; prone to pulmonary overcirculation
  5. **Total Anomalous Pulmonary Venous Return (TAPVR)**: pulmonary veins drain to systemic; obstructed TAPVR = emergency
- Plus: **Pulmonary Atresia** (duct-dependent), **Ebstein anomaly**, **HLHS** (duct-dependent systemic)

**Acyanotic CHD:**

*Left-to-Right Shunts (increase pulmonary blood flow):*
- **VSD** (most common CHD): pansystolic murmur LLSB; presents with heart failure weeks after birth as PVR falls
- **ASD**: fixed split S2; usually asymptomatic in neonates; may present later in childhood
- **PDA**: see [[PDA Physiology]]
- **AV canal (AVSD)**: complete form associated with Trisomy 21; common AV valve + large septal defect

*Obstructive Lesions:*
- **Coarctation of aorta (CoA)**: narrowing of aorta near DA; duct-dependent systemic flow in severe (critical) CoA
- **Aortic stenosis (AS)**: critical AS = duct-dependent systemic flow
- **Pulmonary stenosis (PS)**: critical PS = duct-dependent pulmonary flow

---

## 4. Function

**Duct-Dependent Lesions — Must Recognize:**

| Category | Examples | What the DA provides | PGE1 effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Duct-dependent pulmonary flow | Pulmonary atresia, critical PS, ToF with severe RVOTO, TA | Pulmonary blood flow | Maintains pulmonary perfusion |
| Duct-dependent systemic flow | Critical CoA, HLHS, critical AS, IAA | Systemic/lower body perfusion | Maintains aortic flow |
| Ductal mixing | TGA (without large ASD) | Mixing of oxygenated/deoxygenated blood | Maintains oxygenation |

**Hyperoxia Test (100% O₂ for 10 minutes):**
- PaO₂ rises to >150 mmHg → **pulmonary cause** (V/Q mismatch, lung disease)
- PaO₂ remains <100 mmHg → **cardiac cause** (obligate R→L shunting not correctable by O₂)
- Sensitivity/specificity imperfect; echo is definitive
- Note: pulse oximetry alone is insufficient; requires arterial blood gas

---

## 5. Dysfunction / Pathophysiology

**Lesion-Specific Pathophysiology:**

- **TGA**: two parallel circuits; deoxygenated blood recirculates through systemic circuit; oxygenated blood recirculates through pulmonary circuit; mixing only via ASD/PDA/VSD → **most cyanotic on day 1**
- **HLHS**: absent or hypoplastic left heart; systemic circulation depends on RV → DA → aorta; presents at DA closure as shock + cyanosis
- **Critical CoA**: lower body perfusion via DA (ductus → descending aorta); ductal closure → femoral pulse loss, lower limb ischemia, cardiovascular collapse
- **TAPVR (obstructed)**: pulmonary veins drain to portal system or below diaphragm → pulmonary venous obstruction → severe pulmonary edema + cyanosis → **surgical emergency**
- **ToF**: severity determined by RVOTO; mild → "pink tet"; severe → profound cyanosis at birth; "tet spells" = hypercyanotic episodes (paroxysmal infundibular spasm)

---

## 6. Clinical Phenotype

**Presentation by Timing:**

| Timing | Lesion | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | TGA | Inadequate mixing at birth; most cyanotic |
| Day 1-2 | HLHS, obstructed TAPVR | DA closure → shock; obstructed TAPVR = edema/cyanosis |
| Days 2-7 | Critical CoA, critical AS | DA closes → loss of systemic flow |
| Weeks 2-6 | VSD, PDA, AV canal | PVR falls → L→R shunt → heart failure (poor feeding, tachypnea, sweating) |
| Months | ASD | Large volume L→R; may not present until childhood |

**Differential Cyanosis (Board Pearl):**
- **Pre-ductal sat > post-ductal sat** (right hand > foot): right-to-left PDA shunting → deoxygenated blood enters aorta below subclavian → PPHN pattern (more saturated blood going to upper body via LV)
- **Reverse differential cyanosis** (foot sat > right hand sat): TGA with PPHN → oxygenated pulmonary blood enters descending aorta via DA; ascending aorta gets deoxygenated blood from LV

**Pulse Oximetry Newborn Screening:**
- Performed at 24-48h of life
- Positive screen: SpO₂ <95% pre-ductal OR post-ductal on 3 attempts, OR difference >3%
- Sensitivity ~77% for critical CHD; misses some lesions (e.g., CoA, some AVSD)

---

## 7. Differential / Distinctions

| Presentation | Cyanosis + O₂ unresponsive | Shock + collapse | Heart failure (weeks) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cardiac | TGA, ToF, Pulm atresia | HLHS, CoA, AS | VSD, AV canal, PDA |
| Pulmonary | PPHN, RDS, MAS | Tension PTX, tamponade | BPD |
| Metabolic | Methemoglobin | Sepsis, hypoglycemia | Cardiomyopathy |

**Hyperoxia Test:**
- Performed: arterial blood gas after 100% O₂ for 10 minutes
- PaO₂ >150: non-cardiac (pulmonary disease, PPHN often responds partially)
- PaO₂ <100: cyanotic CHD

---

## 8. Diagnosis

**Approach to Cyanotic Neonate:**
1. Assess: is the baby centrally cyanotic? Confirm with pre-ductal ABG (not just SpO₂)
2. Check: pre-post ductal SpO₂ difference
3. Hyperoxia test (cautiously — avoid if TGA suspected, as O₂ may close the DA and reduce mixing)
4. CXR: cardiac silhouette, pulmonary vascularity, arch sidedness
5. ECG: axis, RV vs LV dominance, arrhythmia
6. **Echocardiography**: definitive diagnosis — anatomy, function, shunt direction, DA patency

**CXR Clues:**
- Boot-shaped heart (coeur en sabot): ToF (RVH + absent PA segment)
- Egg-on-string: TGA (narrow mediastinum, absent thymic shadow)
- Snowman: TAPVR (supracardiac; large mediastinum)
- Rib notching: CoA (older patients; not in neonates)
- Cardiomegaly + pulmonary edema: TAPVR obstructed, HLHS, critical AS

---

## 9. Management

**Initial Stabilization:**
- Secure IV access (umbilical venous catheter preferred)
- **PGE1 (alprostadil)** if duct-dependent lesion suspected or confirmed:
  - 0.05-0.1 mcg/kg/min to start; titrate to response
  - Side effects: apnea (most common → be prepared to intubate), fever, hypotension, jitteriness
- Maintain adequate oxygenation without hyperoxia (excessive O₂ may close DA prematurely)
- Avoid hyperoxia in suspected TGA (closes DA → reduces mixing)
- **Balloon atrial septostomy (BAS)**: for TGA with intact atrial septum → increases mixing; performed at bedside with echo guidance
- Transfer to cardiac center for definitive management

**Specific Management Approaches:**
- **TGA**: PGE1 + BAS (if inadequate mixing) → arterial switch operation (ASO) in first 2 weeks
- **HLHS**: PGE1 → Norwood stage I (or hybrid) → Glenn → Fontan palliation
- **Critical CoA**: PGE1 → surgical repair (end-to-end anastomosis) or balloon dilation
- **ToF**: medical management until elective repair (usually 3-6 months); urgent surgery if severely hypoxic
- **Obstructed TAPVR**: immediate surgical correction (pulmonary venous obstruction is not medically manageable)

---

## 10. Therapeutics

- **PGE1 (alprostadil)** 0.01-0.1 mcg/kg/min IV: EP2/EP4 receptor-mediated ductal smooth muscle relaxation; maintains patency
- **Prostaglandin E1 (higher doses)**: 0.1-0.3 mcg/kg/min for severely reduced ductal patency; risk of apnea increases
- **Atropine/bicarbonate**: for "tet spells" (knee-chest position, morphine, propranolol, phenylephrine)
- **Digoxin**: for heart failure in left-to-right shunt lesions (VSD, AV canal) — less used today
- **Furosemide**: for pulmonary overcirculation symptoms
- **Phenylephrine**: increases SVR → reduces R→L ToF shunt (used in tet spells)

---

## 11. Complications / Outcomes

- **Delayed PGE1 initiation**: ductal closure before treatment → profound shock or severe hypoxemia → death or severe neurological injury
- **PGE1 apnea**: occurs in up to 12% of patients; always have airway management available
- **Missed diagnosis**: some CHD not detected by pulse ox screening (e.g., CoA, lesions without cyanosis)
- **Long-term outcomes by lesion**:
  - TGA after ASO: excellent survival (>95%); some risk of coronary complications
  - HLHS: 5-year survival ~70% after staged palliation
  - ToF: excellent long-term outcomes after repair; late risk of RV dilation from pulmonary regurgitation

---

## 12. Associations / Genetics / Maternal-Fetal Links

- **Trisomy 21**: AV canal (AVSD), VSD, ASD, ToF
- **Trisomy 18**: VSD, ASD, PDA, DORV
- **Turner syndrome (45,X)**: bicuspid aortic valve, CoA
- **22q11 deletion (DiGeorge)**: interrupted aortic arch, ToF, truncus arteriosus, VSD
- **Williams syndrome (7q11)**: supravalvar AS, peripheral pulmonary stenosis
- **Maternal diabetes**: TGA, VSD, CoA, HLHS; hypertrophic cardiomyopathy
- **Maternal lithium**: Ebstein anomaly (risk lower than historically reported)
- **Maternal lupus**: congenital complete heart block (anti-Ro/La)
- **Maternal rubella**: PDA, pulmonary artery stenosis
- **Maternal phenylketonuria (PKU)**: VSD, ToF, CoA

---

## 13. Context / Board Framing

- **"5 Ts of cyanotic CHD"**: Tetralogy, Transposition, Tricuspid atresia, Truncus, Total anomalous PVR
- **"Hyperoxia test"**: PaO₂ <100 on 100% O₂ → cardiac cause; >150 → pulmonary cause
- **"Day 1 cyanosis without murmur"**: TGA until proven otherwise
- **"Week 1 cardiovascular collapse"**: duct-dependent systemic lesion (CoA, HLHS, AS); give PGE1 empirically
- **"Pulse ox screen"**: at 24-48h; sensitivity 77% for critical CHD; does not detect all lesions
- **"Differential cyanosis"**: right hand more saturated than feet → PPHN (R→L PDA); reverse differential (feet > right hand) → TGA + PPHN
- **"22q11 deletion"**: think aortic arch anomalies + T cell deficiency + hypocalcemia

---

## 14. Edge Pearl

- **Critical CoA can present deceptively late** — even up to 7-10 days — because a small amount of ductal patency maintains lower-body perfusion. As the DA gradually closes, femoral pulses slowly weaken and lower-limb BP gradually falls. A neonate discharged at 48h with "normal exam" can return at day 7-10 in severe shock. The mnemonic: **"femoral pulses on every exam"** — asymmetric or absent femoral pulses + cardiovascular compromise in a week-old term infant = critical CoA until proven otherwise; start PGE1 before the echo.
