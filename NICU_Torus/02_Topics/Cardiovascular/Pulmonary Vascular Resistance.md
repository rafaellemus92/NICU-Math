## 1. Identity

- **Pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR)** is the resistance to blood flow through the pulmonary vasculature
- PVR = (mPAP - PCWP) / Qp (× 80 for dynes·s/cm⁵)
- In the fetus: PVR is **high** → only ~10% of CVO reaches the lungs
- At birth: PVR falls precipitously → normal postnatal PVR is 1/10th of fetal levels within hours
- Failed PVR fall → PPHN: persistent fetal circulation pattern → see [[PPHN Hemodynamics]]

---

## 2. Taxonomy / Developmental Placement

- Central to understanding [[Fetal Circulation]], [[Transitional Circulation]], and [[PPHN Hemodynamics]]
- High PVR in fetal life is not pathologic — it is essential for the parallel fetal circuit to function
- PVR falls continuously from birth through the first weeks of life as pulmonary vascular remodeling occurs
- Cross-links: [[Extrauterine Pulmonary Transition]], [[Pulmonary Hypertension]]

---

## 3. Structure

**Pulmonary Vascular Anatomy:**
- Pulmonary arterioles (resistance vessels): smooth muscle layer thins rapidly after birth
- In fetal life: media of small pulmonary arteries is proportionally thick → high basal tone
- Remodeling postnatally: smooth muscle regression → reduced vascular resistance
- In CDH or pulmonary hypoplasia: abnormal vascular development → structurally elevated PVR

**PVR Regulators:**

| Vasoconstrictor | Vasodilator |
|---|---|
| Hypoxia (most potent) | Oxygen (most potent dilator postnatally) |
| Hypercapnia | Low PaCO₂ / alkalosis |
| Acidosis | High pH |
| Endothelin-1 (ET-1) | Nitric oxide (NO) → cGMP |
| Thromboxane A2 | Prostacyclin (PGI2) → cAMP |
| Hypothermia | Heat |
| Atelectasis | Lung recruitment |
| Polycythemia | |

**NO/cGMP Pathway:**
- eNOS in pulmonary endothelium → NO → activates soluble guanylate cyclase (sGC) → cGMP → PKG → smooth muscle relaxation
- PDE5 degrades cGMP (sildenafil inhibits PDE5 → sustains vasodilation)
- Oxygen and shear stress stimulate eNOS

**Endothelin Pathway:**
- ET-1 binds ETA receptors on smooth muscle → vasoconstriction
- ET-1 binds ETB receptors on endothelium → NO release → vasodilation (complex bidirectional effect)
- Bosentan (dual ETA/ETB antagonist) → reduces PVR in chronic PH

---

## 4. Function

**Fetal PVR:**
- High basal PVR → shunts blood away from lungs via DA (right-to-left)
- ~90% of RV output bypasses lungs via DA → descending aorta
- Only ~10% of CVO perfuses fetal lungs (for development, not gas exchange)

**Postnatal PVR Fall:**
- Oxygen is the dominant acute vasodilator
- Additional triggers: lung inflation, pH normalization, bradykinin, shear stress → NO release
- PVR falls to 50% of fetal values within minutes; to near-adult levels within days-weeks
- Complete pulmonary vascular remodeling takes weeks to months

**Relationship to RV Afterload:**
- PVR = primary determinant of RV afterload (see [[Afterload]])
- RV is exquisitely sensitive to acute PVR increases
- Gradual PVR elevation → RV hypertrophy
- Acute PVR elevation → acute RV dilation and failure (e.g., massive pulmonary embolism analogy)

---

## 5. Dysfunction / Pathophysiology

**Failed PVR Fall → PPHN:**
Three mechanisms (see [[PPHN Hemodynamics]]):
1. **Maladaptation**: PVR can fall but fails to in context of lung disease (MAS, RDS, sepsis, hypothermia)
2. **Maldevelopment**: abnormal vascular architecture (CDH); PVR structurally elevated
3. **Underdevelopment**: insufficient pulmonary vascular surface area (pulmonary hypoplasia, CDH, oligohydramnios sequence)

**Chronic Pulmonary Hypertension:**
- BPD-associated PH: chronic hypoxic vasoconstriction + abnormal vascular development → sustained elevated PVR
- In-utero hypoxia → premature vascular remodeling

**Hypoxic Pulmonary Vasoconstriction (HPV):**
- Reflex: local alveolar hypoxia → vasoconstriction of adjacent arteriole → diverts blood to better-oxygenated regions
- Adaptive in focal lung disease; maladaptive when global (worsens PPHN)

---

## 6. Clinical Phenotype

**Elevated PVR (PPHN / Pulmonary Hypertension):**
- Central cyanosis unresponsive to O₂
- Pre-post ductal SpO₂ gradient: right hand (pre-ductal) > foot (post-ductal) by >10%
- Labile oxygenation (responds to stimulation with acute desaturations)
- Loud P2 (increased pulmonary component of S2)
- TR murmur (high RV pressure → TR)
- RV heave
- Hepatomegaly (raised CVP from RV failure)

**Normal/Low PVR:**
- Pink, well-saturated neonate
- Equal pre- and post-ductal saturations
- Soft P2

---

## 7. Differential / Distinctions

| Condition | PVR | Mechanism | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| PPHN (maladaptation) | High (functional) | Hypoxia, acidosis, atelectasis | Treat lung disease, O₂, iNO |
| CDH | High (structural) | Abnormal vascular development | Gentle ventilation, iNO, ECMO |
| BPD-PH | Elevated (chronic) | Hypoxia + vascular remodeling | Sildenafil, bosentan, O₂ |
| Normal transition | Rapidly falling | O₂, NO, lung expansion | No intervention |
| Cyanotic CHD | Variable | Structural shunt | PGE1, surgical repair |

---

## 8. Diagnosis

- **Echocardiography** (gold standard for non-invasive PVR estimation):
  - **TR jet velocity**: simplified Bernoulli → RV systolic pressure ≈ 4v²; if no PS, = PA systolic pressure
  - RVsP > 35 mmHg or >2/3 systemic BP → significant pulmonary hypertension
  - **Septal bowing toward LV** (flattened or reversed "D-sign"): RV pressure ≥ LV pressure
  - DA shunt direction: right-to-left or bidirectional → confirms suprasystemic/systemic-level PVR
  - RV size, function, TAPSE (RV longitudinal function)
- **Pre-post ductal pulse oximetry**: screen for R→L ductal shunting
- **Oxygenation Index (OI)**: = MAP × FiO₂ × 100 / PaO₂; OI >25 → consider iNO; OI >40 → consider ECMO
- **Blood gas**: PaO₂ and A-a gradient; PaCO₂ (hypercapnia worsens PVR)
- **Cardiac catheterization**: rarely needed in neonates; gold standard for PVR measurement

---

## 9. Management

**Optimize Conditions to Lower PVR:**
- Maintain adequate oxygenation (SpO₂ 91-95% in PPHN; avoid hypoxia)
- Optimize ventilation: lung recruitment (prevent atelectasis), avoid air trapping
- Maintain normocapnia-mild alkalosis (pH 7.35-7.45; consider mild hyperventilation cautiously)
- Correct acidosis (metabolic acidosis worsens PVR; sodium bicarbonate judiciously)
- Normothermia (hypothermia → pulmonary vasoconstriction)
- Treat pain/agitation (sympathetic stimulation worsens PVR)
- **Surfactant**: for RDS/MAS → improves lung recruitment → facilitates PVR fall

**Targeted Pulmonary Vasodilators:**
- See [[PPHN Hemodynamics]] for full algorithm
- **iNO**: selective, inhaled; 5-20 ppm; activates cGMP pathway
- **Sildenafil** (PDE5 inhibitor): sustains cGMP; oral/IV
- **Milrinone** (PDE3 inhibitor): reduces PVR + SVR + positive inotropy
- **Prostacyclin (epoprostenol/treprostinil)**: IV or inhaled; cAMP pathway

---

## 10. Therapeutics

- **Oxygen** (targeted SpO₂ 91-95%): most potent acute pulmonary vasodilator
- **iNO** 5-20 ppm: activates sGC → cGMP → pulmonary vasodilation; selective (does not drop SVR)
- **Sildenafil** 0.5-2 mg/kg q6-8h PO/IV: PDE5 inhibitor; amplifies NO-cGMP pathway
- **Milrinone** 0.25-0.75 mcg/kg/min IV: PDE3 inhibitor; reduces PVR and SVR
- **Inhaled prostacyclin (iloprost)**: inhaled; alternative/adjunct to iNO; cAMP-mediated vasodilation
- **Bosentan**: dual endothelin receptor antagonist; oral; used in chronic BPD-associated PH
- **ECMO**: for refractory PPHN (OI >40, failure of all medical therapy)

---

## 11. Complications / Outcomes

- **Uncontrolled elevated PVR (PPHN)**: severe hypoxemia, end-organ ischemia, RV failure, death
- **iNO rebound**: abrupt discontinuation → acute rebound pulmonary vasoconstriction; wean slowly
- **Methemoglobinemia**: rare complication of iNO (especially at high doses >20 ppm); monitor MetHb levels
- **Sildenafil + iNO combination**: can cause systemic hypotension (SVR also reduced)
- **BPD-associated PH**: major morbidity; associated with increased mortality in ex-preterm infants
- **CDH**: persistent pulmonary hypertension is the major determinant of survival; structural PH less responsive to medical therapy

---

## 12. Associations / Genetics / Maternal-Fetal Links

- **Maternal SSRI use**: 6-fold increase in PPHN risk (possible serotonin-mediated vascular effects)
- **CDH**: maldevelopment of pulmonary vasculature; associated with lung hypoplasia
- **Oligohydramnios sequence** (e.g., bilateral renal agenesis): lung hypoplasia → insufficient pulmonary vascular bed → low PVR capacity
- **Post-dates (>41 weeks)**: increased MAS risk → maladaptive PPHN
- **Maternal preeclampsia**: associated with IUGR; fetal hypoxia → increased risk of neonatal PPHN
- **BMPR2 mutations**: rare familial pulmonary arterial hypertension; affects BMP signaling in vascular remodeling

## 13. Context / Board Framing

- **"What % of CVO goes to the fetal lungs?"** → ~10% (because PVR is high)
- **"Most potent pulmonary vasodilator at birth?"** → Oxygen
- **"NO mechanism in pulmonary vasodilation?"** → eNOS → NO → sGC → cGMP → PKG → smooth muscle relaxation
- **"What does PDE5 do?"** → Degrades cGMP → sildenafil inhibits PDE5 → sustains cGMP → vasodilation
- **"Three categories of PPHN?"** → Maladaptation, maldevelopment (CDH), underdevelopment (pulmonary hypoplasia)
- **"OI for iNO use?"** → OI >25; OI >40 → consider ECMO
- **"Why does atelectasis worsen PVR?"** → Hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction in underventilated regions

---

## 14. Edge Pearl

- **Alkalosis reduces PVR** — and historically, deliberate hyperventilation to induce respiratory alkalosis was used to treat PPHN. However, this approach causes lung injury (volutrauma, barotrauma) and impairs cerebral perfusion (hypocapnia → cerebral vasoconstriction). Modern management targets normocapnia (PaCO₂ 40-50 mmHg) with gentle ventilation, reserving pH manipulation to treating metabolic acidosis rather than inducing respiratory alkalosis. The old hyperventilation strategy is now largely abandoned.
