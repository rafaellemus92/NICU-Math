## 1. Identity

- Transitional circulation is the **hemodynamic reorganization** occurring at and immediately after birth
- Converts fetal **parallel** circulation to adult **series** circulation
- Driven by three simultaneous events: **lung expansion**, **cord clamping**, **rise in arterial PO₂**
- Failure of any step → cardiorespiratory compromise, PPHN, or shock
- Complete transition takes hours to days; some elements (anatomic DA closure) take weeks

---

## 2. Taxonomy / Developmental Placement

- Occurs at birth through the first 72 hours of life (functional) and weeks (anatomic)
- Gestational axis: **transitional** (spans fetal → postnatal)
- Preceded by [[Fetal Circulation]]; leads to stable postnatal hemodynamics
- Failure → [[PPHN Hemodynamics]]

---

## 3. Structure

**Sequential Events at Birth:**

1. **Lung expansion with first breaths**
   - Air replaces fluid in alveoli → alveolar PO₂ rises
   - Pulmonary vascular smooth muscle relaxes → PVR begins to fall
   - Pulmonary blood flow increases dramatically (from 10% to 100% of RV output)

2. **Cord clamping**
   - Removes low-resistance placental circuit
   - SVR rises acutely (by ~2-fold)
   - Venous return from placenta ceases → RA pressure falls relative to LA

3. **Foramen Ovale Functional Closure**
   - Increased pulmonary venous return → LA pressure rises
   - LA > RA pressure → flap of FO is pushed against atrial septum
   - Functional closure: minutes; anatomic closure (fusion): weeks to months
   - ~25% of adults retain a patent foramen ovale (PFO) anatomically

4. **Ductus Arteriosus Constriction**
   - Rising PO₂ → O₂-sensitive K⁺ channels in ductal smooth muscle close → depolarization → vasoconstriction
   - Prostaglandin withdrawal (loss of placental PGE2 source)
   - Functional closure: **24-72 hours** in term neonates
   - Anatomic closure (fibrous ligamentum arteriosum): **2-3 weeks**
   - In preterm: immature smooth muscle → delayed or failed closure → see [[PDA Physiology]]

5. **Ductus Venosus Closure**
   - Loss of umbilical venous flow → DV constricts
   - Functional closure within hours of birth
   - Anatomic closure → ligamentum venosum over weeks

---

## 4. Function

- **Series circulation** established: RV → lungs → LA → LV → systemic
- LV now handles systemic afterload (previously shared with RV via DA)
- LV undergoes hypertrophy over first weeks to adapt to increased workload
- RV output now fully directed to lungs; RV wall thins over weeks as PVR falls
- Oxygen delivery to tissues now depends entirely on pulmonary gas exchange

**Hemodynamic Changes Summary:**

| Parameter | Fetal | Transitional/Postnatal |
|---|---|---|
| PVR | High | Falls rapidly |
| SVR | Low (placenta) | Rises (cord clamping) |
| DA flow | R→L | L→R → closes |
| FO | Open (R→L) | Closes (L→R gradient) |
| LV afterload | Low | High |
| RV afterload | High | Low |

---

## 5. Dysfunction / Pathophysiology

- **Failed PVR fall** → PVR remains elevated → right-to-left shunting persists at DA and FO → PPHN
  - Causes: hypoxia, acidosis, hypothermia, sepsis, MAS, CDH, pulmonary hypoplasia
- **SVR rise without adequate LV adaptation** → LV failure in severe cases (e.g., HLHS presenting at DA closure)
- **Premature cord clamping** → reduced placental transfusion → lower blood volume → preload-dependent hypotension
- **Delayed cord clamping** → increased placental transfusion → polycythemia risk in term infants
- **Asphyxia** → impairs myocardial function AND PVR fall → combined cardiac and pulmonary dysfunction

**Key Mediators of PVR Fall:**
- ↑ PO₂ (most important stimulus)
- ↑ Nitric oxide (NO) → cGMP → smooth muscle relaxation
- ↑ Prostacyclin (PGI₂) → cAMP → smooth muscle relaxation
- ↓ Endothelin-1 (vasoconstrictor)
- ↑ pH (alkalosis promotes pulmonary vasodilation)
- Mechanical stretch of lung → NO release

---

## 6. Clinical Phenotype

- **Normal transition**: vigorous cry, rapid improvement in color from blue → pink, heart rate >100, SpO₂ rising over 5-10 min
- **Failed transition (PPHN)**: persistent central cyanosis, labile oxygenation, pre-post ductal SpO₂ gradient >10%
- **Transitional murmur**: tricuspid regurgitation murmur common in first hours (elevated RV pressure during transition)
- **Physiologic RV hypertrophy**: expected on ECG in first days; does not indicate pathology

**Normal SpO₂ Targets During Transition (NRP Guidelines):**

| Age (min) | Target SpO₂ (pre-ductal) |
|---|---|
| 1 | 60-65% |
| 2 | 65-70% |
| 3 | 70-75% |
| 4 | 75-80% |
| 5 | 80-85% |
| 10 | 85-95% |

---

## 7. Differential / Distinctions

- **PPHN vs. cyanotic CHD**: both present with cyanosis; hyperoxia test helps distinguish; echo is definitive
- **Transitional tachypnea vs. PPHN**: TTN has normal O₂ response to supplemental O₂; PPHN does not
- **Asphyxia-associated cardiovascular dysfunction vs. structural CHD**: timing, clinical context, echo
- **Delayed transition** (preterm infants): normally slower PVR fall and DA closure → longer transitional period is expected

---

## 8. Diagnosis

- **Pulse oximetry** (pre- and post-ductal): assess for right-to-left shunting
  - Pre-ductal: right hand
  - Post-ductal: either foot
  - Difference >10% or post-ductal <85% after 10 min → evaluate for PPHN or CHD
- **Chest X-ray**: lung recruitment, cardiac silhouette
- **Echocardiography**: gold standard for assessing shunt direction, PVR estimation (TR jet velocity), ventricular function
- **Blood gas**: pH, PaCO₂, PaO₂ — assess adequacy of transition
- **OI (Oxygenation Index)**: MAP × FiO₂ × 100 / PaO₂ — severity of PPHN

---

## 9. Management

**Supporting Normal Transition:**
- **Delayed cord clamping** ≥60 seconds in most term and preterm infants (if not depressed)
  - Increases placental transfusion; reduces IVH risk in preterms
- **Avoid hypothermia**: cold stress → pulmonary vasoconstriction → impairs PVR fall
- **Avoid hypoxia**: optimize ventilation and FiO₂ to support PO₂ rise
- **Avoid acidosis**: adequate ventilation, treat metabolic acidosis
- **Avoid hypoglycemia**: impairs myocardial function

**Resuscitation (NRP):**
- Positive pressure ventilation if HR <100 or inadequate respiratory effort
- CPAP for preterm infants with respiratory distress
- Supplemental O₂ titrated by SpO₂

**Failed Transition (PPHN):**
- See [[PPHN Hemodynamics]] for full management algorithm

---

## 10. Therapeutics

- **Oxygen**: most potent selective pulmonary vasodilator during transition
- **Inhaled NO (iNO)**: selective pulmonary vasodilator for established PPHN; does not help if lungs not recruited
- **Surfactant**: improves lung recruitment → facilitates PVR fall in RDS/MAS
- **PGE1 (alprostadil)**: maintains DA patency in duct-dependent CHD when structural lesion prevents adequate pulmonary or systemic flow
- **Indomethacin/ibuprofen**: used only when DA closure desired (preterm PDA), not during transition

---

## 11. Complications / Outcomes

- **PPHN**: right-to-left shunting → severe hypoxemia → end-organ ischemia; see [[PPHN Hemodynamics]]
- **Transitional myocardial dysfunction**: transient tricuspid regurgitation, RV dysfunction; usually self-limited over 24-48h
- **Hypoxic-ischemic injury**: if transition severely impaired (e.g., asphyxia) → brain, kidney, gut, myocardium
- **Patent ductus arteriosus**: failure of functional DA closure → left-to-right shunt in postnatal period; see [[PDA Physiology]]
- **Persistent PFO**: benign in most; may allow paradoxical embolism

---

## 12. Associations / Genetics / Maternal-Fetal Links

- **Perinatal asphyxia** → most common cause of impaired transition; associated with meconium aspiration
- **Maternal diabetes** → impairs myocardial adaptation; septal hypertrophy
- **Maternal SSRI use** → associated with PPHN (possible mechanism: altered NO signaling)
- **CDH** → maldeveloped pulmonary vasculature → most severe transitional failure; see [[Congenital Diaphragmatic Hernia]]
- **Prematurity** → slower, more vulnerable transition; lower PO₂ threshold for ductal closure

---

## 13. Context / Board Framing

- **"What causes PVR to fall at birth?"** → Lung expansion, rising PO₂, NO release, pH normalization, prostacyclin
- **"What event drives the foramen ovale to close?"** → LA pressure exceeds RA pressure (increased pulmonary venous return + loss of placental venous return)
- **"What is the most important stimulus for ductal constriction?"** → Rising arterial PO₂
- **"What does a pre-post ductal SpO₂ difference of >10% indicate?"** → Right-to-left shunting at the DA level (PPHN or critical CHD)
- **"When does the ductus arteriosus functionally close in a term infant?"** → 24-72 hours of life
- **"Why does cord clamping raise SVR?"** → Removes the low-resistance placental circuit from the systemic circulation

---

## 14. Edge Pearl

- **Delayed cord clamping in preterm infants** reduces the incidence of IVH and necrotizing enterocolitis — the mechanism is not simply increased blood volume, but also a more gradual hemodynamic transition that avoids the abrupt SVR rise and allows gentler LV loading.
- In the first minutes of life, the **right-to-left DA shunt** may briefly coexist with the beginning of the **left-to-right FO shunt** — creating a complex bidirectional transitional state where oxygenation depends on the balance of PVR vs. SVR rather than on either shunt in isolation.
