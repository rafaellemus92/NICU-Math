## 1. Identity

- The **ductus arteriosus (DA)** is a muscular vascular channel connecting the main pulmonary artery (PA) to the descending aorta
- Derived from the left sixth aortic arch
- **Essential in fetal life**: allows RV output to bypass the high-resistance pulmonary circulation
- **Problematic postnatally** when it fails to close: persistent DA (PDA) → hemodynamic burden on the preterm heart and lungs
- Closure is a two-phase process: **functional** (hours to days) and **anatomic** (weeks)

---

## 2. Taxonomy / Developmental Placement

- Relevant across fetal, transitional, and postnatal phases
- [[Fetal Circulation]]: DA carries right-to-left shunt (PA → aorta)
- [[Transitional Circulation]]: DA constricts as PO₂ rises and PGE2 falls
- Postnatal: in term infants DA functionally closes 24-72h; in preterm → often fails to close → hemodynamically significant PDA (hsPDA)
- Cross-links: [[Pulmonary Vascular Resistance]], [[Cardiac Output]], [[PPHN Hemodynamics]]

---

## 3. Structure

**Anatomy:**
- Located between left PA and descending aorta (just distal to left subclavian artery origin)
- Ductal tissue: smooth muscle that is sensitive to oxygen and prostaglandins
- Distinct from adjacent aortic and pulmonary arterial tissue — specialized vasoactive properties

**Regulation of Ductal Tone:**

| Factor | Effect on DA |
|---|---|
| PGE2 (prostaglandin E2) | Relaxation → patent |
| PGI2 (prostacyclin) | Relaxation → patent |
| Low PO₂ | Relaxation → patent |
| High PO₂ | Constriction → closure |
| Indomethacin/ibuprofen | Constriction → closure |
| Nitric oxide | Relaxation (minor) |
| Endothelin-1 | Constriction |
| Low gestational age | Reduced sensitivity to O₂, higher PGE2 sensitivity → stays open |

---

## 4. Function

**Fetal Role:**
- Right-to-left shunt: RV → PA → DA → descending aorta → bypasses high-resistance fetal lungs
- Carries ~55-60% of combined ventricular output from PA to aorta in fetal life
- Essential: without DA, RV would eject against the entire pulmonary vascular resistance → RV failure

**Postnatal Closure Mechanism:**
1. **Oxygen-mediated constriction** (primary trigger):
   - Rising PaO₂ → inhibits voltage-gated K⁺ channels in ductal smooth muscle → membrane depolarization → Ca²⁺ influx → contraction
   - More effective in term infants (higher O₂ sensitivity)
2. **Prostaglandin withdrawal**:
   - Loss of placental PGE2 source at cord clamping
   - Increased pulmonary metabolism of circulating prostaglandins
3. **Functional closure**: 24-72 hours (term); often delayed or absent in preterm
4. **Anatomic closure**: 2-3 weeks (term); remodeling → ligamentum arteriosum (fibrous cord)

**Hemodynamic Significance of Postnatal PDA (Preterm):**
- As PVR falls after birth, DA shunt reverses to **left-to-right**
- Left-to-right PDA → pulmonary overcirculation (increased pulmonary blood flow) → pulmonary edema
- **Diastolic steal**: aortic diastolic flow reverses toward the lungs → reduced coronary, cerebral, gut perfusion
- Left heart volume overload: increased pulmonary venous return → LA and LV enlargement

---

## 5. Dysfunction / Pathophysiology

**Preterm PDA (hsPDA):**
- Immature ductal smooth muscle → reduced O₂ sensitivity, reduced ability to constrict
- Elevated circulating and local PGE2 in preterm → sustained patency
- Incidence inversely proportional to gestational age:
  - ~50% at <28 weeks; ~80% at <24 weeks
- Low PVR after surfactant treatment → rapid left-to-right reversal of shunt → "pulmonary flood"

**Hemodynamic Consequences:**
- **Pulmonary overcirculation**: increased work of breathing, worsening lung disease, BPD
- **Diastolic steal**: low diastolic BP → impaired coronary perfusion (myocardial ischemia), gut ischemia (NEC risk), IVH (low-flow cerebral injury)
- **LV volume overload**: LA:Ao ratio >1.4 (dilated LA relative to aorta) indicates significant shunt
- **Low SVC flow**: cerebral perfusion marker; reduced in large PDA

**Echo Markers of Hemodynamic Significance:**
- LA:Ao ratio >1.4-1.5: indicates volume overload
- DA diameter >1.5 mm (preterm)
- Pulsatile diastolic flow in descending aorta reversed (retrograde) → diastolic steal
- LVO >300 mL/kg/min (inflated by pulmonary recirculation)
- SVC flow <40 mL/kg/min → compromised systemic perfusion

---

## 6. Clinical Phenotype

**Hemodynamically Significant PDA (hsPDA):**
- Continuous "machinery" murmur (may be systolic only in early phase when PVR still elevated)
- Bounding pulses, wide pulse pressure (>25 mmHg)
- Hyperdynamic precordium
- Increased FiO₂ requirement, worsening respiratory distress
- Metabolic acidosis, elevated lactate (if diastolic steal severe)
- Worsening on CXR: cardiomegaly, increased pulmonary vascular markings, pulmonary edema

**PDA Protective in PPHN (Board Pearl):**
- In some cases of PPHN, keeping the DA open allows R→L shunting at DA level
- This vents the RV, preventing RV failure, while systemic O₂ delivery is maintained by the LV
- Closing the PDA in severe PPHN can precipitate acute RV decompensation
- PGE1 may be used to keep DA open in these scenarios

---

## 7. Differential / Distinctions

| Condition | Key Differentiator |
|---|---|
| PDA | Continuous murmur, bounding pulses, wide pulse pressure |
| VSD | Pansystolic murmur at LLSB; no bounding pulses |
| ASD | Fixed split S2; flow murmur at RUSB |
| AVM | High-output state; no cardiac murmur; cranial bruit in VAGAL |
| Sepsis | Bounding pulses possible; but fever, metabolic acidosis, no murmur |
| PPHN | Pre-post ductal SpO₂ gradient; cyanosis; R→L on echo |

---

## 8. Diagnosis

**Clinical:**
- Bounding pulses, hyperdynamic precordium, continuous murmur
- Wide pulse pressure (>25 mmHg in preterm is significant)

**Echocardiography (Definitive):**
- DA visualization: diameter, direction of flow (Doppler), bidirectional vs. unidirectional
- LA:Ao ratio: >1.4-1.5 suggests volume overload
- LV output, SVC flow
- Retrograde diastolic flow in descending aorta (diastolic steal)
- LV end-diastolic dimension (LVEDD): increased in volume overload

**Chest X-ray:**
- Cardiomegaly
- Increased pulmonary vascular markings
- Pulmonary edema (interstitial pattern)

**Note:** Murmur may be absent in very preterm infants with low BP or in the first hours when PVR is still high and shunt not yet established

---

## 9. Management

**Conservative (Watchful Waiting):**
- Evidence supports conservative management in many preterm infants
- Many PDAs close spontaneously, especially beyond 28 weeks
- Fluid restriction (140-150 mL/kg/day), diuretics as needed
- Optimize respiratory support (reduce pulmonary edema)
- Monitor for signs of hemodynamic compromise
- ELFIN trial and others: indomethacin prophylaxis did not improve neurodevelopmental outcomes

**Medical Closure:**
- **Indomethacin** (COX inhibitor): 0.1-0.2 mg/kg IV q12-24h × 3 doses; 70-80% closure rate in term-equivalent; monitor renal function, platelets, gut perfusion
- **Ibuprofen** (COX inhibitor): similar efficacy to indomethacin; possibly less renal/gut adverse effect
- **Oral/IV Acetaminophen**: mechanism via inhibition of peroxidase component of COX; comparable efficacy in some trials; fewer adverse effects; now commonly used
- Contraindications to COX inhibitors: active bleeding (IVH, NEC), significant renal impairment, thrombocytopenia, oliguria

**Surgical/Interventional:**
- **Surgical ligation**: reserved for failed medical therapy with hemodynamically significant PDA
- **Catheter-based device closure** (Piccolo occluder): increasingly used in NICU; feasible down to ~700 g; avoids thoracotomy

---

## 10. Therapeutics

- **Indomethacin** 0.1-0.2 mg/kg IV: COX-1/2 inhibitor; reduces PGE2 and PGI2 → ductal constriction
- **Ibuprofen** 10 mg/kg then 5 mg/kg × 2 doses: COX-1/2 inhibitor; similar mechanism; may spare renal perfusion
- **Acetaminophen** 15 mg/kg q6h PO/IV: peroxidase inhibition → reduced PGE2 synthesis; similar closure rates in trials
- **Furosemide** 1-2 mg/kg: reduces pulmonary edema from overcirculation; does NOT close the DA (may increase PGE2)
- **PGE1 (alprostadil)**: used to KEEP DA open in duct-dependent CHD (opposite of above)

---

## 11. Complications / Outcomes

- **Untreated hsPDA**: BPD, NEC, IVH, pulmonary hemorrhage, prolonged ventilation, death
- **Indomethacin adverse effects**: oliguria, elevated creatinine, gut ischemia (risk of NEC), thrombocytopenia, hypoglycemia, spontaneous intestinal perforation (when combined with steroids)
- **Surgical ligation complications**: pneumothorax, vocal cord paralysis (recurrent laryngeal nerve), chylothorax, neurodevelopmental concerns in some studies (though may reflect selection bias)
- **Spontaneous closure**: ~75% of PDAs in preterm infants ultimately close without intervention by term equivalent age

---

## 12. Associations / Genetics / Maternal-Fetal Links

- **Prematurity**: dominant risk factor; incidence inversely proportional to GA
- **RDS**: surfactant use → rapid PVR fall → accelerates left-to-right shunt reversal → unmasking of PDA
- **Maternal indomethacin for tocolysis**: risk of in-utero ductal constriction; usually reversible; monitor DA flow
- **Trisomy 21**: associated with persistent PDA
- **Rubella syndrome**: PDA is classic association (pulmonary artery stenosis + PDA)
- **Prostaglandin dehydrogenase (HPGD) gene mutations**: rare cause of familial PDA

---

## 13. Context / Board Framing

- **"What maintains DA patency in the fetus?"** → PGE2, low PO₂
- **"What closes the DA postnatally?"** → Rising PO₂ → smooth muscle constriction; prostaglandin withdrawal
- **"Why is PDA more common in preterms?"** → Immature smooth muscle, higher PGE2 sensitivity, lower O₂ response
- **"LA:Ao ratio >1.4 means?"** → Significant left-to-right shunt with LV volume overload
- **"Diastolic steal"**: retrograde diastolic aortic flow → reduced coronary/gut/cerebral perfusion; manifests as wide pulse pressure, low diastolic BP
- **"When should the PDA be kept open (PGE1)?"** → Duct-dependent CHD: critical CoA, HLHS, pulmonary atresia, severe PS, TGA with inadequate mixing
- **"PDA may be protective in PPHN"** → R→L DA shunt vents RV; closing the DA can precipitate RV failure

---

## 14. Edge Pearl

- **Furosemide causes PDA dilation** via stimulation of renal prostaglandin synthesis (increased PGE2 production). This is counterintuitive — clinicians often use furosemide to treat pulmonary edema from a PDA, but the drug itself may pharmacologically work against ductal closure. This is why furosemide should not be considered a treatment for PDA closure and should be used judiciously alongside other management strategies.
