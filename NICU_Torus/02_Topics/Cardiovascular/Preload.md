## 1. Identity

- **Preload** is the end-diastolic volume (EDV) of the ventricle — the degree of myocardial stretch prior to contraction
- Represents ventricular filling; the "volume load" presented to the myocardium before systole
- Clinically approximated by end-diastolic pressure (EDP), ventricular dimensions on echo, or central venous pressure (CVP)
- Neonatal myocardium has reduced preload reserve due to limited compliance — small increases in EDV raise EDP steeply

---

## 2. Taxonomy / Developmental Placement

- Core determinant of stroke volume via the Frank-Starling mechanism → see [[Frank-Starling Principle]]
- One of four determinants of [[Cardiac Output]]
- Neonatal preload physiology differs from adult:
  - Less compliant (stiffer) myocardium → smaller operating range on the Starling curve
  - More dependent on adequate venous return and circulating volume
- Gestational axis: preterm and term postnatal

---

## 3. Structure

**Determinants of Preload:**

1. **Venous return**
   - Driven by mean systemic filling pressure minus right atrial pressure
   - Increased by: supine position, volume infusion, venoconstriction (catecholamines)
   - Decreased by: positive pressure ventilation, hypovolemia, vasodilation

2. **Circulating blood volume**
   - Neonate: ~80-90 mL/kg (term); ~90-100 mL/kg (preterm)
   - Hemorrhage, third-spacing, or inadequate placental transfusion → reduced preload

3. **Venous tone**
   - ~70% of blood volume is in the venous capacitance system
   - Catecholamines constrict veins → mobilize blood → increase venous return
   - Vasodilatory states (sepsis) → venous pooling → reduced preload

4. **Intrathoracic pressure (ITP)**
   - Negative ITP during spontaneous breathing assists venous return (suction effect)
   - Positive ITP during mechanical ventilation (PEEP, MAP) impedes venous return → reduces preload
   - **Board pearl**: high mean airway pressure on the ventilator reduces preload by impeding venous return

5. **Atrial contribution ("atrial kick")**
   - In neonates, atrial contraction contributes relatively more to ventricular filling than in adults
   - Loss of atrial kick (SVT, atrial flutter) → reduced preload → reduced SV

6. **Heart rate effects on filling time**
   - Bradycardia → increased diastolic filling time → increased preload (up to a point)
   - Tachycardia → reduced filling time → reduced preload

---

## 4. Function

- Preload determines the initial fiber length of the myocyte before contraction
- Per the [[Frank-Starling Principle]]: greater preload → greater cross-bridge formation → greater force development → greater SV
- In a compliant ventricle, a large preload reserve allows CO to increase substantially with volume loading
- In the neonatal ventricle (less compliant): limited preload reserve → small volumes can raise filling pressures dramatically

**Clinical Implication:**
- Neonates may tolerate small volume boluses (10 mL/kg) but excessive volume → pulmonary edema (low threshold)
- Responsiveness to volume must be assessed, not assumed

---

## 5. Dysfunction / Pathophysiology

- **Reduced preload (hypovolemia)**:
  - Causes: hemorrhage (abruption, vasa previa, fetomaternal hemorrhage), iatrogenic blood loss, third-spacing (NEC, ascites)
  - Result: reduced EDV → reduced SV → reduced CO → shock
  - Treatment: volume resuscitation

- **Excessive preload (volume overload)**:
  - Causes: large PDA with left-to-right shunt, iatrogenic fluid overload, TAPS (twin anemia-polycythemia sequence)
  - Result: ventricular dilation, increased wall stress, diastolic dysfunction, pulmonary edema
  - Treatment: diuretics, fluid restriction, PDA management

- **High airway pressure → impaired preload**:
  - High PEEP or MAP in ventilated patients → increased ITP → impedes venous return → reduced right heart preload → reduced CO
  - Especially important in air-trapping (MAS, BPD) or high-frequency ventilation

---

## 6. Clinical Phenotype

**Reduced Preload (Hypovolemia):**
- Tachycardia (compensatory)
- Low BP, narrow pulse pressure
- Pallor, mottling, prolonged CRT
- Flat fontanelle (if volume depleted)
- Decreased urine output
- Metabolic acidosis (if severe)

**Excessive Preload / Volume Overload:**
- Bounding pulses, wide pulse pressure (large PDA)
- Hepatomegaly
- Pulmonary crackles or worsening respiratory support needs
- Edema (periorbital, peripheral, anasarca in severe cases)
- Increasing FiO₂ requirement

---

## 7. Differential / Distinctions

| Condition | Preload Status | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| Hypovolemia | Low | Low BP, tachycardia, flat fontanelle |
| Large PDA | High (LV) | Bounding pulses, wide pulse pressure |
| PPHN | Variable | Pre-post ductal SpO₂ gradient, echo findings |
| Cardiogenic shock | High (backup) | Low CO despite high filling pressures |
| Tamponade | Low (impaired filling) | Muffled heart sounds, pulsus paradoxus |
| High PEEP | Acutely low | Clinical context: ventilated patient, CO falls with MAP rise |

---

## 8. Diagnosis

- **Echocardiography**: best assessment of EDV, LV dimensions, filling
  - Small LV cavity with hyperdynamic function → hypovolemia
  - Dilated LV with reduced function → volume overload or cardiomyopathy
- **Chest X-ray**: cardiac size (cardiomegaly suggests volume overload; small heart suggests hypovolemia)
- **CVP**: poor surrogate for preload in neonates; rarely used in isolation
- **Volume responsiveness testing**: 10 mL/kg NS bolus → reassess HR, BP, CRT, urine output
- **IVC collapsibility** (echocardiography): may predict fluid responsiveness (collapsed IVC → likely preload responsive)
- **Passive leg raise**: elevate legs to auto-transfuse venous blood; observe for improvement in CO (assessed by echo)

---

## 9. Management

**Insufficient Preload:**
- Isotonic crystalloid: 10 mL/kg NS or LR over 15-30 minutes; reassess after each bolus
- Packed RBCs (10-15 mL/kg) if anemic
- Fresh frozen plasma if coagulopathic hemorrhage
- Albumin 5% (10 mL/kg): consider if hypoalbuminemic or crystalloid-resistant
- Reduce airway pressure if ventilator-induced preload reduction suspected

**Excessive Preload:**
- Fluid restriction
- Diuretics: furosemide 1 mg/kg IV
- PDA management (if PDA driving left heart volume overload)
- Consider dobutamine if myocardium is failing under volume overload (offloads while supporting CO)

---

## 10. Therapeutics

- **Normal saline (NS)**: isotonic, first-line volume resuscitation
- **Albumin 5%**: oncotic support; may help retain fluid in intravascular space
- **Furosemide** 0.5-2 mg/kg IV: loop diuretic; reduces preload by promoting venous pooling and diuresis
- **Dobutamine**: in volume-overloaded failing ventricle — reduces cardiac filling pressures while improving contractility

---

## 11. Complications / Outcomes

- **Inadequate preload + untreated hypovolemia**: shock, end-organ injury, death
- **Excessive volume resuscitation**: pulmonary edema, worsening oxygenation, BPD risk, IVH (especially in preterms)
- **Repeated boluses without echo guidance**: risk of over- or under-treatment
- **IVH risk**: rapid volume shifts in preterm neonates alter cerebral perfusion pressure → periventricular capillary rupture

---

## 12. Associations / Genetics / Maternal-Fetal Links

- **Vasa previa / placenta accreta**: major hemorrhage at delivery → profound hypovolemia at birth
- **Fetomaternal hemorrhage**: occult blood loss → unexplained neonatal anemia and hypovolemia
- **Hydrops fetalis**: massive third-spacing → reduced intravascular preload despite total body fluid overload
- **Twin-twin transfusion**: donor twin → hypovolemia; recipient twin → volume overload/polycythemia
- **Delayed cord clamping**: increases neonatal blood volume by 20-30% → increases initial preload

---

## 13. Context / Board Framing

- **"What is preload?"** → End-diastolic volume (EDV); fiber length before contraction
- **"How does mechanical ventilation affect preload?"** → High MAP/PEEP increases intrathoracic pressure → impedes venous return → reduces preload
- **"Why do volume boluses have limited efficacy in neonates?"** → Less compliant neonatal myocardium → already operating near the flat portion of the Starling curve; small increase in EDV causes large rise in EDP without meaningful SV gain
- **"Normal neonatal blood volume?"** → ~80-90 mL/kg (term); ~90-100 mL/kg (preterm)
- **"What echo finding suggests hypovolemia?"** → Small, hyperdynamic LV with near-obliteration of the cavity during systole ("kissing walls")

---

## 14. Edge Pearl

- The neonatal myocardium has a **higher proportion of non-contractile elements** (structural proteins, water) and a less organized sarcomeric architecture compared to the adult. This results in a stiffer, less compliant ventricle that tolerates volume loading poorly. A preterm infant's heart may be only marginally preload-responsive even when clinically volume-depleted — making echo-guided assessment critical before repeating volume boluses that risk IVH or pulmonary edema.
