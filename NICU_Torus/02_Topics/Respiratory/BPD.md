## 1. Identity

- **Bronchopulmonary Dysplasia (BPD)** is the most common serious chronic lung disease of prematurity, defined by the need for supplemental oxygen or positive pressure support at 36 weeks postmenstrual age (PMA) in infants born <32 weeks
- "New BPD" (post-surfactant era) is characterized by **arrested alveolar and vascular development** — alveolar simplification — rather than the fibrotic/emphysematous changes of "old BPD"
- Reflects disrupted alveolar stage of lung development (see [[Lung Development]])
- Affects 40–50% of infants born <28 weeks; major determinant of neurodevelopmental and pulmonary outcomes
- BPD-associated pulmonary hypertension (BPD-PH) is the most serious complication and significantly increases mortality

---

## 2. Taxonomy / Developmental Placement

- **Gestational axis**: Preterm — defined in infants born <32 weeks GA (primarily <28 weeks)
- **Phase axis**: Postnatal — develops over weeks to months in the NICU
- Definition requires survival to 36 weeks PMA (non-survivors cannot be diagnosed)
- Developmental ring: **Distinctions** — "new BPD" vs "old BPD" is a critical board distinction; severity definitions matter clinically

**Jensen 2019 Grading (current standard):**
| Grade | Criteria (at 36 weeks PMA) |
|---|---|
| Grade 1 (Mild) | Nasal cannula ≤2 L/min with FiO₂ ≤0.30 |
| Grade 2 (Moderate) | Nasal cannula >2 L/min OR FiO₂ >0.30 via NC/HFNC |
| Grade 3 (Severe) | Positive pressure support (CPAP, HFNC >3 L/min, MV) |
| Grade 3A | Non-invasive positive pressure |
| Grade 3B | Invasive mechanical ventilation |

**Old NICHD Definition (2001, historical):**
- Mild: room air by 36 weeks PMA
- Moderate: FiO₂ <30% at 36 weeks
- Severe: FiO₂ ≥30% or PPV at 36 weeks

---

## 3. Structure

**"Old BPD" (pre-surfactant era, Wilson-Mikity, classic BPD):**
- Oxygen toxicity + barotrauma + infection in large (>1 kg) preterm infants
- Pathology: squamous metaplasia, fibrosis, smooth muscle hypertrophy, emphysematous cyst formation, heterogeneous inflation
- CXR: coarse, cystic, heterogeneous changes; fibrotic streaks

**"New BPD" (post-surfactant era, current):**
- Occurs in extremely preterm (24–28 weeks) on gentle ventilation
- Pathology: **alveolar simplification** (fewer, larger, simplified alveoli), **dysmorphic microvascular pattern** (reduced capillary density, misaligned vessels), minimal fibrosis
- CXR: diffuse haziness, hyperinflation, some heterogeneity; may appear nearly normal early then show atelectasis/hyperinflation pattern
- Pathogenesis: interrupted alveolarization (normal in utero alveolar stage disrupted by preterm birth + inflammation + O₂ + mechanical injury)

**Vascular component:**
- Reduced capillary density (alveolar simplification = structural underdevelopment of both airspace and vasculature)
- Smooth muscle hypertrophy in arterioles → reactive PH
- BPD-PH: ~20–25% of severe BPD; bidirectional or right-to-left shunting at PFO/PDA

---

## 4. Function

- Severely impaired gas exchange: areas of atelectasis (V/Q <1) coexist with areas of overdistension (V/Q >1)
- Increased resistance: airway remodeling, bronchomalacia (dynamic collapse), mucus plugging
- Decreased compliance in fibrotic regions; increased compliance in overdistended regions
- Pulmonary hypertension: elevated PVR → RV pressure overload → right heart failure if severe
- Long τ in BPD: requires slow ventilator rates, long expiratory times (see [[Respiratory Mechanics]])

---

## 5. Dysfunction / Pathophysiology

**Four Pillars of BPD Pathogenesis:**

1. **Prematurity (arrested alveolar development)**: birth during saccular stage → interrupted alveolarization
2. **Mechanical injury (volutrauma/atelectrauma)**: overdistension injures type I cells; cyclic atelectasis injures alveolar epithelium; activates inflammatory cascade
3. **Oxygen toxicity**: reactive oxygen species → type II cell injury → reduced surfactant production; perpetuates inflammation
4. **Infection/Inflammation**: chorioamnionitis, postnatal sepsis → pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8, TNF-α) → disrupts alveolar signaling (VEGF ↓, TGF-β ↑) → simplification

**Additional contributors:**
- **PDA with left-to-right shunt**: pulmonary edema → surfactant inactivation → ↑ FiO₂/ventilation needed
- **Postnatal infection**: sepsis, NEC → systemic inflammation → lung inflammation
- **Nutritional deficiency**: inadequate vitamin A, antioxidants → reduced repair capacity
- **Genetic susceptibility**: SPOCK2, VEGF, NFATC2 variants associated with BPD risk

---

## 6. Clinical Phenotype

**Early (weeks 1–4):**
- Persistent or worsening oxygen requirement beyond expected RDS course
- Inability to wean ventilator or CPAP
- CXR: diffuse haziness, evolving heterogeneity

**Established BPD (36 weeks PMA):**
- Oxygen-dependent
- Tachypnea (baseline RR 60–80) — compensates with rapid shallow breathing
- Hyperinflated chest (barrel shape) from air trapping in obstructed regions
- Subcostal retractions
- CXR: hyperinflation, atelectasis, coarse interstitial markings

**Severe BPD:**
- Invasive ventilator-dependent at 36 weeks
- BPD-PH: elevated RV pressure on echo; pre/post-ductal SpO₂ difference
- Cor pulmonale: RV dilation, hypertrophy, eventual failure
- Growth failure: high caloric requirements from high WOB; aspiration risk

---

## 7. Differential / Distinctions

| Feature | New BPD | Old BPD | Chronic aspiration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Era | Post-surfactant | Pre-surfactant | Any |
| GA | <28 weeks | 30–35 weeks | Any |
| Pathology | Alveolar simplification | Fibrosis, cysts, metaplasia | Lipid-laden macrophages |
| CXR | Haziness, hyperinflation | Coarse, cystic | Perihilar, patchy |
| O₂ need | Yes | Yes | Variable |

- **BPD vs Wilson-Mikity**: late-onset bilateral cystic CXR, no vent history — distinct from new BPD
- **BPD vs chronic pulmonary HTN without parenchymal disease**: echo + CXR distinguish
- **BPD vs recurrent aspiration**: recurrent events, feeding-related, lipid-laden macrophages
- **BPD vs recurrent viral bronchiolitis**: ex-preterm infants have high rates of RSV/rhinovirus-related severe illness; may look like BPD exacerbations
- **BPD-PH vs cardiac disease**: echo distinguishes; BPD-PH has evidence of RV hypertension without structural cardiac defect

---

## 8. Diagnosis

- **Clinical definition**: oxygen/PPV requirement at 36 weeks PMA in infant born <32 weeks
- **Jensen 2019 criteria**: grade based on mode and level of support (see section 2)
- **CXR**: supportive; heterogeneous, hyperinflated pattern
- **Echocardiogram**: assess for BPD-PH (RV pressure, shunting); repeat every 4–8 weeks in severe BPD
- **BNP/NT-proBNP**: elevated with significant PH; useful monitoring tool
- **Pulmonary function testing** (if available): ↑ resistance, ↓ compliance, ↓ FEV₁ pattern
- **Bronchoscopy**: if bronchomalacia suspected (dynamic airway collapse); not routine

---

## 9. Management

**Prevention strategies (evidence-based):**
- **Antenatal betamethasone**: reduces BPD incidence by reducing RDS severity
- **Caffeine citrate**: start within first 3 days of life; reduces BPD (NNT ~6 in <1250 g infants)
- **CPAP from birth (not intubation)**: COIN, SUPPORT, CURPAP trials: CPAP-first → less BPD
- **Volume-targeted ventilation**: reduces volutrauma; less BPD than pressure-limited
- **LISA/MIST**: less BPD than traditional intubation/surfactant
- **Permissive hypercapnia**: target PaCO₂ 45–55 mmHg; reduces barotrauma; lower pressures needed
- **Conservative fluid management**: restrict fluids; avoid unnecessary fluid boluses
- **Nutritional support**: early, adequate protein and calories; supports lung repair

**Treatment of established BPD:**
- Optimize O₂: target SpO₂ 92–95% (avoid hypoxia → PH; avoid hyperoxia → O₂ toxicity)
- **Caffeine**: continue until 36 weeks PMA or beyond if needed
- **Diuretics**: furosemide (1 mg/kg/dose) for acute fluid overload; thiazide/spironolactone for chronic
- **Bronchodilators**: albuterol, ipratropium for bronchospasm/bronchomalacia; do not reduce BPD
- Gradual ventilator weaning; avoid frequent extubation/reintubation cycles

---

## 10. Therapeutics

**Caffeine (most important pharmacologic intervention):**
- CAP trial: caffeine vs placebo in <1250g → caffeine ↓ BPD 36% vs 47%, ↓ PDA ligation, ↓ MV duration
- Mechanism: ↑ respiratory drive, anti-inflammatory, improves diaphragm contractility, promotes alveolarization (animal data)
- Start day 1–3; continue until apnea-free and respiratory support weaned

**Vitamin A:**
- Supports epithelial integrity and alveolar development
- NICHD trial: IM vitamin A (5000 IU TIW × 4 weeks) in ELBW → modest ↓ BPD (NNT ~15)
- Less commonly used in modern practice (IM administration, modest benefit)

**Postnatal steroids:**
- **Dexamethasone** (high-dose/early): effective at extubating BPD patients; significant risk of neurodevelopmental harm (cerebral palsy) — AVOID routine use in first 2 weeks
- **Low-dose dexamethasone** (DART protocol: 0.075 mg/kg/d tapering): may reduce BPD with less neurotoxicity; reasonable for ventilator-dependent BPD at >2–3 weeks
- **Hydrocortisone** (low-dose): may reduce BPD with less neurotoxic effect; more data needed
- **Inhaled budesonide + surfactant**: NEUROSIS trial showed ↓ BPD but ↑ mortality — not recommended

**BPD-PH treatment:**
- Optimize oxygenation (O₂ is the best pulmonary vasodilator)
- **Sildenafil** (PDE5 inhibitor): first-line for BPD-PH; oral; improve PVR; watch for systemic hypotension
- **Bosentan** (ET-1 receptor antagonist): second-line; monitor LFTs
- **iNO**: not routine for BPD-PH; may be used acutely during PH crises
- **Diuretics**: reduce RV preload; furosemide + aldactone standard

---

## 11. Complications / Outcomes

**NICU complications:**
- Cor pulmonale / right heart failure: most feared complication; high mortality
- Pulmonary hypertensive crises: during intercurrent illness
- Tracheostomy: in ventilator-dependent severe BPD (10–15% of severe cases)
- Recurrent pulmonary infections: RSV, rhinovirus, parainfluenza → prolonged hospitalizations
- Subglottic stenosis: from prolonged intubation

**Long-term pulmonary outcomes:**
- Persistent airflow obstruction: reduced FEV₁ at school age (mean ~70–80% predicted)
- Higher rates of asthma diagnosis
- Reduced exercise capacity
- Emphysematous changes on CT in adulthood (some studies)
- Possible contribution to COPD in adulthood (ongoing research)

**Neurodevelopmental outcomes:**
- BPD independently worsens neurodevelopmental outcomes beyond prematurity alone
- Higher rates of cognitive impairment, cerebral palsy, behavioral issues
- Mechanism: chronic hypoxia, corticosteroid exposure, prolonged hospitalization

---

## 12. Associations / Genetics / Maternal-Fetal Links

- **Chorioamnionitis**: acute → may stimulate maturation; chronic → worst BPD outcomes (Watterberg data)
- **Male sex**: 2× higher BPD rate than female at same GA
- **Genetic factors**:
  - **SPOCK2**: BPD susceptibility locus
  - **VEGF polymorphisms**: reduced VEGF → impaired vasculogenesis → BPD
  - **SP-B, SP-C variants**: mild functional deficiency → susceptibility
- **Down syndrome (Trisomy 21)**: alveolar simplification inherent → BPD phenotype even without RDS
- **Maternal preeclampsia**: associated with lower BPD rates (possible antenatal stress effect)

## 13. Context / Board Framing

- **"Jensen 2019 definition?"** → Based on mode of respiratory support at 36 weeks PMA: Grade 1 = NC ≤2 L; Grade 2 = NC >2 L or FiO₂ >0.30; Grade 3 = PPV (CPAP, HFNC, MV)
- **"New vs old BPD pathology?"** → New = alveolar simplification + vascular simplification; Old = fibrosis + cysts + metaplasia
- **"Most important pharmacologic prevention?"** → Caffeine (CAP trial)
- **"Why avoid early high-dose dexamethasone?"** → Cerebral palsy risk
- **"BPD-PH treatment?"** → Optimize O₂, sildenafil, bosentan; echo monitoring
- **"Four pillars of BPD pathogenesis?"** → Prematurity, mechanical injury, O₂ toxicity, infection/inflammation
- **"Why does new BPD look different on CXR?"** → Simplification not fibrosis; less cystic/coarse; more diffuse haziness and hyperinflation

---

## 14. Edge Pearl

- **BPD-PH dramatically changes prognosis**: a preterm infant with severe BPD but no pulmonary hypertension has a very different trajectory than one with BPD-PH. BPD-PH is present in ~20–25% of severe BPD cases and carries a 2-year mortality of up to 30–40%. Every infant with severe BPD should have **serial echocardiograms** (every 4–8 weeks) to screen for PH, because early detection and treatment (oxygen optimization, sildenafil) may prevent progression to cor pulmonale and improve survival. The cardinal error is attributing all oxygen dependency to parenchymal BPD without screening for the vascular component.
