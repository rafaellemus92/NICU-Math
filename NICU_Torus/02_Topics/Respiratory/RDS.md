## 1. Identity

- **Respiratory Distress Syndrome (RDS)**, formerly Hyaline Membrane Disease (HMD), is a disease of surfactant deficiency primarily affecting preterm neonates
- Results in diffuse alveolar atelectasis, progressive hypoxemia, and increased work of breathing
- The most common serious respiratory condition in preterm infants
- Nearly universal in infants born <28 weeks; frequency decreases with advancing GA
- Treated with exogenous surfactant, CPAP/mechanical ventilation, and antenatal steroids (prevention)
- Uncontrolled → BPD, air leak, PPHN; modern treatment has dramatically improved outcomes

---

## 2. Taxonomy / Developmental Placement

- **Gestational axis**: Preterm — defined by insufficient type II pneumocyte maturation
- **Phase axis**: Transitional/Postnatal — manifests at birth, worsens over first 48–72h without treatment
- Developmental stage: insufficient saccular stage surfactant production
- Developmental ring: **Identity** — the prototype neonatal respiratory disease
- After surfactant era: RDS remains common but mortality rare; BPD is the primary downstream consequence

---

## 3. Structure

**Pathologic Basis:**
- Insufficient [[Surfactant]] → alveolar surface tension not reduced → alveolar collapse at end-expiration
- **Laplace's law** (P = 2T/r): small alveoli with high surface tension collapse first → atelectasis cascade
- Each breath requires re-recruitment of collapsed alveoli → massive elastic work of breathing
- Protein-rich edema floods alveoli (disrupted alveolar-capillary barrier from stretching)
- Edema proteins further inactivate residual surfactant
- **Hyaline membranes**: eosinophilic deposits of fibrin, necrotic cells, proteins lining alveolar ducts → histologic hallmark (seen at autopsy in untreated cases)

**Radiographic Pattern:**
- **Ground-glass opacification**: diffuse, bilateral, symmetric haziness from microatelectasis
- **Air bronchograms**: air-filled bronchi visible against collapsed opacified lung parenchyma
- Decreased lung volumes
- Bell-shaped chest (reduced AP diameter)

---

## 4. Function (Disrupted)

- Without surfactant: FRC cannot be maintained → lung volume approaches closing capacity with each breath
- Progressive atelectasis → V/Q mismatch → intrapulmonary shunting → hypoxemia
- Hypoxemia → pulmonary vasoconstriction → PVR rises → right-to-left shunting → worsening hypoxemia
- Progressive hypercarbia as respiratory muscles fatigue
- Apnea of prematurity superimposed on RDS → further hypoxemia

---

## 5. Dysfunction / Pathophysiology

**Primary mechanism:**
- Preterm type II pneumocytes: insufficient lamellar body content, low DPPC synthesis, immature surfactant protein expression

**Amplifying factors:**
- Pulmonary edema (from PDA, fluid overload): inactivates surfactant, floods alveoli
- Aspiration (blood, meconium, formula): chemical inactivation of surfactant
- Infection/sepsis: cytokine-mediated surfactant degradation
- Oxygen toxicity: injures type II cells → reduces endogenous surfactant production

**Natural course without treatment:**
- Birth: mild-moderate distress
- Hours 6–12: worsening as residual surfactant depleted, edema increases
- Hours 24–48: peak severity (oxygen requirement maximal)
- Day 3–4: type II cell recovery begins if no superimposed complications
- Resolution: **recovery diuresis** — endogenous cortisol rising → [[ENaC and Fetal Lung Fluid|ENaC activates]] → Na⁺ absorbed from airspace → fluid clears. The recovery diuresis at ~72h coincides with ENaC finally activating (same mechanism as [[TTN]] resolution)
- With treatment: dramatically accelerated recovery
- **Antenatal betamethasone primes BOTH surfactant production AND ENaC transcription simultaneously** — one drug, two targets

---

## 6. Clinical Phenotype

**Signs (present at birth or within first hours):**
- Tachypnea (RR >60)
- **Grunting**: auto-PEEP generation to maintain FRC
- Nasal flaring
- Subcostal and intercostal retractions
- Cyanosis
- Pallor, poor perfusion (if severe)

**Severity grading (Silverman-Anderson score or CRIB score)**:
- Mild: minimal O₂ requirement (<30%), resolves quickly with surfactant
- Moderate: FiO₂ 30–50%, CPAP or brief intubation needed
- Severe: FiO₂ >50%, prolonged mechanical ventilation, high risk of air leak and BPD

**Risk factors for RDS:**
- **Prematurity** (most important; <28 weeks = ~100%)
- **Male sex** (androgens delay surfactant maturation)
- **Cesarean section without labor** (no cortisol surge, no catecholamines)
- **Maternal diabetes** (hyperinsulinemia antagonizes cortisol-driven type II maturation)
- **Perinatal asphyxia** (disrupts surfactant synthesis)
- **Chorioamnionitis** (acute: may stimulate maturation; chronic: worsens inflammation)

**Protective factors:**
- **Antenatal corticosteroids** (most important protection)
- Female sex
- Prolonged PPROM (chronic stress → cortisol surge)
- Intrauterine growth restriction (stress → cortisol)
- African American race (mechanism unclear)

## 7. Differential / Distinctions

| Diagnosis | GA | CXR | Course | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RDS | Preterm | Ground-glass, air bronchograms | Worsens then improves | Responds to surfactant |
| TTN | Term/late preterm | Wet, hyperinflated | Improves in 24–72h | C-section, no risk factors |
| MAS | Term | Patchy, hyperinflated | Variable | Meconium-stained fluid |
| GBS pneumonia | Any | May mimic RDS | Does not resolve easily | Positive culture, sepsis |
| PPHN | Term > preterm | Often normal | Labile SpO₂ | Pre/post-ductal gradient |
| Genetic surfactant deficiency | Term | Ground-glass | No response to surfactant | Family history, genetic |

- **RDS vs GBS pneumonia**: CXR identical; culture, CBC, clinical history distinguish; treat both empirically
- **RDS vs [[TTN]]**: TTN = larger infants, rapid resolution <72h, wet lungs on CXR with perihilar streaking + fluid in fissures + hyperinflation, **NO air bronchograms** (alveoli open, just wet). RDS = preterm, ground-glass with air bronchograms, low volumes. Both have wet lungs but different alveolar state: **collapsed (RDS) vs open (TTN)**
- **RDS vs [[Meconium Aspiration Syndrome|MAS]]**: MAS in term/post-term with meconium-stained fluid; patchy CXR with hyperinflation + flat diaphragms (vs ground-glass in RDS)
- **RDS vs [[Congenital Diaphragmatic Hernia|CDH]]**: scaphoid abdomen, dextrocardia, bowel sounds in chest

---

## 8. Diagnosis

- **Clinical + CXR**: sufficient in typical case (preterm, ground-glass, air bronchograms, low volumes)
- **Blood gas**: hypoxemia (↓ PaO₂), hypercarbia (↑ PaCO₂), respiratory acidosis
- **SpO₂ monitoring**: continuous; assess FiO₂ requirement
- **L/S ratio** (amniocentesis): if delivery decision pending; **L/S >2 predicts maturity**; **PG (phosphatidylglycerol) presence = definitive maturity marker**
- **Gastric aspirate lamellar body count**: rapid, bedside; <25,000/μL = immature
- **Lung ultrasound**: bilateral B-lines (absent A-lines), lung consolidation pattern; high sensitivity/specificity
- **Echocardiogram**: if PPHN suspected or to evaluate for PDA

---

## 9. Management

**Prevention:**
- **Antenatal betamethasone** 24–34+6 weeks (extend to 22–23 for periviable): most impactful prevention
  - Reduces RDS incidence ~50%, severity substantially, IVH, NEC, mortality
  - 48h–7 days after first dose = peak effect
- **Rescue course**: if >2 weeks since initial course and still <34 weeks
- **CPAP at delivery room** (for VLBW): reduces need for surfactant, reduces BPD (COIN, SUPPORT trials)

**Respiratory support:**
- **CPAP first strategy**: start CPAP (5–8 cmH₂O) at birth for VLBW; give surfactant if FiO₂ >0.30
- **Mechanical ventilation**: if CPAP fails, apnea, severe disease
  - Volume-targeted ventilation preferred: reduces BPD, pneumothorax (see [[Respiratory Mechanics]])
  - Target tidal volume 4–6 mL/kg
  - PEEP 4–6 cmH₂O to maintain FRC

**Surfactant therapy:**
- **INSURE** (Intubate-Surfactant-Extubate to CPAP): brief intubation; minimize ventilator exposure
- **LISA/MIST** (Less Invasive Surfactant Administration): thin catheter while on CPAP; infant breathing spontaneously; ↓ BPD, IVH vs INSURE in some trials; preferred where expertise available
- **Early rescue** (within 2h of birth) superior to late rescue
- **Dose**: poractant 200 mg/kg first dose; 100 mg/kg subsequent doses (q6–12h × 2 max if still intubated)

---

## 10. Therapeutics

**Surfactant agents:**
- Poractant alfa (Curosurf): 200 mg/kg first dose; porcine, high phospholipid concentration
- Beractant (Survanta): 100 mg/kg; bovine
- Calfactant (Infasurf): 105 mg/kg; high SP-B

**Adjuncts:**
- **Caffeine citrate**: start early (day 1); reduces apnea, reduces BPD, improves neurodevelopmental outcomes; loading dose 20 mg/kg, maintenance 5–10 mg/kg/day
- **Vitamin A** (IM): modest reduction in BPD in ELBW; less commonly used now
- **Diuretics**: only if fluid overload documented; not routine for RDS

**Avoid:**
- Routine early postnatal steroids (dexamethasone): increases cerebral palsy risk if given in first week
- Hyperoxia: target SpO₂ 90–95%
- Aggressive fluid administration: worsens pulmonary edema, PDA

---

## 11. Complications / Outcomes

**Acute complications:**
- **Air leak** (pneumothorax, PIE, pneumomediastinum): from overdistension or CPAP; see [[Air Leak]]
- **PDA**: fall in PVR with surfactant → ↑ left-to-right PDA shunt → pulmonary hemorrhage
- **Pulmonary hemorrhage**: acute, hemorrhagic surfactant inactivation; often complicates PDA + surfactant
- **Apnea**: universal in <28 weeks; caffeine treats; may require brief reintubation

**Chronic complications:**
- **BPD** (see [[BPD]]): most significant long-term consequence; occurs in 40–50% of infants <28 weeks
- **Retinopathy of prematurity (ROP)**: oxygen exposure
- **Intraventricular hemorrhage (IVH)**: hypoxemia, hemodynamic instability

**Outcomes (modern era):**
- Mortality from RDS alone: <5% in well-resourced NICUs
- Pre-surfactant era: ~50% mortality in extremely preterm
- Neurodevelopmental outcomes: related to BPD, IVH, prematurity — not RDS per se

---

## 12. Associations / Genetics / Maternal-Fetal Links

- **Maternal diabetes**: delays surfactant maturation; higher RDS risk even at 36–37 weeks
- **Maternal steroids**: single most effective intervention; binds glucocorticoid receptors in type II pneumocytes → ↑ surfactant gene expression
- **Male sex**: androgens delay type II maturation; females have ~2× advantage in lung maturity at equivalent GA
- **Twin gestation**: second twin at higher RDS risk (more likely to be malpresented, less labor exposure)
- **Genetic SP-B/ABCA3 deficiency**: mimics RDS in term infants; must be considered when term infant fails multiple surfactant doses
- **Chorioamnionitis**: acute may accelerate maturation (cytokine stimulation of type II cells); chronic inflammatory exposure worsens lung injury

## 13. Context / Board Framing

- **"Classic CXR of RDS?"** → Ground-glass opacification, air bronchograms, low lung volumes
- **"Most important prevention?"** → Antenatal betamethasone 24–34+6 weeks
- **"Risk factors?"** → Prematurity, male, C-section without labor, maternal DM, asphyxia
- **"Protective factors?"** → Steroids, female, PPROM, IUGR, Black race
- **"INSURE vs LISA?"** → LISA: thin catheter, no intubation, breathing on CPAP; ↓ BPD/IVH; preferred where available
- **"Why does maternal DM increase RDS?"** → Fetal hyperinsulinemia antagonizes cortisol-mediated surfactant maturation
- **"When does RDS peak?"** → Hours 6–12 as surfactant depletes; improves day 3–4 as type II cells recover
- **"Recovery diuresis?"** → ~72h: endogenous cortisol rising → ENaC activates → Na⁺ absorbed from airspace → fluid clears (same ENaC mechanism as TTN resolution)
- **"Betamethasone does what exactly?"** → Primes BOTH surfactant production (type II pneumocytes) AND ENaC transcription (Na⁺ absorption) — one drug, two targets
- **"RDS in term infant?"** → Think genetic surfactant deficiency (SP-B, ABCA3)

## 14. Edge Pearl

- **The LISA technique changes NRP reflex**: the traditional sequence of intubate → surfactant → extubate (INSURE) is giving way to LISA where a 5-Fr feeding catheter is inserted under direct laryngoscopy while the infant remains on CPAP and breathes spontaneously. The surfactant is instilled slowly while watching the SpO₂. No PPV, no ETT, no prolonged intubation. Meta-analyses show LISA reduces BPD and severe IVH compared to INSURE. The key skill is **visualizing the cords without intubating** — a subtle but important procedural evolution. In units without LISA expertise, INSURE remains an excellent approach.
