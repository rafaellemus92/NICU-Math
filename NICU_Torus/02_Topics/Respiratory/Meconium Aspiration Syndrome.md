## 1. Identity

- **Meconium Aspiration Syndrome (MAS)** is respiratory distress from aspiration of meconium-stained amniotic fluid (MSAF), causing airway obstruction, chemical pneumonitis, surfactant inactivation, and PPHN
- Occurs in term/post-term infants — meconium passage rare before 34 weeks
- MSAF present in ~12–15% of deliveries; MAS develops in ~5% of those with MSAF
- Late gestation fetal stress → meconium passage → aspiration (in utero gasping or during delivery)

---

## 2. Taxonomy / Developmental Placement

- **Gestational axis**: Term/post-term — fetal GI maturation required for meconium passage
- **Phase axis**: Transitional/postnatal — perinatal event with postnatal manifestation
- Risk factors: **post-term**, SGA, fetal distress, oligohydramnios, cord compression
- Developmental ring: **Identity** — distinct disease with multi-mechanism pathophysiology

---

## 3. Structure

**Meconium Composition:**
- Fetal intestinal secretions, bile acids, mucus, desquamated cells, lanugo, vernix
- Bile acids are potent surfactant inactivators and chemical irritants

**Pathologic Mechanisms (4 simultaneous):**
1. **Mechanical obstruction**: meconium plugs act as ball-valve → air enters on inspiration, cannot exit → air trapping → hyperinflation → [[Air Leak]] risk
2. **Chemical pneumonitis**: bile acids and enzymes → direct injury to airway and alveolar epithelium → inflammatory cascade
3. **Surfactant inactivation**: meconium components INACTIVATE [[Surfactant]] → secondary surfactant deficiency → atelectasis
4. **Pulmonary vasoconstriction**: hypoxia + inflammation → elevated PVR → [[Pulmonary Hypertension|PPHN]]

**CXR Pattern:**
- **Patchy bilateral infiltrates** (asymmetric)
- **Hyperinflation** with flattened diaphragms (air trapping)
- Coarse, irregular opacities
- NOT ground-glass (vs RDS); NOT perihilar streaking (vs TTN)

---

## 4. Function

- Ball-valve mechanism: partial obstruction → unidirectional air flow → progressive distal overdistension → alveolar rupture → pneumothorax, PIE
- Surfactant inactivation → ↑ surface tension → atelectasis coexists with air-trapped regions → severe V/Q mismatch
- Chemical pneumonitis → intense neutrophilic inflammation → edema → further surfactant destruction
- PPHN: hypoxemia + acidosis + inflammation → pulmonary vasoconstriction → right-to-left shunting → worsening hypoxemia (self-perpetuating cycle)

---

## 5. Dysfunction / Pathophysiology

- Fetal stress (cord compression, uteroplacental insufficiency) → vagal stimulation → meconium passage into amniotic fluid
- In utero gasping (asphyxia) or aspiration during delivery → meconium reaches distal airways
- Thick meconium worse than thin (more obstruction, more inflammation)
- **MAS → PPHN** is the most common severe pathway — PPHN is the primary driver of mortality in MAS

---

## 6. Clinical Phenotype

- **Term/post-term infant** with MSAF at delivery
- Respiratory distress within first hours: tachypnea, grunting, retractions, cyanosis
- Barrel-shaped/hyperinflated chest
- Meconium staining of skin, nails, umbilical cord (if prolonged exposure)
- **Labile oxygenation** if PPHN develops (pre/post-ductal gradient)
- Severity: mild (O₂ only) → moderate (ventilator) → severe (PPHN → ECMO)

---

## 7. Differential / Distinctions

| Feature | MAS | [[RDS]] | [[TTN]] | Pneumonia |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GA | Term/post-term | Preterm | Term/late preterm | Any |
| MSAF | Yes | No | No | No |
| CXR | Patchy + hyperinflated | Ground-glass + low volumes | Wet + hyperinflated | Focal infiltrates |
| Air bronchograms | Variable | YES | NO | Variable |
| Mechanism | Obstruction + inactivation | Deficiency | ENaC failure | Infection |

- **MAS vs RDS**: different populations (term vs preterm), different CXR (patchy vs ground-glass), MSAF present in MAS
- **MAS vs pneumonia**: MSAF history, CXR pattern; can coexist (meconium may promote bacterial growth)

---

## 8. Diagnosis

- **Clinical**: MSAF at delivery + respiratory distress in term/post-term infant
- **CXR**: patchy bilateral infiltrates, hyperinflation, flattened diaphragms
- **Blood gas**: hypoxemia, possible hypercarbia, acidosis
- **Pre/post-ductal SpO₂**: gradient >5–10% suggests PPHN component
- **OI (Oxygenation Index)**: OI = (MAP × FiO₂ × 100) / PaO₂; OI >25 = severe; OI >40 = ECMO criteria
- **Echo**: assess for PPHN (TR jet, septal bowing, PDA shunt direction)
- **Blood culture/CBC**: rule out concurrent sepsis

---

## 9. Management

**Delivery Room:**
- **Routine intrapartum suctioning NO LONGER recommended** (2015 NRP guideline change)
- Only suction if airway obstruction present (no spontaneous breathing, obstruction evident)
- Standard NRP resuscitation; intubate for severe distress

**Respiratory Support:**
- Supplemental O₂, CPAP, or mechanical ventilation based on severity
- **[[Surfactant]] replacement**: effective because meconium INACTIVATES surfactant — exogenous surfactant replaces what meconium destroyed
- [[High-Frequency Ventilation|HFOV]]: useful for severe MAS with diffuse disease
- Avoid excessive PEEP: air trapping already a problem

**PPHN Management:**
- **iNO** (20 ppm): first-line for PPHN component
- Optimize lung recruitment, sedation, avoid acidosis
- **Sildenafil, milrinone** as adjuncts
- **ECMO**: if OI >40 or refractory hypoxemia despite maximal therapy

---

## 10. Therapeutics

- **Surfactant**: 100–200 mg/kg; may need repeated doses (meconium continues to inactivate)
- **iNO**: selective pulmonary vasodilator for PPHN; 20 ppm starting dose
- **Antibiotics**: empiric coverage often started (meconium is a bacterial culture medium; cannot clinically exclude sepsis)
- **Sedation**: minimize agitation-triggered PVR spikes
- **ECMO**: VA-ECMO for combined cardiac + respiratory failure; VV-ECMO if cardiac function adequate

---

## 11. Complications / Outcomes

**Acute:**
- **PPHN**: most common severe complication; primary driver of mortality
- **Air leak** (pneumothorax, pneumomediastinum): ball-valve mechanism → overdistension → rupture
- **Respiratory failure** requiring ECMO

**Outcomes:**
- Mild MAS: excellent prognosis, resolves 2–4 days
- Severe MAS with PPHN: mortality 5–10% in ECMO-capable centers
- **Neurodevelopmental**: related to severity of hypoxia and ECMO exposure; 10–20% may have long-term issues
- Most survivors have normal long-term pulmonary function

---

## 12. Associations / Genetics / Maternal-Fetal Links

- **Post-term pregnancy**: strongest association (mature GI → meconium passage, oligohydramnios → thick meconium)
- **SGA/IUGR**: chronic fetal stress → meconium passage
- **Cord compression/oligohydramnios**: fetal distress trigger
- **Chorioamnionitis**: may compound inflammation
- No genetic predisposition — entirely a perinatal mechanical/chemical event

---

## 13. Context / Board Framing

- **"Routine suctioning at delivery?"** → NO longer recommended (2015 NRP); only if airway obstruction
- **"Why does surfactant work in MAS?"** → Meconium INACTIVATES endogenous surfactant; exogenous replacement restores surface tension reduction
- **"CXR of MAS?"** → Patchy infiltrates + hyperinflation + flat diaphragms (NOT ground-glass like RDS)
- **"Most common severe complication?"** → PPHN
- **"Ball-valve mechanism?"** → Meconium plugs allow air in but not out → progressive air trapping → rupture → pneumothorax
- **"ECMO criteria?"** → OI >40 on two ABGs 30 min apart
- **"Post-term risk?"** → Major risk factor: mature GI passes meconium + oligohydramnios concentrates it

---

## 14. Edge Pearl

- **The 2015 NRP paradigm shift**: for decades, the reflex was to intubate and suction meconium from the trachea of every non-vigorous infant born through MSAF. The evidence showed this did NOT reduce MAS severity or improve outcomes, and it delayed ventilation. Current NRP: proceed with standard resuscitation steps. Only intervene for visible airway obstruction. The board question tests whether you still reflexively intubate-to-suction (wrong) vs resuscitate normally (right). Meanwhile, surfactant replacement in established MAS is effective precisely because meconium inactivates surfactant — the exogenous dose replaces what meconium destroyed, not what was never made (unlike RDS).
