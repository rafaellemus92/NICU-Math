## 1. Identity

- **Lung Development** describes the five sequential stages of pulmonary organogenesis occurring from early embryogenesis through childhood
- Completion of each stage determines viability threshold, surfactant capacity, and long-term respiratory resilience
- Disruption at any stage underlies major neonatal respiratory disease ([[Pulmonary Hypoplasia]], [[RDS]], [[BPD]])

---

## 2. Taxonomy / Developmental Placement

| Stage | Gestational Age | Key Events |
|---|---|---|
| Embryonic | 3–7 weeks | Lung bud, trachea, primary bronchi |
| Pseudoglandular | 5–17 weeks | Airway branching, no gas exchange |
| Canalicular | 16–26 weeks | Acinar formation, capillary proximity, type II cells appear |
| Saccular | 24–38 weeks | Saccules expand, surfactant secretion begins |
| Alveolar | 36 weeks–8 years | True alveoli form; ~20–50M at birth → 300M adult |

- Stages overlap; timing is approximate and varies by individual
- **Viability threshold** ~22–24 weeks = late canalicular/early saccular (capillaries reach air spaces)

---

## 3. Structure

- **Embryonic stage**: ventral foregut outgrowth → lung bud → two mainstem bronchi; tracheoesophageal septation
- **Pseudoglandular**: dichotomous branching (~23 generations); cartilage, smooth muscle, ciliated epithelium laid down; gland-like appearance histologically
- **Canalicular**: mesenchyme thins; capillary ingrowth; airspace lumens widen; type II pneumocytes differentiate into type I
- **Saccular**: terminal saccules lined by thin type I cells separated by double capillary loops; secondary crests begin to form
- **Alveolar**: secondary septal thinning; single capillary loops; alveolar multiplication continues postnatally

**Key transcription factors:**
- **TTF-1 (NKX2.1)**: master regulator of lung epithelial identity; also governs surfactant protein and thyroid gene expression
- **FGF10/FGFR2**: epithelial–mesenchymal signaling driving branching morphogenesis
- **Sonic Hedgehog (SHH)**: patterning; interacts with mesenchyme
- **Wnt/β-catenin**: proximal-distal patterning, type II cell maintenance
- **GATA6, FOXA2 (HNF-3β)**: differentiation of distal epithelium

**Branching morphogenesis**: stereotyped program of ~23 airway generations; tips express FGF10, stalks express Sprouty2 (inhibitor); creates non-random, reproducible airway tree

---

## 4. Function

- Each stage adds structural capacity enabling eventual gas exchange:
  - Pseudoglandular → airways conduct future airflow
  - Canalicular → establishes first gas-exchange units; type II cells begin [[Surfactant]] synthesis
  - Saccular → sufficient surface area for extrauterine survival (minimal viable ~24 wks)
  - Alveolar → exponential increase in gas-exchange surface area; continues postnatally
- [[Fetal Lung Fluid]] distension drives growth; fetal breathing movements stimulate branching

---

## 5. Dysfunction / Pathophysiology

- **Embryonic disruption**: tracheoesophageal fistula, pulmonary agenesis
- **Pseudoglandular disruption**: congenital pulmonary airway malformation (CPAM), bronchogenic cysts
- **Canalicular disruption**: extreme preterm birth → surfactant deficiency ([[RDS]]), insufficient gas exchange units
- **Space-occupying lesions** (CDH, CCAM) during pseudoglandular/canalicular → [[Pulmonary Hypoplasia]]
- **Saccular disruption**: preterm birth → incomplete saccule formation; antenatal steroids accelerate maturation
- **Alveolar disruption**: O₂ toxicity, mechanical ventilation, inflammation → alveolar simplification ([[BPD]])

---

## 6. Clinical Phenotype

- **<24 weeks**: usually not viable; no sufficient gas-exchange surface
- **24–28 weeks**: severe RDS, high BPD risk; surfactant-dependent
- **28–34 weeks**: significant RDS risk; antenatal steroids markedly reduce severity
- **34–36 weeks (late preterm)**: subtle surfactant insufficiency; TTN more common
- **>36 weeks**: near-complete alveolar formation; lung disease usually not developmental

---

## 7. Differential / Distinctions

- **Pulmonary hypoplasia**: reduced total lung growth (mass and DNA content); not a stage arrest but an arrest of quantity
- **CPAM**: abnormal branching at pseudoglandular stage → hamartomatous mass
- **CDH**: not a primary lung defect; herniated viscera compress normally developing lung

---

## 8. Diagnosis

- **Prenatal**: fetal MRI lung volume, o/e LHR (lung-to-head ratio) for CDH/hypoplasia
- **Postnatal**: CXR lung volumes, need for respiratory support correlates with developmental maturity
- **Histology**: lung biopsy confirms stage of development in rare cases (e.g., ACD, [[Alveolar Capillary Dysplasia]])

---

## 9. Management

- **Antenatal corticosteroids** (betamethasone 24–34+6 weeks, consider 22–23 weeks): accelerate type II cell maturation, surfactant synthesis, structural saccular formation
- **Magnesium sulfate** <32 weeks: neuroprotection; indirect respiratory benefit
- **Caffeine** postnatally: stimulates respiratory drive; reduces BPD
- **Gentle ventilation**: minimize volutrauma during alveolar stage; protect against BPD

---

## 10. Therapeutics

- **Antenatal steroids**: most evidence-based intervention; reduce RDS, IVH, NEC, mortality
- **Postnatal surfactant**: replaces deficient [[Surfactant]] in RDS
- **Postnatal steroids (systemic)**: reserved for severe BPD; risk of neurodevelopmental harm limits routine use
- **Vitamin A**: supports alveolar development; reduces BPD in VLBW (modest effect)

---

## 11. Complications / Outcomes

- Disrupted alveolar stage → reduced total alveolar number → lifelong reduced pulmonary reserve
- Survivors of extreme prematurity: increased asthma, exercise intolerance, reduced FEV₁ in adulthood
- BPD with pulmonary hypertension: significant morbidity/mortality
- CPAM: risk of infection, malignant transformation (controversial), respiratory compromise

---

## 12. Associations / Genetics / Maternal-Fetal Links

- **TTF-1 mutations**: congenital hypothyroidism + neonatal respiratory distress syndrome + neurological issues ("brain-thyroid-lung" syndrome)
- **FGFR2 mutations**: Apert/Crouzon syndrome → airway anomalies
- **Maternal diabetes**: delays surfactant maturation (hyperinsulinism antagonizes cortisol effect)
- **Oligohydramnios**: reduces [[Fetal Lung Fluid]] distension → hypoplasia
- **Smoking**: reduces airway branching, alters smooth muscle tone

---

## 13. Context / Board Framing

- **"What stage allows extrauterine viability?"** → Canalicular (capillaries reach air spaces ~24 weeks)
- **"Antenatal steroids work on which stage?"** → Saccular → accelerate type II differentiation, surfactant expression
- **"TTF-1 function?"** → Lung, thyroid, brain epithelial differentiation; mutation = brain-thyroid-lung triad
- **"Fetal breathing movement function?"** → Mechanical distension driving branching; absence → hypoplasia
- **"Most alveoli form when?"** → Postnatally, from 36 weeks through age ~8 years

---

## 14. Edge Pearl

- **Alveolar multiplication is postnatal**: a term infant has only ~20–50 million alveoli; adult has ~300 million. This means NICU interventions during the alveolar stage (postnatal weeks–months) can permanently reduce alveolar number — making gentle ventilation and anti-inflammatory strategies essential for lifelong lung health.
- **FGF10 knockout mice** have no lungs — only a trachea — underscoring the indispensable role of epithelial–mesenchymal FGF signaling in branching morphogenesis.
