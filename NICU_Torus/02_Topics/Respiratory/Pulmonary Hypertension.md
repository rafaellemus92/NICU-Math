## 1. Identity

- **Neonatal pulmonary hypertension (PH)** is a syndrome of elevated pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) causing right-to-left or bidirectional shunting at the foramen ovale and/or ductus arteriosus, resulting in severe hypoxemia disproportionate to parenchymal lung disease
- When occurring in the perinatal period, termed **Persistent Pulmonary Hypertension of the Newborn (PPHN)**
- A spectrum of pathophysiology: failed vascular transition, maladaptive remodeling, pulmonary underdevelopment, or fixed anatomic abnormality
- Untreated: progressive hypoxemia → RV failure → death

---

## 2. Taxonomy / Developmental Placement

**Three Pathophysiologic Types:**

| Type | Mechanism | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Failed Transition (Acute)** | Normal vascular anatomy, abnormal vasoreactivity; fails to vasodilate at birth | Idiopathic PPHN, MAS, sepsis, birth asphyxia |
| **Maladaptive Remodeling** | Abnormal intrauterine vascular remodeling; structural changes | CDH, chronic hypoxia, NSAID exposure |
| **Underdevelopment** | Insufficient pulmonary vascular bed | Pulmonary hypoplasia, CDH |
| **BPD-PH** | Postnatal alveolar-vascular mismatch, inflammatory remodeling | Chronic BPD |
| **Fixed Anatomic** | Alveolar capillary dysplasia; irreversible | ACD (see [[Alveolar Capillary Dysplasia]]) |

- Developmental ring: **Distinctions** — critical to distinguish type because management differs
- Gestational axis: spans fetal (CDH remodeling) through postnatal (BPD-PH)

---

## 3. Structure

**Pulmonary Vascular Anatomy in PPHN:**
- Pulmonary arterioles: medial smooth muscle hypertrophy → thick-walled vessels
- In CDH/hypoplasia: reduced total vascular cross-sectional area (underdeveloped bed)
- In ACD: misaligned pulmonary veins running alongside arteries in broncho-vascular bundles instead of interlobular septa

**Vasoactive Mediator Imbalance:**
- ↓ NO (endothelial NOS activity reduced) → ↓ cGMP → vasoconstriction
- ↓ Prostacyclin (PGI₂) → ↓ cAMP → vasoconstriction
- ↑ Endothelin-1 (ET-1) → potent vasoconstriction + smooth muscle proliferation
- ↑ Thromboxane A₂: vasoconstriction
- Result: sustained high PVR despite O₂ exposure and lung inflation

**Shunting Physiology:**
- PVR > SVR → right-to-left shunting at PFO (desaturated blood enters LA) and PDA (desaturated blood enters aorta distal to right subclavian)
- Pre-ductal SpO₂ (right hand) higher than post-ductal SpO₂ (feet): >10% difference = significant ductal-level shunt
- At foramen ovale: right-to-left shunt → mixing at atrial level → uniform desaturation pre and post-ductal

---

## 4. Function (Normal PVR Physiology)

- Fetal PVR: intentionally high (hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction + compressed unexpanded lungs + low NO/PGI₂)
- Birth triggers PVR fall: O₂ → ↑ eNOS → ↑ NO → cGMP → SMC relaxation (primary)
- Prostacyclin, physical lung expansion, loss of hypoxic environment all contribute
- Normal postnatal PVR: falls to ~10–15% of SVR within hours; continues to fall over weeks
- PVR remodeling over first weeks of life: smooth muscle involutes, vessels dilate

---

## 5. Dysfunction / Pathophysiology

**Triggers for PPHN:**
- **Hypoxia/asphyxia**: most potent pulmonary vasoconstrictor; perpetuates cycle
- **Acidosis**: vasoconstriction; target pH >7.35 in PPHN management
- **Hypothermia**: vasoconstriction; maintain normothermia strictly
- **Meconium aspiration**: chemical pneumonitis → hypoxia + vasoconstriction + surfactant inactivation
- **Sepsis (GBS, E. coli)**: endotoxin and cytokines → pulmonary vasoconstriction
- **CDH**: pulmonary hypoplasia + abnormal vascular remodeling → fixed + reactive PH
- **NSAID use (indomethacin, NSAIDs) in pregnancy**: premature ductal constriction → pulmonary overcirculation → vascular remodeling → PPHN at birth
- **Polycythemia**: hyperviscosity → ↑ PVR

**Self-perpetuating cycle:**
- Elevated PVR → right-to-left shunt → hypoxemia → hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction → ↑ PVR

**BPD-associated PH:**
- Chronic alveolar-vascular mismatch
- Inflammatory mediators → smooth muscle hypertrophy
- Hypoxia (chronic low SpO₂) → chronic HPV
- Occurs in ~20–25% of severe BPD; significantly worsens prognosis

---

## 6. Clinical Phenotype

- **Severe hypoxemia disproportionate to CXR/lung disease** — classic presentation
- **Pre/post-ductal SpO₂ gradient**: right hand (pre-ductal) SpO₂ >10% higher than feet (post-ductal) → ductus-level right-to-left shunt
- **Labile oxygenation**: SpO₂ oscillates dramatically with stimulation, suctioning, crying — reflects reactive vascular tone
- **Minimal response to O₂ alone**: PaO₂ may improve only modestly with FiO₂ 1.0 if shunt is large
- **Echocardiogram findings**:
  - Flattened/D-shaped septum (RV pressure ≥ LV pressure)
  - RV dilation, tricuspid regurgitation (allows RV pressure estimate)
  - Right-to-left or bidirectional flow at ductus/PFO
- **Hyperoxia test**: PaO₂ >200 on FiO₂ 1.0 makes cyanotic CHD less likely (not PPHN diagnosis)

---

## 7. Differential / Distinctions

| Diagnosis | Key Feature | Distinguishing Test |
|---|---|---|
| PPHN (failed transition) | Term, acute, reactive | Echo: no structural defect; vasodilator response |
| PPHN (CDH) | Prenatal diagnosis, scaphoid abdomen | CXR, echo: bowel in chest |
| Cyanotic CHD | Fixed; no pre/post gradient with PFO | Echo: structural defect |
| RDS alone | Preterm, ground-glass; responsive to O₂ | Echo: no elevated PVR |
| ACD | Refractory PPHN, term, no vasodilator response | Lung biopsy, FOXF1 gene |
| Sepsis-associated | Fever, positive culture, pneumonia | Blood culture, CBC |

- PPHN vs cyanotic CHD: hyperoxia test + echo critical; do NOT delay echo
- BPD-PH vs acute PPHN: BPD-PH occurs at weeks-months of age; chronic course; different management

---

## 8. Diagnosis

- **Echocardiogram**: essential; defines anatomy, estimates RV/PA pressure (TR jet), assesses shunt direction, evaluates RV function
- **Pre/post-ductal SpO₂ monitoring**: bilateral pulse oximetry (right hand + foot) simultaneously; >10% difference = ductal shunt
- **ABG**: severity of hypoxemia; calculate oxygenation index (OI) = (MAP × FiO₂ × 100) / PaO₂
  - OI >25: severe; consider iNO
  - OI >40: ECMO criteria
- **CXR**: assess lung disease, cardiac silhouette, cardiomegaly
- **BNP/NT-proBNP**: elevated in PH; useful in BPD-PH monitoring
- **Cardiac catheterization**: rarely needed acutely; may be done for chronic BPD-PH refractory cases

---

## 9. Management

**Supportive Care (avoid all vasoconstrictive triggers):**
- **Normothermia**: hypothermia is a pulmonary vasoconstrictor
- **Normoglycemia/normocalcemia**: metabolic disturbances worsen PH
- **Minimize stimulation**: handle gently; pre-medicate before suctioning
- **Adequate sedation/analgesia**: pain/agitation → catecholamine surge → vasoconstriction
- **Target pH 7.35–7.45**: avoid acidosis (vasoconstrictor); avoid aggressive alkalosis (reduces cerebral blood flow, associated with hearing loss)
- **Avoid hyperoxia**: target SpO₂ 91–95%; hyperoxia causes oxidative injury without additional vasodilation benefit

**Ventilation:**
- Optimize lung volumes: both atelectasis and overdistension increase PVR
- Mean airway pressure high enough to recruit atelectasis, low enough to avoid overdistension
- HFOV: useful when parenchymal disease present; allows independent MAP optimization
- Permissive hypercapnia: NOT in acute PPHN (hypercapnia → acidosis → vasoconstriction); target normal PaCO₂

**Pulmonary Vasodilators:**
- **iNO (inhaled nitric oxide)**: 20 ppm; first-line; selective pulmonary vasodilator; → ↑ cGMP → SMC relaxation
  - 60–70% response rate in term PPHN
  - No proven benefit in preterm; may increase IVH risk
  - Must wean slowly (rebound PH on abrupt discontinuation)
- **Sildenafil**: PDE5 inhibitor → prevents cGMP breakdown → sustained NO effect; oral/IV
- **Milrinone**: PDE3 inhibitor → ↑ cAMP; pulmonary vasodilation + positive inotropic effect on RV
- **Prostaglandins (PGI₂, epoprostenol, treprostinil)**: for refractory or chronic BPD-PH
- **Bosentan**: endothelin receptor antagonist; used in BPD-PH; limited neonatal data

---

## 10. Therapeutics

**iNO Protocol:**
- Start at 20 ppm (higher doses not more effective; higher adverse effect risk)
- Wean by 5 ppm q4–6h if stable; wean to 5 ppm before stopping (prevent rebound)
- Monitor methemoglobin (rare but possible)
- Monitor NO₂ levels in circuit

**ECMO (Extracorporeal Membrane Oxygenation):**
- Criteria: OI >40 on two ABGs 30 min apart (or >25 persistent despite maximal therapy)
- Provides oxygenation while lung/vascular recovery occurs
- VA-ECMO (venoarterial): supports both cardiac and respiratory function; preferred in RV failure
- VV-ECMO (venovenous): respiratory support only; no cardiac support
- ECMO not indicated in irreversible conditions: ACD, lethal chromosomal anomalies, severe IVH
- CDH: ECMO used as bridge; does not improve survival over optimal medical management in most studies

---

## 11. Complications / Outcomes

- **Acute PPHN (idiopathic/reactive)**: mortality 10–20%; responds to iNO in majority
- **Neurosensory hearing loss**: associated with PPHN (hypoxia), iNO (rebound), and therapeutic hypothermia
- **Neurodevelopmental impairment**: 25–50% of PPHN survivors have some neurodevelopmental issue
- **CDH-associated PH**: mortality 30–50%; pulmonary hypertension is primary determinant of mortality
- **BPD-PH**: significantly worsens prognosis; 2-year mortality up to 30–40% in severe BPD with PH
- **ACD**: nearly universally fatal within days to weeks

---

## 12. Associations / Genetics / Maternal-Fetal Links

- **Maternal NSAID/aspirin use late pregnancy**: premature ductal constriction → pulmonary overcirculation → remodeling → PPHN
- **Maternal SSRI use**: controversial; some data suggest ↑ PPHN risk (particularly late pregnancy)
- **CDH**: see [[Congenital Diaphragmatic Hernia]]; associated FOXF1 pathway disruption
- **ACD**: FOXF1 (16q24.1) mutation/deletion → see [[Alveolar Capillary Dysplasia]]
- **Trisomy 21**: nearly 40% develop PH; particularly with untreated ASD/VSD; subglottic stenosis worsens
- **BMPR2 mutations**: adult PAH gene; rare pediatric/neonatal contribution

---

## 13. Context / Board Framing

- **"Mechanism of iNO?"** → Inhaled → absorbed by pulmonary endothelium → activates soluble guanylate cyclase → ↑ cGMP → smooth muscle relaxation → selective pulmonary vasodilation (inactivated by Hgb before reaching systemic circulation)
- **"OI formula?"** → OI = (MAP × FiO₂ × 100) / PaO₂; OI >40 = ECMO criteria
- **"Pre/post-ductal gradient means?"** → Ductal-level right-to-left shunt; right hand pre-ductal (higher), feet post-ductal (lower)
- **"Why NOT hyperventilate to alkalosis?"** → Causes cerebral vasoconstriction, sensorineural hearing loss, no proven benefit; target normal pH
- **"Why does CDH have PH that doesn't respond well to iNO?"** → Combination of vascular underdevelopment (structural, fixed) + maladaptive remodeling; not purely vasoreactive
- **"BPD-PH treatment?"** → Optimize O₂ (SpO₂ 92–95%), sildenafil, bosentan; diuretics for volume management
- **"PPHN in term infant not responding to anything?"** → Think ACD ([[Alveolar Capillary Dysplasia]])

---

## 14. Edge Pearl

- **iNO rebound phenomenon**: when iNO is weaned too rapidly, the pulmonary vasculature — having been dependent on exogenous NO — can acutely vasoconstrict as endogenous NO production has been suppressed. This causes a dangerous rebound pulmonary hypertensive crisis. The mechanism: exogenous NO suppresses endogenous eNOS expression via negative feedback. Rapid iNO withdrawal → sudden loss of NO → acute vasoconstriction. Management: wean slowly (5 ppm decrements), have emergency 20 ppm available to restart if deterioration occurs. This is also why sildenafil is sometimes used as a bridge during iNO weaning in severe cases.
