## 1. Identity

- **Non-invasive ventilation (NIV)** provides respiratory support via nasal interface without an endotracheal tube
- Modalities: **CPAP**, **NIPPV** (nasal intermittent positive pressure ventilation), **HFNC** (high-flow nasal cannula)
- First-line respiratory support for preterm infants — SUPPORT trial changed practice from intubation-first to CPAP-first
- Core principle: maintain FRC, prevent alveolar collapse, reduce work of breathing without intubation

---

## 2. Taxonomy / Developmental Placement

- **Gestational axis**: All GAs; most impactful in preterm (VLBW, ELBW)
- **Phase axis**: Postnatal — from delivery room through NICU course
- Developmental ring: **Taxonomy** — classification of support modalities matters for management decisions

**Modality Hierarchy:**
| Mode | Mechanism | Typical Settings | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CPAP** | Continuous distending pressure | 5–8 cmH₂O | RDS, post-extubation, apnea |
| **NIPPV** | CPAP + intermittent breaths | PIP 15–25, rate 20–40 | Post-extubation failure, apnea |
| **HFNC** | Warmed humidified high-flow | 2–8 LPM | Weaning from CPAP, mild support |

---

## 3. Structure

**CPAP Mechanics:**
- Constant positive airway pressure applied via nasal prongs, mask, or pharyngeal tube
- Maintains FRC by preventing end-expiratory alveolar collapse
- **Stents airways open** → reduces obstructive apnea
- Splints chest wall (preterm has extremely compliant chest wall → paradoxical breathing)
- Reduces work of breathing by 30–40% (supports diaphragm)

**NIPPV:**
- CPAP + superimposed intermittent positive pressure breaths
- **Synchronized** (sNIPPV): breaths triggered by infant effort — may be superior
- **Non-synchronized**: time-cycled mandatory breaths — easier to implement
- Provides augmented tidal volume during backup breaths

**HFNC:**
- Heated, humidified air/O₂ at 2–8 LPM through nasal cannula
- Generates variable, unmeasured positive pressure (depends on flow, leak, mouth opening)
- Better tolerated (less nasal trauma) but less reliable pressure delivery than CPAP

---

## 4. Function

- **CPAP maintains FRC**: prevents atelectasis between breaths → improves V/Q matching → reduces O₂ requirement
- **Reduces intubation need**: SUPPORT trial showed early CPAP = intubation + surfactant for composite death/BPD at 36 weeks
- **Facilitates surfactant clearance**: CPAP + LISA protocol = deliver surfactant via thin catheter while infant breathes on CPAP
- **NIPPV reduces post-extubation failure**: ~30% reduction vs CPAP alone in some trials
- **HFNC**: adequate for mild-moderate support; inferior to CPAP for acute RDS

---

## 5. Dysfunction / Pathophysiology

**When NIV fails:**
- Severe surfactant deficiency (FiO₂ >0.40 on CPAP → surfactant indicated)
- Severe apnea unresponsive to caffeine + CPAP → reintubation
- Air leak on CPAP (pneumothorax risk real — high CPAP + surfactant-deficient lung)
- Hemodynamic instability: CPAP increases intrathoracic pressure → may reduce preload in hypovolemic infants
- Nasal anatomy: choanal atresia, severe micrognathia → CPAP fails mechanically

---

## 6. Clinical Phenotype

**Infant on effective NIV:**
- Comfortable, synchronous breathing
- FiO₂ <0.30–0.40
- Adequate chest rise, no severe retractions
- Stable SpO₂, minimal apnea/brady episodes

**NIV failure:**
- Escalating FiO₂ >0.40 (surfactant threshold)
- Severe retractions, nasal flaring, grunting despite CPAP
- Recurrent apnea/bradycardia despite caffeine
- Hypercarbia (PaCO₂ >60–65 mmHg)

---

## 7. Differential / Distinctions

| Feature | CPAP | NIPPV | HFNC | Mechanical Ventilation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Invasive? | No | No | No | Yes (ETT) |
| Pressure delivery | Reliable | Reliable | Variable | Reliable |
| Tidal volume | Patient-generated | Patient + machine | Patient-generated | Machine-controlled |
| Best evidence | SUPPORT trial | Post-extubation | Weaning | Severe respiratory failure |
| Nasal trauma | Higher | Higher | Lower | N/A |
| BPD reduction | Yes | Likely | Less clear | Baseline risk |

- **CPAP vs HFNC**: CPAP more effective for acute RDS (delivers reliable pressure); HFNC better tolerated (less nasal injury), suitable for weaning
- **CPAP vs NIPPV**: NIPPV may reduce reintubation post-extubation; not clearly superior as primary mode

---

## 8. Diagnosis

- NIV is a therapeutic modality, not a diagnosis
- **Indications for CPAP**: RDS, post-extubation, apnea of prematurity, TTN
- **Indications for intubation instead**: severe RDS with FiO₂ >0.40, refractory apnea, hemodynamic instability, airway anomaly

---

## 9. Management

**Delivery Room:**
- CPAP from birth for VLBW/ELBW infants (SUPPORT trial approach)
- Start at 5–7 cmH₂O
- Give surfactant via LISA if FiO₂ >0.30 (threshold varies by unit)

**NICU Course:**
- **CPAP 5–8 cmH₂O** as first-line for RDS, post-extubation
- Escalate to NIPPV if CPAP insufficient (recurrent apnea, post-extubation failure)
- Wean to HFNC when stable on low CPAP settings
- **LISA protocol**: thin catheter surfactant while on CPAP → avoids intubation entirely

**Nasal Care:**
- Nasal septal trauma is the **main complication** of prolonged nasal CPAP/NIPPV
- Alternate prong sizes, use barrier dressings, adjust headgear frequently
- Severe nasal injury can cause permanent septal erosion

---

## 10. Therapeutics

**SUPPORT Trial (2010):**
- VLBW infants randomized to early CPAP vs intubation + surfactant
- Primary outcome: death or BPD at 36 weeks PMA — **no difference** (equivalent)
- CPAP group: less intubation, less mechanical ventilation, fewer ventilator days
- Changed practice: CPAP-first is now standard for VLBW infants

**LISA/MIST:**
- Less Invasive Surfactant Administration / Minimally Invasive Surfactant Therapy
- Thin catheter (5 Fr) inserted under direct laryngoscopy while infant breathes on CPAP
- Surfactant instilled slowly; no intubation; no PPV
- Growing evidence: reduces BPD and severe IVH compared to INSURE
- Preferred where expertise available

---

## 11. Complications / Outcomes

- **Nasal septal trauma**: most common complication of prolonged CPAP/NIPPV
- **Pneumothorax**: real risk on CPAP (especially with surfactant-deficient lung)
- **Gastric distension**: swallowed air; managed with OG tube
- **Abdominal distension**: may mimic NEC; decompress with OG
- **BPD reduction**: NIV-first strategies reduce BPD compared to routine intubation

---

## 12. Associations / Genetics / Maternal-Fetal Links

- **Antenatal steroids + CPAP**: synergistic — steroids prime surfactant + ENaC; CPAP maintains FRC → together reduce intubation need
- **Caffeine + CPAP**: caffeine improves respiratory drive; CPAP maintains airway → together reduce apnea + BPD
- No genetic associations — NIV is a treatment modality

---

## 13. Context / Board Framing

- **"SUPPORT trial?"** → Early CPAP = intubation + surfactant for death/BPD in VLBW — CPAP-first is standard
- **"CPAP vs HFNC?"** → CPAP more effective for RDS; HFNC better tolerated, good for weaning
- **"LISA protocol?"** → Thin catheter surfactant on CPAP; no intubation; reduces BPD/IVH vs INSURE
- **"Main complication of nasal CPAP?"** → Nasal septal trauma
- **"Typical CPAP pressure?"** → 5–8 cmH₂O
- **"When to intubate instead?"** → FiO₂ >0.40, refractory apnea, hemodynamic instability

---

## 14. Edge Pearl

- **LISA is the procedural evolution**: the traditional INSURE (intubate-surfactant-extubate) required brief intubation, which introduces airway trauma and may trigger inflammatory cascades. LISA (thin catheter while breathing on CPAP) avoids intubation entirely. The key skill is **visualizing the cords without intubating** — a subtle but important procedural evolution. Meta-analyses show LISA reduces BPD and severe IVH compared to INSURE. In units without LISA expertise, INSURE remains excellent. The board question tests whether you know LISA exists and why it may be superior.
