## 1. Identity

- **Congenital Diaphragmatic Hernia (CDH)** is a structural defect in the diaphragm allowing herniation of abdominal viscera into the thoracic cavity during fetal development, resulting in pulmonary hypoplasia and abnormal pulmonary vascular remodeling
- Incidence: ~1 in 2,500 live births
- Two main types: **Bochdalek** (postero-lateral, 85% left-sided, 80% of all CDH) and **Morgagni** (anterior, retrosternal, ~2%, benign)
- Primary determinants of mortality: severity of pulmonary hypoplasia and severity of pulmonary hypertension — not the hernia itself
- Survival: 60–80% overall in tertiary centers; <60% in severe cases (liver "up," low LHR)

---

## 2. Taxonomy / Developmental Placement

- **Gestational axis**: Fetal — defect occurs at 8–10 weeks gestation
- **Phase axis**: Fetal (lung hypoplasia develops throughout gestation) + Postnatal (clinical manifestation)
- Developmental ring: **Identity** — a discrete structural anomaly with profound developmental consequences
- CDH is not primarily a lung disease — it is a **space-occupying defect** that disrupts [[Lung Development]] during the critical pseudoglandular and canalicular stages

**Embryology:**
- Diaphragm forms from 4 components: septum transversum (anterior), pleuroperitoneal membranes (posterolateral), esophageal mesentery (posterior), body wall muscles (lateral)
- **Bochdalek defect**: failure of the pleuroperitoneal membrane to close at 8–10 weeks → left-sided posterolateral defect (right side protected by liver)
- Herniation occurs during the pseudoglandular stage (7–16 weeks) when airways are forming → compresses ipsilateral and (via mediastinal shift) contralateral lung
- Result: bilateral pulmonary hypoplasia (ipsilateral > contralateral) + abnormal pulmonary arterial remodeling (thick medial layer, abnormal branching)

---

## 3. Structure

**Diaphragmatic Defect:**
- Left Bochdalek (80%): stomach, small bowel, colon, spleen, and sometimes liver herniate into left hemithorax
- Right Bochdalek (15%): liver ± bowel herniates into right hemithorax; often more severe (liver herniation)
- Morgagni (2%): parasternal, anterior; usually presents incidentally; minimal cardiorespiratory compromise

**Pulmonary Consequences:**
- **Ipsilateral lung**: severe hypoplasia; reduced airway generations, fewer alveoli, abnormal surfactant (may have surfactant dysfunction)
- **Contralateral lung**: less severe hypoplasia from mediastinal shift pressure; better functional reserve
- **Pulmonary vasculature (both lungs)**: medial smooth muscle hypertrophy in small arteries → fixed + reactive PH; abnormal arterial branching pattern; reduced total cross-sectional vascular area

---

## 4. Function

- After birth: hernia contents fill chest → prevents lung inflation and gas exchange on ipsilateral side
- PVR extremely elevated: combination of anatomically reduced vascular bed + reactive vasoconstriction → persistent massive PH
- Limited pulmonary compliance: hypoplastic lungs are stiff, require high pressures to ventilate → overdistension risk of contralateral lung
- Cardiac function: mediastinal shift → RV afterload ↑, LV compression → biventricular dysfunction possible
- Scaphoid abdomen: bowel absent from abdomen → abdominal cavity underdeveloped

---

## 5. Dysfunction / Pathophysiology

**Two components that determine severity:**

**1. Pulmonary hypoplasia (structural):**
- Reduced total lung volume (airway generations, alveolar count)
- Insufficient gas-exchange surface area for survival
- Worsened by liver herniation into chest (reduces space for lung growth)
- Not reversible after birth — surgical repair removes hernia but does not create new lung tissue

**2. Pulmonary hypertension (vascular):**
- Abnormal remodeling → media hypertrophy → high resting PVR
- Reactive component: additional vasoconstriction triggered by hypoxia, acidosis, agitation
- PVR may be very responsive to vasodilators OR poorly responsive (fixed component dominant)
- iNO efficacy: variable; less responsive than idiopathic PPHN; may improve reactive component

**CDH-associated PPHN:**
- Combination of structural (fixed) + reactive PH
- Explains why iNO does not uniformly work in CDH as it does in idiopathic PPHN
- Vasodilators helpful but not curative; surgical repair reduces herniated mass effect but PH persists

---

## 6. Clinical Phenotype

**Prenatal:**
- Most diagnosed on routine fetal ultrasound (18–20 week anatomy scan)
- Bowel/stomach in chest, mediastinal shift, absent stomach in abdomen
- Polyhydramnios (bowel obstruction)

**Postnatal (delivery room):**
- **Respiratory distress from birth**: hypoxia, hypercarbia, poor air entry
- **Scaphoid abdomen**: bowel absent → flat/sunken abdomen (classic)
- **Barrel chest** or asymmetric chest expansion: bowel in thorax
- **Decreased breath sounds** on affected side; bowel sounds sometimes heard in chest
- Heart sounds displaced to right (with left-sided CDH)
- Rapid deterioration if bag-mask ventilation used (inflates bowel in chest → worsens compression)

**Severity Predictors:**
- **o/e LHR (observed/expected lung-to-head ratio)** by fetal ultrasound: 
  - <25%: severe; high mortality
  - 25–45%: moderate
  - >45%: good prognosis
- **Liver herniation into chest** ("liver up"): worsens prognosis; reduces available space for lung growth
- **Fetal MRI total lung volume**: more accurate than LHR
- **Total fetal lung volume (TFLV)**: standard metric; correlates with postnatal course

---

## 7. Differential / Distinctions

| Diagnosis | Key Feature | Distinguisher |
|---|---|---|
| CDH (Bochdalek) | Bowel in chest, posterolateral defect | CXR, scaphoid abdomen, prenatal US |
| Morgagni hernia | Anterior defect, rare, often incidental | Location on CXR/CT |
| CCAM/CPAM | Cystic lung mass | No bowel herniation; lung mass not bowel |
| Bronchogenic cyst | Mediastinal or lung | No diaphragmatic defect |
| PPHN (idiopathic) | No structural defect, reactive | Echo: no bowel in chest |
| Esophageal atresia/TEF | Esophageal anomaly | Coiling of NG tube in esophagus |

---

## 8. Diagnosis

**Prenatal:**
- **Obstetric ultrasound**: visualization of bowel/stomach in thorax, polyhydramnios, mediastinal shift
- **Fetal MRI**: more precise lung volumetry; liver position assessment; gold standard for severity prediction
- **o/e LHR** and **TFLV%**: risk stratification for counseling and FETO candidacy
- **Genetic testing**: chromosomal microarray; CDH associated with Trisomy 18, 21, Pallister-Killian (tetrasomy 12p), CHARGE syndrome, Cornelia de Lange

**Postnatal:**
- **CXR**: bowel loops in chest, mediastinal shift, no normal gastric bubble in abdomen, ETT placement confirmation
- **NG tube**: if coils in chest → confirms stomach herniation; also decompresses bowel (reduces chest compression)
- **Echocardiogram**: essential; estimates RV/PA pressure, assesses cardiac function, evaluates for structural CHD (30% have associated cardiac defects), guides PH management
- **Arterial blood gas**: severity of oxygenation failure; pre/post-ductal SpO₂

---

## 9. Management

**Delivery Room:**
- **Immediate intubation**: do NOT mask ventilate (inflates bowel → worsens chest compression)
- Insert **NG/OG tube immediately**: deflate bowel to reduce intrathoracic volume
- Avoid aggressive resuscitation with high pressures; contralateral lung protection is paramount

**Ventilation Strategy — "Gentle Ventilation":**
- **Permissive hypercapnia**: target PaCO₂ 45–65 mmHg; accept pH ≥7.25
- **Low PIP**: PIP ≤25 cmH₂O; avoid barotrauma to hypoplastic lungs
- **Low PEEP**: 3–5 cmH₂O; higher PEEP risks overdistension
- Conventional ventilation preferred for many centers; HFOV for some (debate exists)
- **Avoid hyperoxia**: FiO₂ titrated to pre-ductal SpO₂ 85–95%
- Target: pre-ductal SpO₂ >85%, post-ductal >70%; accept lower targets to avoid volutrauma

**Pulmonary Hypertension Management:**
- **iNO**: FDA-approved for ≥34 weeks; improves oxygenation in ~50% of CDH (less than idiopathic PPHN); reduces ECMO need modestly
- **Sildenafil**: adjunct; oral
- **Milrinone**: for biventricular dysfunction + PH; inotropic + vasodilatory
- Avoid aggressive alkalosis; avoid hyperoxia

**ECMO (Extracorporeal Membrane Oxygenation):**
- Criteria: OI >40 on two ABGs, refractory hypoxemia, failure of medical management
- VA-ECMO (preferred in CDH): supports cardiac + respiratory function
- Provides time for lung maturation and PH regression before surgical repair
- Overall ECMO use in CDH: ~20–30% of cases in specialized centers
- Note: ECMO in CDH improves oxygenation but does not improve overall survival compared to optimal medical management in some studies

**Surgical Repair:**
- Timing: **delayed repair** is standard of care — repair only when hemodynamically stable (PH managed, off ECMO or stable on ECMO)
- Immediate repair in delivery room: obsolete; worsened outcomes
- Approach: primary closure (small defects) or patch repair (Dacron, Gore-Tex, or biological patches for large defects)
- Laparoscopic repair: feasible in selected hemodynamically stable infants
- Post-repair: continued PH management; lung growth does not occur rapidly after repair

---

## 10. Therapeutics

**FETO (Fetal Endoscopic Tracheal Occlusion):**
- Experimental; for severe left CDH with o/e LHR <25% and liver up
- Balloon occluded in trachea at 26–30 weeks → retains [[Fetal Lung Fluid]] → ↑ lung growth stimulus
- Balloon removed at 34 weeks (allows surfactant accumulation)
- TOTAL trial: FETO improved survival in severe CDH; modest benefit; significant risk (preterm labor)
- Not standard of care; performed in select fetal surgery centers

**Post-operative management:**
- Continue PH therapy; wean iNO slowly
- Nutritional support: high caloric needs; often require NG/nasojejunal feeds
- GERD: nearly universal; proton pump inhibitors; may require fundoplication
- Hearing screening: iNO associated with sensorineural hearing loss

---

## 11. Complications / Outcomes

**Acute:**
- Respiratory failure despite repair: lung growth is fixed; repair removes mass effect only
- Post-repair rebound PH: common in first 24–48h; anticipate and treat
- Chylothorax: lymphatic injury during repair; treat with MCT formula, octreotide, or thoracic duct ligation

**Chronic:**
- **BPD phenotype**: 40–60% of CDH survivors have significant chronic lung disease
- **Pulmonary hypertension**: may persist months to years; requires long-term cardiac follow-up
- **Recurrent hernia**: 5–30% depending on patch size and repair type
- **GERD/feeding difficulties**: nearly universal; esophageal dysmotility
- **Scoliosis**: spinal deformities from asymmetric thoracic development
- **Neurodevelopmental delay**: 25–50%; multifactorial (hypoxia, ECMO, NICU exposure)
- **Hearing loss**: ECMO and iNO both risk factors

**Mortality:**
- Overall: 30–40% (including prenatally diagnosed)
- Severe CDH (liver up, low LHR): >50% mortality
- Morgagni hernia: near-zero mortality if isolated

---

## 12. Associations / Genetics / Maternal-Fetal Links

- **Chromosomal anomalies** (~30% of CDH):
  - Trisomy 18 (most common): lethal; CDH is one of many anomalies
  - Trisomy 21: CDH rare but reported
  - **Pallister-Killian syndrome** (tetrasomy 12p): CDH + dysmorphic features; mosaic
  - **CHARGE syndrome**: CDH, coloboma, heart defects, choanal atresia, growth retardation, ear anomalies
  - **Cornelia de Lange**: CDH variant
- **Isolated CDH genetic causes**:
  - FOXF1 mutations (also causes ACD): transcription factor for lung and gut development
  - GATA4, WT1, NR2F2 (COUP-TFII) variants
- **Maternal factors**: some maternal nutritional deficiencies (vitamin A, retinol pathway) may increase CDH risk; teratogen exposure

**Associated anomalies (30–50% of CDH):**
- Congenital heart disease: most common associated anomaly (30%); VSD, ASD, HLHS
- Malrotation of gut (universal — bowel herniates before rotation completes)
- Splenomegaly/splenic abnormalities
- Vertebral/rib anomalies

---

## 13. Context / Board Framing

- **"Side of Bochdalek?"** → 85% LEFT (right side protected by liver); posterolateral defect; pleuroperitoneal fold failure at 8–10 weeks
- **"Why no bag-mask ventilation in delivery room?"** → Inflates herniated bowel in chest → worsens lung compression
- **"Gentle ventilation means?"** → Permissive hypercapnia (PaCO₂ 45–65), low PIP (≤25), low PEEP (3–5), accept pre-ductal SpO₂ >85%
- **"Why does iNO work less in CDH than idiopathic PPHN?"** → CDH has both fixed (structural) and reactive PH; reactive component may respond; fixed component does not
- **"Repair timing?"** → Delayed (stabilize first); repair immediately after birth obsolete
- **"o/e LHR?"** → Observed/expected lung-to-head ratio; <25% = severe; predicts mortality
- **"FETO?"** → Fetal tracheal occlusion; retains FLF; stimulates lung growth; for severe CDH at 26–30 weeks; remove balloon at 34 weeks
- **"Most common associated anomaly?"** → Congenital heart disease (~30%)

---

## 14. Edge Pearl

- **CDH mortality is a PH story, not a hernia story**: a critical conceptual point for boards and clinical practice. The bowel in the chest is not what kills CDH infants — many can be surgically repaired successfully. What kills them is the **permanently hypoplastic and vascular-remodeled lungs** that cannot sustain gas exchange at birth. This is why "gentle ventilation" is so critical: every pneumothorax in the contralateral lung (the only functioning lung) is potentially fatal, and every episode of hypoxia drives the already-reactive pulmonary vasculature into further vasoconstriction. The therapeutic goal before repair is not to fix the hernia — it is to **tame the pulmonary hypertension** and maintain oxygenation with the lung tissue available.
