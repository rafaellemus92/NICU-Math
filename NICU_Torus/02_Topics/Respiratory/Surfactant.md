## 1. Identity

- **Surfactant** is a complex lipoprotein mixture synthesized and secreted by type II alveolar pneumocytes that reduces alveolar surface tension, preventing end-expiratory collapse
- Deficiency is the primary cause of [[RDS]] in preterm infants
- Exogenous surfactant replacement is one of the most impactful interventions in neonatal medicine
- Surfactant proteins are critical for function; genetic deficiency of SP-B is uniformly lethal

---

## 2. Taxonomy / Developmental Placement

- Type II pneumocytes differentiate in the **canalicular stage** (~22–24 weeks GA)
- Lamellar bodies (storage organelles) appear at ~22–24 weeks; secretion ramps up through the **saccular stage**
- Mature surfactant function established ~35–36 weeks in most infants
- Developmental ring: **Identity** — the functional molecule defining alveolar stability
- Gestational axis: fetal origin, critical at transitional and postnatal phases

---

## 3. Structure

**Composition (~100% of surfactant by weight):**
- **Phospholipids (80–85%)**: primarily dipalmitoylphosphatidylcholine (**DPPC**) — the main surface-tension-reducing component
  - Phosphatidylglycerol (PG): second most important; its presence in amniotic fluid = lung maturity marker
  - Phosphatidylethanolamine, phosphatidylinositol: minor components
- **Neutral lipids (5–10%)**: cholesterol, triglycerides
- **Proteins (5–10%)**:
  - **SP-A**: largest, most abundant; innate immunity, regulator of surfactant metabolism, promotes tubular myelin formation
  - **SP-B**: small, hydrophobic; essential for lipid packaging and surface film formation; **absence is lethal**
  - **SP-C**: small, hydrophobic; stabilizes surfactant film during compression; SP-C mutations → chronic ILD
  - **SP-D**: collectin; innate host defense; regulates surfactant pool size

**Intracellular pathway:**
- Synthesized in ER of type II pneumocytes → packaged into **lamellar bodies** → secreted by exocytosis → forms **tubular myelin** (intermediate form) → adsorbs to air-liquid interface as surface film

---

## 4. Function

- **Reduces alveolar surface tension** at end-expiration → prevents alveolar collapse
- **LaPlace Law**: P = 2T/r — in small alveoli (small r), pressure required to maintain patency is high; surfactant lowers T (surface tension), reducing required distending pressure
- **Prevents atelectasis**: maintains FRC between breaths
- **Stabilizes alveoli of different sizes**: prevents large alveoli from overinflating at expense of small ones (anti-LaPlace instability effect)
- **Innate immunity** (SP-A, SP-D): opsonization, pathogen binding, macrophage activation
- **Regulation of fluid balance**: limits transudation of fluid into alveolar space

---

## 5. Dysfunction / Pathophysiology

**Deficiency states:**
- **Prematurity**: insufficient type II cell maturation → low phospholipid output; principal cause of [[RDS]]
- **SP-B deficiency** (SFTPB gene mutation): complete absence → lethal neonatal respiratory failure; no exogenous surfactant is effective long-term; only cure = lung transplant
- **SP-C deficiency** (SFTPC gene mutation): variable phenotype; chronic ILD in infants/children; less severe than SP-B
- **ABCA3 deficiency**: transporter required for lamellar body formation; presents as neonatal respiratory failure; lethal or chronic ILD
- **Surfactant inactivation**: meconium, blood, edema proteins inactivate surfactant → secondary surfactant dysfunction (MAS, ARDS)

**Consequences of deficiency:**
- Diffuse atelectasis → reduced compliance → increased work of breathing → hypoxia, hypercarbia
- Protein-rich exudate leaks into alveoli → hyaline membranes → further inactivation
- V/Q mismatch → intrapulmonary shunting

---

## 6. Clinical Phenotype

- See [[RDS]] for full clinical phenotype
- **SP-B deficiency**: term infant with severe, refractory RDS; does not respond to surfactant
- **ABCA3 deficiency**: similar to SP-B; term or near-term; severe respiratory failure
- **SP-C mutations**: may present in neonatal period with mild-to-moderate respiratory distress, or not until childhood with chronic ILD (variable penetrance)
- Secondary surfactant dysfunction: any critically ill neonate with diffuse alveolar disease

---

## 7. Differential / Distinctions

| Feature | SP-B Deficiency | SP-C Deficiency | ABCA3 Deficiency | RDS (preterm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GA | Term | Variable | Term/near-term | Preterm |
| Response to exogenous surfactant | Transient/none | Variable | Transient | Good |
| Prognosis | Lethal without transplant | Chronic ILD | Variable (lethal to chronic) | Excellent with treatment |
| Inheritance | AR | AD (de novo) | AR | N/A |

- SP-B vs SP-C: SP-B deficiency is uniformly lethal neonatally; SP-C is variable and may present later
- Animal-derived vs synthetic: animal-derived (poractant, beractant, calfactant) contain SP-B/C analogs; older synthetic (Exosurf) had none; newer synthetic (lucinactant, CHF5633) contain SP-B/C peptide analogs

---

## 8. Diagnosis

- **Clinical**: preterm infant with respiratory distress, ground-glass CXR, air bronchograms → RDS; response to surfactant confirms
- **Amniocentesis** (if needed prenatally): L/S ratio ≥2.0, presence of PG = mature
- **Genetic testing**: for SP-B, SP-C, ABCA3 mutations if term infant with refractory RDS or family history of neonatal respiratory death
- **Lung biopsy**: in refractory cases; shows characteristic histology (e.g., PAP-like for SP-B deficiency)
- **Lamellar body count** (LBC) in gastric aspirate or amniotic fluid: rapid bedside maturity assessment

---

## 9. Management

**Prevention (antenatal):**
- **Betamethasone** 12 mg IM × 2 doses 24h apart at 24–34+6 weeks: induces SP-A, SP-B, SP-C, phospholipid synthesis; also accelerates ENaC (fluid clearance)
- Reduces RDS incidence ~50%, severity substantially

**Exogenous surfactant therapy:**
- Indicated for: RDS (rescue), prophylaxis in ELBW (selective), meconium aspiration syndrome (adjunct)
- **Timing**: early rescue (within 2 hours of birth) superior to late rescue
- **Route**: intratracheal (via ETT or thin catheter)
- **INSURE** (Intubate-Surfactant-Extubate): intubate briefly for surfactant, immediately extubate to CPAP — reduces BPD vs prolonged intubation
- **LISA / MIST** (Less Invasive Surfactant Administration): thin catheter inserted into trachea while infant breathes spontaneously on CPAP; infant not intubated; growing evidence of reduced BPD, IVH vs INSURE

---

## 10. Therapeutics

**Animal-derived surfactants (contain SP-B/C analogs):**
- **Poractant alfa (Curosurf)**: porcine; high phospholipid concentration; 100–200 mg/kg dose; most widely used globally
- **Beractant (Survanta)**: bovine; 100 mg/kg dose; lipid-supplemented
- **Calfactant (Infasurf)**: bovine; 105 mg/kg; high SP-B content
- Animal-derived are superior to synthetic for speed of response

**Synthetic surfactants:**
- **Lucinactant (Surfaxin)**: contains KL4 peptide (SP-B analog); FDA approved; less widely used
- **CHF5633**: SP-B + SP-C peptide analogs; investigational

**Dosing (poractant as example):**
- First dose: **200 mg/kg** (rescue); subsequent doses: 100 mg/kg
- May repeat q6–12h × 2 additional doses if still intubated with ongoing RDS
- Prophylactic dosing (in delivery room for ELBW): 100–200 mg/kg; now less common with CPAP-first strategy

**CPAP-first strategy:**
- SUPPORT trial: CPAP from birth in VLBW infants → similar outcomes to intubation + surfactant; reduced BPD/death composite
- Current approach: CPAP at birth; surfactant if FiO₂ >0.30 (or per unit protocol)

---

## 11. Complications / Outcomes

- **Complications of surfactant administration**: pulmonary hemorrhage (acute, especially if PDA present), transient hypoxia during instillation, ETT obstruction
- **PDA**: surfactant → rapid ↓ in PVR → ↑ left-to-right PDA shunt → hemorrhagic pulmonary edema (rare but serious)
- **Outcomes with surfactant treatment**: dramatically reduces mortality from RDS; RDS mortality <5% in modern NICUs vs 50%+ pre-surfactant era
- Risk of [[BPD]] persists despite surfactant — BPD is now primarily a disease of alveolar disruption, not just surfactant deficiency

---

## 12. Associations / Genetics / Maternal-Fetal Links

- **Maternal diabetes**: hyperinsulinemia delays type II cell maturation → reduced surfactant synthesis even at term GA
- **Maternal corticosteroids**: upregulate SFTPB, SFTPC, ABCA3 gene expression in type II cells
- **SP-B deficiency (SFTPB)**: autosomal recessive; ~1:1,000,000; requires heterozygous carrier parents; prenatal diagnosis available
- **ABCA3 deficiency (ABCA3)**: autosomal recessive; more common than SP-B; variable severity by mutation type
- **SP-C (SFTPC)**: autosomal dominant, often de novo; variable penetrance
- **Trisomy 21**: reduced type II cell maturation delay; may have relative surfactant sufficiency but alveolar simplification

---

## 13. Context / Board Framing

- **"Composition of surfactant?"** → 80% phospholipids (DPPC is most important), 10% neutral lipids, 5–10% proteins (SP-A, -B, -C, -D)
- **"Most important phospholipid?"** → DPPC
- **"SP-B deficiency?"** → Lethal; term infant; refractory RDS; autosomal recessive; no exogenous surfactant works; lung transplant only option
- **"SP-C deficiency?"** → Variable; may present as chronic ILD in childhood; autosomal dominant
- **"LaPlace Law application?"** → P = 2T/r; surfactant lowers T, allowing small alveoli to remain open at lower distending pressures
- **"INSURE vs LISA?"** → INSURE = intubate-surfactant-extubate; LISA = thin catheter, no intubation, breathing on CPAP; LISA → less BPD/IVH (emerging evidence)
- **"CPAP first?"** → SUPPORT trial; CPAP at birth reduces need for surfactant in many VLBW infants

---

## 14. Edge Pearl

- **SP-B deficiency board trap**: a term infant (not preterm) presenting with severe, refractory RDS that does not respond to exogenous surfactant should prompt consideration of genetic surfactant deficiency. SP-B deficiency is the prototype: CXR shows diffuse ground-glass identical to RDS; surfactant gives transient improvement only. The clue is a **term or near-term** infant with **no risk factors** for standard RDS who fails multiple surfactant doses. Lung biopsy or genetic testing confirms. Only definitive treatment is **lung transplantation**.
