## 1. Identity

- **High-frequency ventilation (HFV)** delivers very small tidal volumes (less than anatomic dead space) at supraphysiologic rates (240–900 breaths/min)
- Two main types: **HFOV** (oscillator) and **HFJV** (jet ventilator)
- Rescue therapy for severe respiratory failure, air leaks, CDH
- Minimizes volutrauma by using tidal volumes smaller than dead space — gas exchange occurs via enhanced molecular diffusion

---

## 2. Taxonomy / Developmental Placement

- **Gestational axis**: All GAs — used in both preterm (RDS, PIE) and term (CDH, PPHN, MAS)
- **Phase axis**: Postnatal — rescue modality during respiratory failure
- Developmental ring: **Distinctions** — HFOV vs HFJV selection is a critical board and clinical distinction

**Two Main Types:**
| Feature | HFOV (Oscillator) | HFJV (Jet Ventilator) |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | Sinusoidal, piston-driven | Pulsed gas jets |
| Exhalation | **ACTIVE** (piston pulls back) | **PASSIVE** (elastic recoil) |
| Rate | 8–15 Hz (480–900/min) | 240–660/min (4–11 Hz) |
| Waveform | Symmetric sinusoidal | Asymmetric (short inspiration, long expiration) |
| PEEP | Set as MAP (continuous) | Uses conventional ventilator PEEP |
| Best for | Diffuse parenchymal disease | **Air leaks / PIE** |

---

## 3. Structure

**HFOV Mechanics:**
- Piston oscillates diaphragm → creates sinusoidal pressure wave
- Active inspiration AND active exhalation (pulls expired gas out)
- MAP (mean airway pressure) set directly → controls oxygenation
- Amplitude (ΔP) controls ventilation (CO₂ clearance)
- Frequency (Hz) inversely affects tidal volume

**HFJV Mechanics:**
- Short pulses of gas injected through a narrow cannula in the ETT
- Passive exhalation via elastic recoil of lung
- Works in tandem with conventional ventilator (provides PEEP and sigh breaths)
- Lower MAP achievable than HFOV → less gas trapping
- Passive exhalation = gas escapes more easily from air leaks → less air trapping → better for PIE/pneumothorax

---

## 4. Function

**Gas Exchange on HFV:**
- Tidal volume < anatomic dead space — so how does gas exchange occur?
- **Enhanced molecular diffusion**: Taylor dispersion, pendelluft, asymmetric velocity profiles, cardiogenic mixing, molecular diffusion all contribute
- Net result: CO₂ clearance occurs despite sub-dead-space volumes

**Controlling Parameters:**
| Parameter | Controls | How |
|---|---|---|
| **MAP (mean airway pressure)** | Oxygenation | Higher MAP → more alveolar recruitment → better V/Q matching |
| **FiO₂** | Oxygenation | Standard |
| **Amplitude (ΔP)** | Ventilation (CO₂) | Higher amplitude → more CO₂ cleared |
| **Frequency (Hz)** | Tidal volume | **INCREASE frequency = DECREASE tidal volume** (counterintuitive!) |

**The counterintuitive rule**: on HFOV, raising the frequency DECREASES tidal volume and may INCREASE PaCO₂ — the opposite of conventional ventilation where raising rate usually lowers PaCO₂.

---

## 5. Dysfunction / Pathophysiology

**When HFV is indicated:**
- **Air leaks (PIE, pneumothorax)**: HFJV preferred — passive exhalation + lower MAP = less gas trapping
- **Diffuse parenchymal disease (severe RDS, MAS)**: HFOV preferred — optimal lung recruitment at stable MAP
- **CDH**: HFOV often first-line — gentle ventilation with consistent MAP
- **PPHN**: HFOV often combined with iNO — optimal recruitment reduces intrapulmonary shunt
- **Refractory hypercarbia**: amplitude adjustment for CO₂ clearance

**When HFV fails:**
- Inadequate lung recruitment → persistent hypoxemia despite ↑ MAP
- Air trapping (especially HFOV in obstructive disease) → worsening hyperinflation
- Inadequate amplitude → persistent hypercarbia

---

## 6. Clinical Phenotype

**Infant on effective HFV:**
- Visible chest wall vibration ("wiggle" from clavicle to mid-abdomen)
- Stable SpO₂ on manageable FiO₂
- Blood gas: PaCO₂ in target range, adequate PaO₂
- CXR: 8–9 rib expansion (optimal recruitment)

**Trouble signs:**
- Loss of chest wiggle → ETT obstruction, circuit disconnect
- Hyperinflated CXR >9 ribs → air trapping → reduce MAP
- Progressive hypoxemia → under-recruitment or air leak → adjust MAP, check CXR
- Persistent hypercarbia → ↑ amplitude or ↓ frequency (to increase Vt)

---

## 7. Differential / Distinctions

**HFOV vs HFJV — Board-Critical Distinction:**

| Feature | HFOV | HFJV |
|---|---|---|
| Exhalation | **Active** (piston) | **Passive** (recoil) |
| Air leak/PIE | Less ideal (active exhalation can trap gas) | **Better** (passive exhalation allows gas escape) |
| Diffuse disease | **Better** (uniform recruitment) | Adequate |
| CDH | Common choice | Also used |
| Gas trapping risk | Higher | Lower |
| Conventional vent needed? | No (standalone) | Yes (tandem) |

**Key rule: HFJV for air leaks, HFOV for diffuse parenchymal disease**

**HFV vs Conventional:**
- HFV: Vt < dead space, minimizes volutrauma
- Conventional: larger Vt, more familiar settings, volume-targeted available
- HFV rescue when conventional fails (not first-line for most RDS)

---

## 8. Diagnosis

- HFV is a treatment modality, not a diagnosis
- **Indications**: failure of conventional ventilation, air leak syndromes, CDH, severe PPHN
- **Assessment on HFV**: blood gas, CXR (rib expansion 8–9 ribs), SpO₂, chest wiggle assessment

---

## 9. Management

**Starting HFOV:**
- MAP: start 1–2 cmH₂O above conventional MAP (or higher for recruitment)
- Amplitude (ΔP): adjust until visible chest vibration → titrate to PaCO₂
- Frequency: 10–15 Hz for preterm; 8–10 Hz for term
- FiO₂: titrate to SpO₂ target

**Starting HFJV:**
- PIP: 15–20 cmH₂O (lower than conventional)
- Rate: 420/min (7 Hz) typical starting rate
- PEEP: set on conventional vent (3–5 cmH₂O)
- IT: 0.02 sec (very short)
- Conventional sigh breaths: optional, rate 2–5/min

**Weaning HFV:**
- Oxygenation improving → ↓ MAP, ↓ FiO₂
- Ventilation improving → ↓ amplitude (HFOV) or ↓ PIP (HFJV)
- Transition to conventional vent when MAP <10–12 cmH₂O, FiO₂ <0.40
- Then wean conventional → extubate to NIV

---

## 10. Therapeutics

**HFOV + iNO Combination:**
- Commonly used together in severe PPHN
- HFOV optimizes lung recruitment → maximizes alveolar surface area exposed to iNO
- Synergistic: iNO vasodilates only ventilated regions → HFOV ensures more regions are ventilated
- Optimal pairing in: MAS + PPHN, CDH + PPHN, idiopathic PPHN

**HFV in specific diseases:**
- **[[Air Leak|PIE]]**: HFJV first-line — passive exhalation + low MAP → PIE resolves faster
- **[[Congenital Diaphragmatic Hernia|CDH]]**: HFOV common — gentle, consistent MAP without large pressure swings
- **[[Pulmonary Hypertension|PPHN]]**: HFOV + iNO often paired
- **[[RDS]] (refractory)**: rescue HFOV if conventional + surfactant failing

---

## 11. Complications / Outcomes

- **Air trapping**: especially on HFOV in obstructive disease; monitor CXR for overexpansion
- **Pneumothorax**: can still occur on HFV; reduced risk vs conventional in some studies
- **IVH**: concern about hemodynamic effects of high MAP; data inconclusive
- **Tracheal injury**: high gas velocities (HFJV) may dry airway → humidification essential
- **Necrotizing tracheobronchitis**: rare, associated with inadequate humidification on HFJV
- **Outcomes**: no consistent evidence that routine HFV reduces BPD vs optimal conventional ventilation; HFV is rescue, not primary prevention

---

## 12. Associations / Genetics / Maternal-Fetal Links

- No genetic associations — HFV is a treatment modality
- **Prematurity**: most common population for HFV use (RDS, PIE, BPD)
- **CDH**: HFV often first-line from birth
- **MAS/PPHN**: HFV + iNO combination in severe cases

---

## 13. Context / Board Framing

- **"HFJV vs HFOV for PIE?"** → HFJV — passive exhalation allows gas to escape; lower MAP; less gas trapping
- **"HFOV vs HFJV for diffuse disease?"** → HFOV — uniform recruitment, active exhalation maintains consistent MAP
- **"Increase frequency = ?"** → DECREASE tidal volume (counterintuitive!) — may INCREASE PaCO₂
- **"Oxygenation on HFV?"** → MAP + FiO₂ (same as conventional)
- **"Ventilation on HFV?"** → Amplitude (ΔP) — higher amplitude = more CO₂ clearance
- **"Vt on HFV?"** → Less than anatomic dead space — gas exchange via enhanced diffusion
- **"HFOV + iNO?"** → Synergistic — recruitment maximizes alveoli exposed to iNO

---

## 14. Edge Pearl

- **The frequency-Vt paradox**: on conventional ventilation, increasing rate usually improves CO₂ clearance (more breaths = more minute ventilation). On HFOV, increasing frequency DECREASES tidal volume because the piston has less time to oscillate fully — so PaCO₂ may actually RISE when you increase frequency. This is counterintuitive and is a classic board trap. The correct response to hypercarbia on HFOV: increase amplitude (ΔP) to deliver more CO₂ clearance per oscillation, NOT increase frequency. Similarly, HFJV is better for air leaks than HFOV because passive exhalation lets trapped gas escape through the leak site — active exhalation (HFOV) can paradoxically push more gas into the leak.
