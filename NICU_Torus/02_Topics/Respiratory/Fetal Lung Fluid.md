## 1. Identity

- **Fetal Lung Fluid (FLF)** is a chloride-rich fluid actively secreted by the fetal lung epithelium throughout gestation
- Serves as the mechanical distending force that drives lung growth during fetal life
- Must be rapidly cleared at birth to establish functional residual capacity (FRC) and enable gas exchange
- Failure of clearance → [[Extrauterine Pulmonary Transition]] failure; excess retained fluid → Transient Tachypnea of the Newborn (TTN)

---

## 2. Taxonomy / Developmental Placement

- Present from ~16–17 weeks gestation through birth
- Distinct from amniotic fluid (though some FLF is expelled into amniotic cavity via fetal breathing)
- Volume: **~30 mL/kg** at term (~150 mL total in a 3.5 kg infant)
- Rate of secretion: ~4–5 mL/kg/hr at term
- **Transitional phase**: FLF clearance begins hours before birth (labor onset) and continues through first hours after birth

---

## 3. Structure

**Composition:**
- High Cl⁻ (≈150 mEq/L), low protein, low bicarbonate — distinct from plasma ultrafiltrate
- Contains small amounts of [[Surfactant]] phospholipids shed from type II pneumocytes (used in amniocentesis L/S ratio)
- pH ~6.27 (relatively acidic compared to plasma)

**Secretion mechanism:**
- Driven by **Cl⁻ channels (CFTR and TMEM16A)** on the apical surface of lung epithelial cells
- Cl⁻ secreted into airspace lumen → creates osmotic gradient → water follows
- Na⁺ and water accompany Cl⁻ via paracellular routes and aquaporins

**Clearance mechanism (4 pathways):**
1. **Labor catecholamines** (epinephrine surge): β-adrenergic receptor → cAMP → activates ENaC; switches epithelium from Cl⁻ secretion to Na⁺ absorption
2. **ENaC (epithelial sodium channel)** activation: Na⁺ absorbed into interstitium → water follows; principal postnatal mechanism
3. **Lymphatic absorption**: interstitial fluid drained via pulmonary lymphatics (active during labor)
4. **Vascular absorption**: into pulmonary capillaries as PVR falls after birth

---

## 4. Function

- **Primary function**: distends developing airways → provides mechanical stimulus for branching morphogenesis and alveolar growth (see [[Lung Development]])
- Fetal tracheal occlusion (experimental/therapeutic) → ↑ FLF retention → ↑ lung growth (basis of FETO procedure in CDH)
- Fetal tracheal ligation → overgrowth; tracheal drainage → hypoplasia
- **Fetal breathing movements**: mix FLF in and out of the trachea; mechanical stimulus for growth; absence → [[Pulmonary Hypoplasia]]
- Small efflux into amniotic cavity → contributes to amniotic fluid volume; FLF lecithin/sphingomyelin ratio reflects lung maturity

---

## 5. Dysfunction / Pathophysiology

**Inadequate FLF (oligohydramnios/reduced production):**
- Reduced distension → [[Pulmonary Hypoplasia]]
- Causes: renal agenesis (Potter sequence), severe PPROM, bilateral renal dysplasia

**Failed clearance at birth:**
- Retained FLF fills airspaces → ↑ work of breathing, hypoxia, tachypnea
- **TTN (Transient Tachypnea of the Newborn)**: most common cause of respiratory distress in term/near-term
- Risk factors for failed clearance:
  - **Cesarean section without labor**: no catecholamine surge, no labor-mediated ENaC activation
  - Sedating maternal medications (suppress catecholamine response)
  - Prematurity: fewer ENaC channels, immature β-adrenergic signaling
  - Male sex, macrosomia (associated with TTN)

**ENaC dysfunction:**
- Genetic ENaC mutations → retained FLF → severe neonatal respiratory distress (rare)
- Prematurity → reduced ENaC expression → TTN-like presentation

---

## 6. Clinical Phenotype

**TTN:**
- Term or late preterm infant (especially C-section without labor)
- Tachypnea (RR >60), mild-moderate oxygen requirement
- CXR: hyperinflation, perihilar streaking, fluid in fissures, small pleural effusions
- Self-limited: usually resolves 24–72 hours
- Rarely requires >30–40% FiO₂; CPAP occasionally needed

**Pulmonary hypoplasia from reduced FLF distension:**
- Severe respiratory failure at birth
- Small lung volumes on CXR
- Bilateral: often lethal
- Associated with Potter sequence (oligohydramnios from renal agenesis)

---

## 7. Differential / Distinctions

| Feature | TTN | RDS | PPHN |
|---|---|---|---|
| Timing | Birth to hours | Birth, worsens | Birth or hours |
| GA | Term/late preterm | Preterm | Term/near-term |
| CXR | Wet, hyperinflated | Ground glass | Often normal |
| Course | Resolves 24–72h | Worsens without Rx | Variable |
| Mechanism | Retained FLF | Surfactant deficiency | Elevated PVR |

- TTN vs RDS: TTN improves with time; RDS worsens; CXR distinguishes
- TTN vs PPHN: TTN has no right-to-left shunt; PPHN has pre/post ductal SpO₂ difference

---

## 8. Diagnosis

- **TTN**: clinical + CXR (wet lung pattern); resolution within 72 hrs confirms diagnosis retrospectively
- **Lung ultrasound**: B-lines (comet tails) indicate retained interstitial fluid; becoming standard bedside tool
- **Blood gas**: mild hypoxia, possible CO₂ retention; rarely severe
- **Echocardiogram**: if concern for PPHN (right-to-left shunting at PDA/PFO)
- **Amniocentesis L/S ratio**: FLF-derived; L/S ≥2.0 indicates lung maturity (FLF is source of surfactant phospholipids in amniotic fluid)

---

## 9. Management

**TTN:**
- Supportive: supplemental O₂ (nasal cannula, HFNC, CPAP)
- NPO or minimal enteral feeds during tachypnea (RR >60–80: aspiration risk)
- IV fluids as needed
- Monitor for worsening (consider RDS, PPHN if not improving)
- Furosemide: NOT proven beneficial; not routinely recommended
- Most resolve without escalation

**Pulmonary hypoplasia:**
- See [[Pulmonary Hypoplasia]] note
- Aggressive respiratory support; often requires high-frequency ventilation, iNO

---

## 10. Therapeutics

- **Antenatal steroids**: upregulate ENaC expression → accelerate FLF clearance capacity at birth; reduce TTN severity
- **Elective C-section timing**: ACOG recommends ≥39 weeks for elective repeat C-section (reduces TTN/RDS risk)
- **FETO (fetal endoscopic tracheal occlusion)**: experimental for severe CDH — occludes trachea to retain FLF and stimulate lung growth
- **No role for diuretics** in routine TTN management

---

## 11. Complications / Outcomes

- **TTN**: excellent prognosis; fully resolves; very rarely progresses to respiratory failure
- **Emerging evidence**: infants with TTN may have higher rates of childhood asthma (retained fluid may have inflammatory effects)
- **Pulmonary hypoplasia**: mortality directly related to degree of hypoplasia; bilateral severe = near-universally fatal
- **PPROM with lung hypoplasia**: prolonged PPROM <24 weeks → >80% pulmonary hypoplasia if latency >5 weeks

---

## 12. Associations / Genetics / Maternal-Fetal Links

- **Maternal diabetes**: associated with delayed FLF clearance (insulin may impair catecholamine-driven ENaC activation)
- **Maternal asthma medications (β-agonists)**: theoretically may pre-activate ENaC; clinical significance unclear
- **PPROM**: loss of amniotic fluid → reduced fetal swallowing → net loss of lung fluid → [[Pulmonary Hypoplasia]]
- **Male sex**: higher TTN incidence; possibly related to androgen-mediated delay in ENaC maturation
- **ENaC gene mutations (SCNN1A/B/G)**: rare cause of neonatal respiratory distress

---

## 13. Context / Board Framing

- **"Why does C-section without labor cause TTN?"** → No catecholamine surge → ENaC not activated → FLF not absorbed → retained fluid = TTN
- **"What drives FLF secretion?"** → Chloride channels (CFTR, TMEM16A); Cl⁻ secretion osmotically draws water into airspace
- **"FLF volume at term?"** → ~30 mL/kg (~150 mL in a term infant)
- **"What activates ENaC at birth?"** → Epinephrine (labor catecholamine surge) → β-adrenergic → cAMP → ENaC → Na⁺ absorption → fluid clearance
- **"L/S ratio source?"** → Surfactant phospholipids from FLF expelled into amniotic cavity
- **"What happens if fetal breathing movements are absent?"** → No mechanical distension → [[Pulmonary Hypoplasia]]

---

## 14. Edge Pearl

- **FETO procedure exploits FLF physiology**: in severe left CDH, a balloon is placed endoscopically into the fetal trachea at 26–30 weeks to prevent FLF efflux → lung distension increases → lung grows against herniated bowel. Balloon is removed at ~34 weeks to allow surfactant accumulation. This is a direct clinical application of the understanding that FLF distension is the growth signal for the fetal lung.
