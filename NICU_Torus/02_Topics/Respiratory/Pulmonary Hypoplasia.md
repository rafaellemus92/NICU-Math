## 1. Identity

- **Pulmonary hypoplasia** is the incomplete development of the lung resulting in reduced lung weight, DNA content, and airway/alveolar count relative to body weight
- Bilateral, severe pulmonary hypoplasia is nearly universally lethal; unilateral hypoplasia may be compatible with life
- Not a primary lung defect in most cases — a **secondary consequence** of conditions that restrict lung growth during critical developmental windows
- Final common pathway: inadequate mechanical distension of the developing lung OR insufficient space for lung expansion

---

## 2. Taxonomy / Developmental Placement

- **Gestational axis**: Fetal — lung growth impairment occurs during fetal life, primarily in the **pseudoglandular** and **canalicular stages** (7–26 weeks)
- **Phase axis**: Fetal (growth defect develops) → Postnatal (clinical manifestation)
- Developmental ring: **Identity** — a quantitative failure of lung growth
- Relates directly to [[Fetal Lung Fluid]] (mechanical distension stimulus) and [[Lung Development]] (developmental stage vulnerability)

**Critical Window:**
- Lung most vulnerable to hypoplasia: **canalicular stage** (16–26 weeks) — airway branching continues and vascularization begins
- Pseudoglandular stage (7–16 weeks): airway generation reduction if compressive lesion present early
- Third trimester: mainly reduces alveolar multiplication; less severe if earlier stages relatively normal

---

## 3. Structure

**Normal Lung Growth Requirements:**
1. **Mechanical distension** by [[Fetal Lung Fluid]]: stretch signal activating branching morphogenesis
2. **Adequate intrathoracic space**: no space-occupying lesions
3. **Fetal breathing movements (FBMs)**: diaphragmatic activity generates cyclic compression/expansion; absent FBMs → hypoplasia
4. **Normal pulmonary blood flow**: vascular growth co-develops with airway growth; vascular insufficiency → alveolar simplification

**Quantitative Criteria for Pulmonary Hypoplasia:**
- Lung weight/body weight ratio: normal >0.012; hypoplasia <0.012
- DNA content per gram: reduced
- Radial alveolar count: reduced (cross-section of alveoli from respiratory bronchiole to pleura)
- Airway generation count: reduced in early-onset lesions

---

## 4. Function

- Reduced lung volume → insufficient gas-exchange surface area → inability to oxygenate at birth
- Pulmonary vasculature also underdeveloped → high PVR → PPHN (see [[Pulmonary Hypertension]])
- Surfactant production may be reduced proportionately (fewer type II cells)
- High compliance of chest wall + low lung volume → FRC extremely difficult to maintain

---

## 5. Dysfunction / Pathophysiology

**Four Mechanistic Categories:**

**1. Oligohydramnios (reduced amniotic fluid → reduced FLF production/pressure)**
- Renal agenesis (Potter sequence): no fetal urine → no amniotic fluid → no swallowing cycle → reduced FLF volume → no distension → most severe hypoplasia
- **Bilateral renal agenesis**: classic Potter sequence; bilateral hypoplasia; lethal
- **Bilateral renal dysplasia/multicystic kidneys**: similar but variable severity
- **Severe PPROM (<20 weeks)**: prolonged oligohydramnios → hypoplasia if latency >3–5 weeks
- Posterior urethral valves (males): severe oligo → significant hypoplasia

**2. Space-Occupying Intrathoracic Lesions**
- **CDH (Bochdalek)**: bowel herniation → compresses developing lung; most common cause in surgical context
- **CCAM/CPAM**: large lesion occupies space → compresses surrounding normal lung
- **Fetal hydrops**: pleural effusions → compression
- **Mediastinal masses**: thymoma, lymphangioma, bronchogenic cyst

**3. Absent or Reduced Fetal Breathing Movements (FBMs)**
- Normal FBMs: 30–60% of the time; cycling generates liquid flow → mechanical stimulus
- **Phrenic nerve agenesis/injury**: no diaphragm movement → no FBMs → hypoplasia despite normal fluid
- **Severe neuromuscular disease**: myotonic dystrophy (congenital), SMA type 0 → reduced FBMs
- **CNS anomalies**: anencephaly → absent central respiratory drive
- This mechanism is easily missed: infant can have **normal amniotic fluid volume** but still develop hypoplasia (no fluid distension cycling)

**4. Skeletal Dysplasias**
- Small chest syndromes: thoracic dystrophies → physically restrict lung expansion
- **Thanatophoric dysplasia**: most common lethal skeletal dysplasia; narrow thorax → bilateral hypoplasia; lethal
- **Jeune asphyxiating thoracic dystrophy**: narrow chest; variable severity; some compatible with life with respiratory support
- **Short rib-polydactyly syndromes**: typically lethal
- Mechanism: rigid, small chest → physically limits lung growth

---

## 6. Clinical Phenotype

**Presentation at Birth:**
- Severe respiratory failure immediately or within minutes of birth
- Requires high ventilator support from the start
- Significant PPHN (underdeveloped vascular bed)
- **CXR**: small lung volumes; narrow intercostal spaces; "bell-shaped" thorax in skeletal dysplasias
- Small thorax on physical exam; short chest in skeletal dysplasias

**Potter Sequence (classic):**
- Bilateral renal agenesis → oligohydramnios → fetal compression deformities:
  - **Pulmonary hypoplasia** (lethal component)
  - Facial deformation (flattened nose, retrognathia, low-set ears)
  - Limb positioning deformities (talipes equinovarus, hip dislocation)
  - Dry, leathery skin
- "POTTER" mnemonic: Pulmonary hypoplasia, Oligohydramnios, Twisted limbs, Twisted face, Ears anomalous, Renal agenesis
- Compatible with delivery but NOT with survival (lungs insufficient for gas exchange)

**Severity Spectrum:**
- Mild (unilateral): often incidentally found; compensatory hypertrophy of other lung; may survive well
- Moderate bilateral: severe respiratory failure; PPHN; may survive with aggressive support in some cases
- Severe bilateral (Potter, bilateral CDH): near-universally lethal

---

## 7. Differential / Distinctions

| Cause | Key Feature | Distinguishing Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Renal agenesis | Potter sequence | No kidneys on US; oligohydramnios; Potter facies |
| CDH | Bowel in chest | CXR: bowel in thorax; scaphoid abdomen |
| PPROM | Preterm, prolonged | Membrane rupture <20 weeks; prolonged latency |
| Skeletal dysplasia | Small chest, short ribs | Bone survey; molecular genetic testing |
| CCAM/CPAM | Lung mass | CXR/MRI: cystic/solid lung mass |
| NMD (myotonic dystrophy) | Floppy, hypotonic | Maternal myotonia; EMG/genetic testing |

- **Primary pulmonary hypoplasia** (rare, idiopathic): no identifiable cause; bilateral; lethal; diagnosis of exclusion
- **Unilateral pulmonary agenesis vs hypoplasia**: agenesis = complete absence of lung; hypoplasia = partial; both may be compatible with life if unilateral

---

## 8. Diagnosis

**Prenatal:**
- **Fetal ultrasound**: decreased amniotic fluid, absent or small kidneys, small thorax-to-abdomen ratio, lung-to-head ratio (LHR)
- **Fetal MRI**: accurate total lung volume measurement; gold standard for severity quantification
- **Total fetal lung volume (TFLV%)**: <35–40% predicted = severe hypoplasia; correlates with postnatal survival
- Karyotype/chromosomal microarray: for associated anomalies

**Postnatal:**
- **CXR**: lung volumes, rib anomalies, skeletal morphology, mediastinal shift
- **Blood gas**: severity of respiratory failure
- **Echocardiogram**: PH severity and type
- **Renal ultrasound**: look for absent/dysplastic kidneys (Potter)
- **Autopsy**: lung weight/body weight ratio, radial alveolar count, DNA content — definitive diagnosis (usually post-mortem for severe cases)

---

## 9. Management

**Prenatal intervention:**
- **Amnioinfusion**: in PPROM with oligohydramnios — serial infusions to restore fluid volume; limited evidence for preventing hypoplasia but may improve outcomes
- **FETO** (for CDH): retains FLF → stimulates growth; only for CDH-related space compression, not renal hypoplasia
- No proven prenatal therapy for renal-caused oligohydramnios (renal agenesis is irreversible)

**Postnatal support:**
- **High PIP/MAP**: required to recruit hypoplastic lungs; accept high pressures cautiously
- **HFOV**: often needed; allows oxygenation without excessive volutrauma
- **iNO**: for PPHN component; improves oxygenation if vascular bed is responsive
- **Permissive hypercapnia**: accept PaCO₂ 55–65 mmHg if needed to minimize barotrauma
- **ECMO**: may bridge severe cases; not helpful if lung hypoplasia is too severe for meaningful gas exchange recovery

**Palliative approach:**
- Bilateral severe pulmonary hypoplasia (Potter sequence, lethal skeletal dysplasia): prognosis universally dismal; compassionate/comfort-focused care is appropriate and should be offered and discussed prenatally when possible

---

## 10. Therapeutics

- **iNO**: if PPHN present and lung is adequately (even if minimally) gas-exchanging
- **Surfactant**: if surfactant deficiency component coexists (very preterm, or with RDS features)
- **Prostacyclin/sildenafil**: for chronic or refractory PPHN in hypoplasia survivors
- **No proven pharmacologic therapies** to grow new alveoli postnatally — alveolarization cannot be rescued pharmacologically once the critical window is past

---

## 11. Complications / Outcomes

- **Bilateral severe**: nearly universally fatal within hours to days
- **Moderate bilateral (CDH-related)**: 30–50% mortality; survivors have chronic lung disease
- **Unilateral**: usually survives; compensatory growth of contralateral lung (very limited); chronic mild respiratory issues
- **PPROM-associated hypoplasia**: variable; severity depends on GA at rupture and duration of oligohydramnios
  - PPROM <20 weeks with prolonged oligohydramnios (>5 weeks): >80% pulmonary hypoplasia
- **Skeletal dysplasias**: most are lethal (thanatophoric, short-rib syndromes); exceptions exist (Jeune — progressive disease with tracheostomy/long-term ventilation option)

---

## 12. Associations / Genetics / Maternal-Fetal Links

**Renal causes:**
- **Bilateral renal agenesis**: autosomal recessive (some); associated with RET, FGF signaling pathway mutations
- **Polycystic kidney disease (AR/AD)**: both forms can cause oligohydramnios; AR-PKD (PKHD1) more severe antenatally

**Skeletal dysplasias:**
- **Thanatophoric dysplasia**: FGFR3 gain-of-function mutation; autosomal dominant (de novo); always lethal
- **Jeune syndrome (JATD)**: IFT80, WDR19, TTC21B, and others (ciliopathy); AR; variable severity
- **Short-rib polydactyly**: various DYNC2H1, NEK1 mutations; lethal

**Neuromuscular:**
- **Congenital myotonic dystrophy** (CTG repeat expansion in DMPK): maternal transmission; hypotonic, reduced FBMs; hypoplasia
- **SMA type 0**: prenatal onset; absent FBMs; respiratory failure at birth

**Molecular:**
- FOXF1 mutations (also CDH, ACD): pleiotropic role in lung, gut, and diaphragm development

---

## 13. Context / Board Framing

- **"Potter sequence?"** → Bilateral renal agenesis → oligohydramnios → pulmonary hypoplasia + facial/limb deformities; LETHAL (lungs, not kidneys, cause death)
- **"How does oligohydramnios cause hypoplasia?"** → Reduced amniotic fluid → reduced fetal swallowing/lung fluid cycling → reduced [[Fetal Lung Fluid]] distension → reduced mechanical growth stimulus
- **"Why does absent FBM cause hypoplasia without oligohydramnios?"** → FBMs generate oscillatory stretch signal for airway branching; absent FBMs → hypoplasia even with normal fluid (phrenic nerve agenesis, neuromuscular disease)
- **"What is the lethal component of Potter sequence?"** → Pulmonary hypoplasia (NOT the renal agenesis — kidneys not needed postnatally with dialysis, but lungs cannot function if hypoplastic)
- **"PPROM and hypoplasia risk?"** → High risk if rupture <20 weeks with prolonged latency >5 weeks; ~80% hypoplasia
- **"Skeletal dysplasia + lethal?"** → Thanatophoric; FGFR3 mutation; narrow chest → bilateral hypoplasia → lethal

---

## 14. Edge Pearl

- **The fetal breathing movement trap**: pulmonary hypoplasia is almost always attributed to oligohydramnios or space-occupying lesions on boards and in clinical thinking. But a less recognized and clinically important cause is **absent fetal breathing movements due to neuromuscular disease**, particularly congenital myotonic dystrophy. A mother with myotonia (grip release difficulty, facial weakness, percussion myotonia) who delivers a profoundly hypotonic infant with respiratory failure at birth may have a baby with pulmonary hypoplasia from absent FBMs — despite completely normal amniotic fluid volume. The amniotic fluid volume can be entirely normal, yet the lung is hypoplastic because the mechanical cycling signal never occurred. This is a board-favored combination: maternal myotonic dystrophy → congenital myotonic dystrophy in infant → absent FBMs → pulmonary hypoplasia → respiratory failure.
