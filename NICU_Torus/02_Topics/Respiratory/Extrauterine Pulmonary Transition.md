## 1. Identity

- **Extrauterine pulmonary transition** is the rapid physiologic transformation of the fluid-filled, non-ventilated fetal lung into an air-breathing, gas-exchanging organ at birth
- Occurs within seconds to minutes of delivery; must succeed for survival
- Requires coordinated clearance of fetal lung fluid, establishment of FRC, and a dramatic fall in pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR)
- Failure of any component → respiratory distress, hypoxia, and persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn ([[Pulmonary Hypertension]])
- Directly parallels and is inseparable from **transitional cardiovascular physiology** (see [[PPHN Hemodynamics]])

---

## 2. Taxonomy / Developmental Placement

- **Phase axis**: Transitional — the bridge between fetal and postnatal physiology
- Begins with onset of labor (catecholamine surge) and is substantially complete within 1–6 hours of birth
- Dependent on developmental readiness: adequate surfactant (saccular/alveolar stage), adequate ENaC expression, intact vascular response to O₂ and vasoactive mediators
- Developmental ring: **Identity** — defines the moment of birth physiology

---

## 3. Structure

**Components of the transition:**

1. **Fetal lung fluid (FLF) clearance** (see [[Fetal Lung Fluid]])
   - Labor catecholamine surge → β₂-adrenergic receptor → ↑ cAMP → ENaC activation → Na⁺/fluid absorbed from airspaces
   - Vascular absorption as PVR falls (pulmonary capillary hydrostatic pressure drops → fluid drawn into vasculature)
   - Lymphatic drainage
   - Mechanical squeeze through birth canal (minor contribution in vaginal delivery)

2. **First breath mechanics**
   - Requires generation of negative intrathoracic pressure of **–40 to –80 cmH₂O** to overcome surface tension and viscous resistance of fluid-filled airways
   - Far greater effort than subsequent breaths; establishes initial FRC
   - [[Surfactant]] lowers surface tension, reducing required effort for subsequent breaths
   - Air-liquid interface replaces fluid-fluid interface → surface tension forces become critical

3. **FRC establishment**
   - Functional residual capacity: the lung volume at end-expiration (~30 mL/kg at term)
   - Surfactant prevents alveolar collapse between breaths → FRC maintained
   - Neonatal laryngeal braking (grunting) also maintains FRC by generating auto-PEEP

4. **Pulmonary vascular resistance fall**
   - In utero: PVR is high (hypoxia-driven vasoconstriction, compressed vessels, lack of vasoactive mediators)
   - At birth: **oxygen** is the most potent pulmonary vasodilator → PaO₂ rise triggers:
     - ↑ endothelial NO synthase (eNOS) → ↑ NO → cGMP → smooth muscle relaxation
     - ↑ prostacyclin (PGI₂) → cAMP → vasodilation
     - ↓ endothelin-1 (ET-1) influence (vasoconstrictive)
   - Physical lung inflation also mechanically dilates vessels
   - PVR falls to ~10–15% of systemic vascular resistance (SVR) within minutes to hours

---

## 4. Function

- Establishes gas exchange immediately after birth
- The fall in PVR → ↑ pulmonary blood flow → ↑ pulmonary venous return → ↑ left atrial pressure → functional closure of foramen ovale (PFO)
- Combined with rise in SVR (cord clamping) → eliminates right-to-left fetal shunts → serial circulation established
- **Cord clamping timing matters**: delayed cord clamping (DCC) allows placental transfusion (autotransfusion of ~20–30 mL/kg blood) while PVR-SVR transition occurs, buffering the hemodynamic shift
- Parallel → series circulation transition requires successful pulmonary transition

---

## 5. Dysfunction / Pathophysiology

**Failed FLF clearance:**
- TTN (transient tachypnea) — see [[Fetal Lung Fluid]]
- C-section without labor: no catecholamine surge → impaired ENaC activation

**Failed FRC establishment:**
- Surfactant deficiency (RDS) — inadequate surface tension reduction → alveolar collapse after each breath
- Apnea or failure to initiate breathing → no first breath → fluid-filled lung persists

**Failed PVR fall:**
- Persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn (**PPHN**): PVR remains high → continued right-to-left shunting → hypoxemia → [[Pulmonary Hypertension]]
- Triggers: hypoxia, acidosis, hypothermia, meconium aspiration, sepsis, CDH
- Self-perpetuating: hypoxia → vasoconstriction → more hypoxia

**Combined failure:**
- Most critically ill newborns have elements of all three — the transition fails globally
- Severely asphyxiated infants: airway fluid, no surfactant mobilization, acidosis → severe PPHN

---

## 6. Clinical Phenotype

- **Normal transition**: grunting/crying with first breath → rapid improvement over minutes; pink by 10 min
- **TTN**: tachypnea persisting >2 hours, self-limited, mild O₂ requirement
- **RDS**: progressive respiratory distress, grunting, flaring, retractions; worsens without surfactant
- **PPHN**: cyanosis disproportionate to lung disease; pre/post-ductal SpO₂ gradient; poor response to O₂ alone; echocardiogram confirms
- **Birth depression/asphyxia**: absent or weak respiratory effort; failure to initiate transition → NRP required

---

## 7. Differential / Distinctions

| Failure Type | Mechanism | Key Feature | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| TTN | Retained FLF | Self-limited, wet CXR | Supportive O₂ |
| RDS | Surfactant deficiency | Preterm, ground-glass CXR | Surfactant, CPAP |
| PPHN | Elevated PVR | Pre/post-ductal gradient, echo | iNO, optimize ventilation |
| Apnea/depression | No first breath | Low APGAR, no effort | NRP, PPV |

- Cardiovascular transition (foramen ovale, ductus arteriosus closure) is inseparable from pulmonary transition — see [[PPHN Hemodynamics]] and [[Transitional Circulation]]

---

## 8. Diagnosis

- **Clinical assessment**: respiratory rate, work of breathing, SpO₂, pre/post-ductal saturation difference
- **CXR**: lung volumes, pattern (wet = TTN, ground-glass = RDS, hyperinflated = air trapping, small = hypoplasia)
- **Blood gas**: determine adequacy of ventilation and oxygenation
- **Echocardiogram**: assess PVR (right ventricular pressure estimation), ductal/atrial level shunting direction
- **Lung ultrasound**: bedside assessment of lung aeration, fluid, pneumothorax

---

## 9. Management

**Immediate birth room support:**
- **Warm, dry, stimulate**: maintain temperature (hypothermia ↑ PVR, worsens transition)
- **Positive pressure ventilation (PPV)**: if no respiratory effort or HR <100 — NRP protocol
- **Sustained inflation lung recruitment**: controversial; SAIL trial showed no benefit; not routine
- **CPAP at delivery room**: reduces surfactant need, BPD in preterm; standard for VLBW infants

**FLF clearance support:**
- Supportive O₂ (nasal cannula, HFNC, CPAP) for TTN

**FRC support:**
- Surfactant for RDS; CPAP to stent alveoli open

**PVR reduction:**
- O₂: most important initial vasodilator
- Correct acidosis and hypothermia
- iNO: if PPHN confirmed (see [[Pulmonary Hypertension]])
- Mechanical ventilation: optimize lung volumes (atelectasis ↑ PVR; overdistension also ↑ PVR)

---

## 10. Therapeutics

- **iNO (inhaled nitric oxide)**: 20 ppm; selective pulmonary vasodilator; first-line for PPHN if term/near-term
- **Sildenafil (oral/IV)**: PDE5 inhibitor → ↑ cGMP; second-line PPHN; useful when iNO unavailable
- **Milrinone**: PDE3 inhibitor → ↑ cAMP; pulmonary + cardiac benefit in PPHN with RV dysfunction
- **Surfactant**: restores FRC, reduces hypoxia-driven PVR elevation in RDS-associated PPHN
- **ECMO**: rescue for refractory PPHN with reversible lung disease (not ACD — see [[Alveolar Capillary Dysplasia]])

---

## 11. Complications / Outcomes

- **Successful transition**: nearly all term infants; most well-supported preterm infants
- **PPHN**: mortality 10–20% despite therapy; significant neurodevelopmental morbidity (sensorineural hearing loss with iNO)
- **Hypoxic ischemic encephalopathy (HIE)**: complication of failed transition with asphyxia; requires therapeutic hypothermia
- **Air leak**: aggressive resuscitation, especially with meconium, can cause pneumothorax
- **BPD**: in preterm with RDS, poor transition may require prolonged ventilation → BPD

---

## 12. Associations / Genetics / Maternal-Fetal Links

- **Maternal sedatives/opioids**: suppress catecholamine response and fetal respiratory drive at birth
- **Maternal diabetes**: insulin inhibits catecholamine-driven ENaC activation; delayed FLF clearance
- **C-section without labor**: no labor catecholamine surge → impaired transition; elective C/S should be ≥39 weeks
- **Meconium aspiration**: chemical pneumonitis + surfactant inactivation + air trapping → combined transition failure
- **Sepsis (GBS, E. coli)**: endotoxin → pulmonary vasoconstriction → PPHN; impairs all three transition mechanisms
- **Perinatal asphyxia**: global failure of transition; leads to PPHN + HIE combination

---

## 13. Context / Board Framing

- **"What causes the fall in PVR at birth?"** → O₂ (primary), NO, prostacyclin; physical lung expansion
- **"Why is DCC beneficial?"** → Allows autotransfusion of placental blood while transition occurs; improves preterm Hgb, reduces IVH
- **"What is grunting?"** → Laryngeal braking to generate auto-PEEP and maintain FRC; sign of respiratory distress
- **"Why does C-section increase TTN?"** → No labor catecholamine surge → no ENaC activation → retained FLF
- **"What is the first breath pressure required?"** → –40 to –80 cmH₂O — explains why surfactant deficiency (↑ surface tension) makes subsequent breaths so costly
- **"Failed transition in CDH?"** → Pulmonary hypoplasia + abnormal vascular remodeling → PVR never falls normally

---

## 14. Edge Pearl

- **Oxygen is the most important pulmonary vasodilator at birth**, but it is a double-edged sword: in a premature infant, hyperoxia causes oxidative injury and can worsen outcomes. Current NRP guidelines recommend **room air (21%) as starting FiO₂** for term infants, with oxygen titrated by SpO₂ targets (60–65% at 1 min, rising to 85–95% by 10 min). For preterm <35 weeks, start at 21–30%. Over-oxygenation at birth is a preventable iatrogenic harm — the transitional pulmonary vasodilation can be achieved with modest oxygen targets that avoid oxidative toxicity.
