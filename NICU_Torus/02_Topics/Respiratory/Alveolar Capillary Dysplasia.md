## 1. Identity

- **Alveolar Capillary Dysplasia with Misalignment of Pulmonary Veins (ACD/MPV)** is a rare, lethal developmental disorder of the pulmonary vasculature presenting as refractory, irreversible pulmonary hypertension in term or near-term neonates
- Characterized by three histologic features: (1) reduced alveolar capillary density, (2) misalignment of pulmonary veins (veins run alongside arteries in bronchovascular bundles instead of interlobular septa), and (3) medial hypertrophy of pulmonary arterioles
- **FOXF1** gene mutation/deletion on chromosome 16q24.1 is the primary genetic cause
- Nearly universally fatal; does not respond to iNO, ECMO, or other pulmonary vasodilators in the long term
- Definitive diagnosis historically by autopsy; increasingly diagnosed by lung biopsy + genetic testing in life

---

## 2. Taxonomy / Developmental Placement

- **Gestational axis**: Postnatal — clinically presents after birth
- Developmentally: arrest of normal pulmonary vascular morphogenesis during fetal life
- Defect occurs during **canalicular-saccular stages** when pulmonary capillaries are being positioned adjacent to the alveolar epithelium and pulmonary veins are migrating to interlobular septa
- Developmental ring: **Edge** — ultra-rare, nearly always fatal, critical diagnostic distinction
- Must be considered in the differential for any term infant with **refractory PPHN unresponsive to all therapies**

---

## 3. Structure

**Histopathologic Triad (all three required for diagnosis):**

1. **Reduced/absent alveolar capillaries**: capillaries are present but displaced away from alveolar surfaces → no effective gas-blood interface → functional dead space despite air entry
2. **Misalignment of pulmonary veins**: pulmonary veins located in bronchovascular bundles alongside arteries (arteries, veins, and airways should be spatially separated; in ACD they are not)
   - "Arterialization" of the venous system: misaligned veins can be confused with arteries histologically
   - Results in increased resistance to pulmonary venous drainage → postcapillary hypertension
3. **Medial smooth muscle hypertrophy of pulmonary arterioles**: structural, not purely reactive

**Associated structural findings (some cases):**
- Dilated lymphatics
- Irregular alveolar development (alveolar simplification)
- Absent or markedly reduced type II pneumocytes in some areas
- Concomitant cardiovascular anomalies in ~50% (see section 12)

---

## 4. Function (Absent)

- Gas-blood interface is absent or severely reduced → cannot oxygenate regardless of ventilator support
- Misaligned pulmonary veins create a fixed post-capillary obstruction → venous hypertension → pulmonary arterial hypertension
- Smooth muscle hypertrophy → fixed arteriolar obstruction on top of venous obstruction
- iNO acts on precapillary arterioles via NO → cGMP mechanism; in ACD, post-capillary obstruction means even if arterioles dilate, blood cannot drain effectively
- ECMO can oxygenate the blood extracorporeally, but the underlying lung pathology is irreversible; ECMO does not treat the lung

---

## 5. Dysfunction / Pathophysiology

**FOXF1 Pathway:**
- FOXF1 (Forkhead Box F1) is a transcription factor expressed in pulmonary mesenchyme during fetal vascular development
- Required for normal positioning of pulmonary venous endothelium and capillarogenesis
- FOXF1 mutations (point mutations, deletions of 16q24.1) → impaired mesenchymal signaling → misalignment of veins + reduced capillary formation
- Inheritance:
  - Most cases: **de novo** or **maternal transmission** (genomic imprinting — FOXF1 region shows preferential maternal allele expression in some cases)
  - Point mutations and genomic deletions (16q24.1) are the two main molecular mechanisms

**Molecular consequence:**
- ↓ FOXF1 → ↓ downstream targets (VEGFR2, PECAM-1, other angiogenic factors) → capillary mispositioning
- Co-expression with SHH signaling pathway (explains why ACD can occur with intestinal malrotation and other anomalies — SHH pathway is pleiotropic)

**Why ECMO fails:**
- ACD is a developmental defect — no amount of time on ECMO will allow the lung to "recover" because the vascular anatomy is permanently incorrect
- Lung transplantation is the only potential curative therapy; donor availability and severity of associated anomalies make this almost never feasible in the neonatal period

---

## 6. Clinical Phenotype

**Presentation:**
- **Term or near-term infant** (occasionally late preterm): most important clinical clue
- **No prenatal risk factors** for PPHN: no meconium aspiration, no sepsis, no CDH, no significant prematurity
- Appears relatively normal at birth or has mild initial respiratory distress
- Develops **progressive, severe, labile hypoxemia** within hours to days
- **Responds transiently to iNO** initially (partial reactive component from medial hypertrophy) then becomes completely refractory
- Pre/post-ductal SpO₂ gradient (ductal right-to-left shunting)
- **Labile oxygenation**: extreme fluctuations with minimal handling

**Clinical Course:**
- Typical PPHN management (iNO, HFOV, sedation) → partial or transient response
- Escalation to ECMO → oxygenation supported but no lung improvement on subsequent echocardiograms
- Prolonged ECMO (weeks) without improvement → diagnosis suspected
- Death typically within days to weeks; rare survivors beyond 2 months without lung transplant

**"Delayed" presentation variant:**
- Some ACD cases have delayed onset of PH (days to weeks of life) — a recently recognized variant
- These infants may appear to stabilize then acutely deteriorate with refractory PH
- Mechanism: reactive vasoconstriction initially mild; structural disease becomes dominant

---

## 7. Differential / Distinctions

| Diagnosis | Key Distinguisher | Response to iNO | Prognosis |
|---|---|---|---|
| ACD/MPV | Term, refractory, FOXF1, biopsy | Transient then none | Lethal |
| Idiopathic PPHN | Term, reactive, no structural defect | Good (60–70%) | Good |
| CDH-associated PPHN | Bowel in chest, CXR | Partial | 60–80% survival |
| Pulmonary hypoplasia | Potter, CDH, skeletal dysplasia | Variable | Depends on severity |
| SP-B deficiency | Refractory RDS, not PPHN dominant | N/A | Lethal without transplant |
| Congenital heart disease | Echo shows structural defect | Varies | Surgical |

- **ACD vs idiopathic PPHN**: the critical distinction; ACD does not fully respond to vasodilators; lung biopsy differentiates
- **ACD vs SP-B deficiency**: SP-B deficiency presents as RDS (diffuse ground-glass), not primarily PPHN; lung biopsy distinguishes
- **ACD vs CDH**: CXR normal in ACD (no bowel in chest); CDH has clear radiographic diagnosis

---

## 8. Diagnosis

**Definitive Diagnosis:**

1. **Lung biopsy** (open or video-assisted thoracoscopic, or at autopsy):
   - Must be performed by experienced pathologist
   - Diagnosis requires all 3 histologic criteria: reduced capillaries, misaligned veins, medial hypertrophy
   - False negatives possible if sample misses involved areas (disease can be patchy)
   - Consider early in any term infant with refractory PPHN unresponsive to standard therapy

2. **Genetic testing** (FOXF1 gene sequencing + 16q24.1 deletion analysis by chromosomal microarray):
   - FOXF1 point mutations or 16q24.1 deletion in ~50–70% of ACD cases
   - Negative genetic test does NOT exclude ACD (30–50% have no identified mutation)
   - Testing also allows family counseling

**Supportive Investigations:**
- **CXR**: typically normal or non-specific; no ground-glass, no bowel in chest, normal heart size
- **Echo**: RV hypertension, dilated RA/RV, right-to-left ductal/atrial shunting; no structural cardiac defect
- **ABG**: profound hypoxemia; OI often >40 despite maximal therapy
- **CT chest**: may show abnormal vascular patterns; not diagnostic alone

---

## 9. Management

**Acute:**
- Standard PPHN management: iNO, HFOV, sedation/paralysis, hemodynamic support
- Expect partial or transient response only
- ECMO: indicated when OI >40 and reversible disease is possible — but ACD is NOT reversible; ECMO in ACD is a bridge to diagnosis (not to recovery)

**Diagnostic urgency:**
- Pursue lung biopsy early (within first week of ECMO if possible) to establish diagnosis
- If ACD confirmed: prognosis discussion with family; futility of continued ECMO support; transition to comfort care

**Palliative:**
- Once ACD confirmed and lung transplant not feasible, comfort-focused care is appropriate
- Family support, spiritual care, bereavement services

**Lung transplantation:**
- Theoretically curative
- Extremely limited by donor availability in neonatal period
- Associated anomalies often preclude transplant
- Very rare reports of neonatal lung transplant for ACD; most die before donor available

---

## 10. Therapeutics

- **iNO**: 20 ppm; initiated for PPHN regardless of suspected diagnosis; gives partial initial response in ACD due to reactive component
- **Sildenafil**: may be added; similarly limited by fixed anatomy
- **Milrinone**: supports RV function; does not address underlying pathology
- **ECMO**: VA-ECMO used; physiologically effective at oxygenation; does NOT treat ACD
- **No effective pharmacologic therapy exists** for the structural abnormality

---

## 11. Complications / Outcomes

- **Mortality**: >99% without lung transplant; most infants die within days to weeks of birth
- **Delayed variant**: slightly longer survival; still fatal
- **ECMO complications**: bleeding, thrombosis, infection; prolonged ECMO for irreversible disease adds harm without benefit
- **Neurodevelopmental**: irrelevant in practice (survival too short); rare survivors have significant impairment

**Recurrence risk (family counseling):**
- De novo FOXF1 mutations: recurrence risk low (~1–3%) for future pregnancies
- Familial cases (maternal or paternal inheritance): recurrence risk can be 25–50% depending on mechanism
- Chromosomal microarray + FOXF1 sequencing in parents → recurrence risk stratification
- Prenatal testing (chorionic villus sampling, amniocentesis) available in subsequent pregnancies if mutation identified

---

## 12. Associations / Genetics / Maternal-Fetal Links

**FOXF1 gene and 16q24.1 locus:**
- FOXF1: transcription factor in lung mesenchyme; also expressed in gut mesenchyme, urogenital tract
- Associated anomalies in ~50% of ACD cases:
  - **Cardiovascular**: ASD, VSD, AVSD, TAPVR, TGA — found in ~50%
  - **Gastrointestinal**: intestinal malrotation, atresia, Meckel's diverticulum — very common; pathway overlap with SHH
  - **Genitourinary**: renal anomalies, cryptorchidism
  - **Asplenia/polysplenia**: some cases

**Imprinting:**
- FOXF1 region (16q24.1) shows imprinting in some tissues: preferential expression of maternal allele
- De novo mutations arising on the paternal allele may not be expressed → explains some mutation-negative cases
- Deletion of maternal 16q24.1 → more severe phenotype than paternal deletion in some reports

**Genetic testing strategy:**
- Chromosomal microarray (detects 16q24.1 deletions)
- FOXF1 Sanger sequencing or gene panel (detects point mutations)
- Testing both parents for inherited variants

## 13. Context / Board Framing

- **"PPHN in term infant not responding to iNO?"** → Think ACD/MPV
- **"What gene is mutated in ACD?"** → FOXF1 on chromosome 16q24.1
- **"Three histologic findings in ACD?"** → Reduced alveolar capillary density + misaligned pulmonary veins (alongside arteries instead of in septa) + medial smooth muscle hypertrophy
- **"Why doesn't ECMO help in ACD?"** → ECMO oxygenates extracorporeally but the lung is structurally irreparable; veins will always be misaligned; cannot recover
- **"What is the inheritance pattern?"** → De novo most common; maternal transmission due to imprinting; 16q24.1 deletion or FOXF1 point mutation
- **"Diagnosis in life vs autopsy?"** → Lung biopsy (open or VATS) + genetic testing; definitive
- **"What associated anomalies?"** → Cardiac (50%), GI malrotation (common), genitourinary; explained by pleiotropic FOXF1/SHH expression
- **"Only cure?"** → Lung transplantation (essentially never feasible in neonatal period)

---

## 14. Edge Pearl

- **ACD is the "refractory PPHN trap"**: every neonatologist knows to try iNO, then sildenafil, then ECMO for PPHN. ACD exploits this algorithm — the infant responds transiently to iNO (reactive medial hypertrophy component), partially stabilizes on ECMO, and weeks pass before the diagnosis is made, often on autopsy. The board pearl is recognizing the pattern: **term infant, no risk factors, refractory PPHN, fails iNO and ECMO = ACD until proven otherwise**. The clinical action point is to perform **lung biopsy early** rather than continuing ECMO indefinitely, because earlier diagnosis allows timely and compassionate conversations with families about prognosis, preventing weeks of painful futile therapy. ACD is also important because **FOXF1 testing enables recurrence risk counseling** — a de novo mutation carries a low risk for future pregnancies, but a familial mutation can mean 25–50% risk, allowing prenatal diagnosis.
