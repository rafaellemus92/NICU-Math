## 1. Identity

- **Transient Tachypnea of the Newborn (TTN)** is delayed clearance of [[Fetal Lung Fluid]] due to failure of ENaC activation at birth
- Benign, self-limited respiratory distress in term or late-preterm infants
- Most common cause of respiratory distress in term/near-term newborns
- Resolves <48–72h as endogenous catecholamines and cortisol rise and ENaC activates
- The ENaC story: C/S without labor → no catecholamine surge → ENaC not inserted into apical membrane → Na⁺ cannot be absorbed from airspace → FLF stays

---

## 2. Taxonomy / Developmental Placement

- **Gestational axis**: Term/late-preterm — rarely seen in very preterm (their problem is surfactant, not ENaC alone)
- **Phase axis**: Transitional — a disorder of the birth transition
- Risk inversely proportional to exposure to labor (more labor = more catecholamines = more ENaC activation)
- Risk factors: **C/S without labor** (no catecholamine surge), maternal DM, macrosomia, fetal distress, maternal sedation
- Developmental ring: **Identity** — the prototype ENaC activation failure

---

## 3. Structure

**The ENaC Molecular Switch:**
- **Fetal state**: NKCC2-like Cl⁻ secretion on apical surface fills airspace → FLF distends lung → growth
- **Birth switch**: ENaC (apical gate) opens → Na⁺ flows from airspace into cell → Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase (basolateral engine) pumps Na⁺ to interstitium → water follows osmotically → FLF cleared
- **Same ENaC channel** exists in kidney collecting duct, controlled by aldosterone
- Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase is the basolateral engine — always on, requires ATP

**CXR Pattern:**
- Perihilar streaking (interstitial fluid along lymphatics)
- Fluid in horizontal fissure
- Mild cardiomegaly (volume overload)
- **Hyperinflation** (alveoli OPEN, just wet — not collapsed)
- **NO air bronchograms** — this is the key CXR distinction from [[RDS]]

---

## 4. Function

**Three ENaC triggers (fast → slow):**
1. **Catecholamines** (seconds): labor → epinephrine surge → β-adrenergic → cAMP → ENaC membrane insertion (trafficking from intracellular pool to apical surface)
2. **Cortisol** (hours): glucocorticoid receptor → gene transcription → ↑ ENaC protein synthesis
3. **Thyroid hormone** (days): structural maturation of channels and Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase

**Normal clearance timeline:**
- ~35% prenatal (cortisol/thyroid priming in late gestation)
- ~30% during labor (catecholamine surge — fastest, most important acutely)
- ~35% postnatal (continued ENaC activation + lymphatic drainage + capillary absorption)

---

## 5. Dysfunction / Pathophysiology

**The ENaC failure cascade:**
- Elective C/S → **no labor** → no catecholamine surge → ENaC remains dormant in intracellular pools → Na⁺ cannot be absorbed → FLF persists in airspace → respiratory distress
- Alveoli are OPEN (surfactant present) but FULL of fluid → wet lungs, not collapsed lungs
- **Maternal DM** delays BOTH surfactant maturation AND ENaC expression (insulin antagonizes cortisol maturation signal) → double hit: surfactant delay + ENaC delay
- Prematurity: immature ENaC expression + immature Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase → overlaps with RDS mechanism
- Rapid resolution <72h as:
  - Endogenous cortisol rises postnatally → gene-level ENaC transcription upregulates
  - Postnatal catecholamines normalize → cAMP-driven ENaC insertion continues
  - Visible as **postnatal diuresis** when ENaC finally fully activates

---

## 6. Clinical Phenotype

- **Tachypnea** (RR 60–100+), mild subcostal retractions
- Mild O₂ requirement (usually <40% FiO₂)
- **Rapid resolution <48–72h** — if not improving by 72h, reconsider diagnosis
- Onset: immediate postnatal → peaks 1–12h → resolves as ENaC activates
- CXR: perihilar streaking, fissure fluid, hyperinflation, NO air bronchograms
- May need CPAP; rarely needs intubation
- Afebrile, non-toxic appearance (unlike pneumonia)

---

## 7. Differential / Distinctions

| Feature | TTN | [[RDS]] | Pneumonia | [[Pulmonary Hypertension\|PPHN]] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GA | Term/late preterm | Preterm | Any | Term |
| CXR | Perihilar streaking, hyperinflation | Ground-glass, air bronchograms | Localized infiltrates | Often normal |
| Air bronchograms | **NO** | **YES** | Variable | No |
| Alveolar state | Open but wet | Collapsed | Exudative | Normal |
| Course | Resolves <72h | Worsens without Rx | Depends on Rx | Variable |
| Mechanism | ENaC failure | Surfactant deficiency | Infection | Elevated PVR |

**Key board distinction: Both RDS and TTN have wet lungs but different alveolar state:**
- **RDS**: collapsed alveoli (surfactant absent → high surface tension → LaPlace collapse)
- **TTN**: open alveoli (surfactant present, just fluid-filled → ENaC not clearing fluid)

---

## 8. Diagnosis

- **Clinical + CXR**: term/late preterm, tachypnea, perihilar streaking, fissure fluid, hyperinflation, NO air bronchograms
- **Resolution within 72h** confirms diagnosis retrospectively
- **Lung ultrasound**: bilateral B-lines (comet tails) = interstitial fluid; increasingly used as first-line
- **Blood gas**: mild hypoxia, possible mild CO₂ retention; rarely severe
- **Blood culture/CBC**: send to rule out sepsis/pneumonia (cannot distinguish clinically at onset)
- **Echo**: if concern for PPHN (pre/post-ductal gradient, labile sats)

---

## 9. Management

- **Supportive**: supplemental O₂ (nasal cannula, HFNC, CPAP 5–8 cmH₂O)
- **NPO or minimal feeds** during tachypnea (RR >60–80: aspiration risk)
- IV fluids as needed
- Monitor for non-resolution (consider RDS, pneumonia, PPHN if not improving by 72h)
- **Diuretics NOT helpful** — furosemide acts at the **loop of Henle**, not at alveolar ENaC — cannot directly clear FLF. Furosemide may venodilate but does not address the alveolar ENaC failure
- No surfactant needed (alveoli are open, not collapsed)
- Most resolve without escalation

---

## 10. Therapeutics

- **Antenatal betamethasone**: primes BOTH surfactant production AND ENaC transcription in one shot — reduces risk of TTN in late preterm deliveries
- **Elective C-section timing**: ≥39 weeks reduces TTN risk (more fetal cortisol maturation)
- **No pharmacologic treatment accelerates resolution** — must wait for endogenous ENaC activation
- CPAP: maintains FRC, splints open airways, supports gas exchange while FLF clears
- **Avoid diuretics**: mechanism does not target the correct channel

---

## 11. Complications / Outcomes

- **Excellent prognosis** — fully resolves; very rarely progresses to respiratory failure
- Small subset may require CPAP or brief intubation
- **No long-term pulmonary sequelae** in uncomplicated TTN
- Emerging evidence: infants with TTN may have higher rates of childhood wheezing/asthma
- Association with maternal DM may compound with DM-related surfactant delay in some cases

---

## 12. Associations / Genetics / Maternal-Fetal Links

- **C/S without labor**: strongest risk factor (no catecholamine surge → ENaC dormant)
- **Maternal DM**: insulin opposes cortisol → delays BOTH surfactant AND ENaC maturation
- **Late preterm (34–37 wk)**: immature ENaC + borderline surfactant → TTN-like or TTN/RDS overlap
- **Male sex**: higher incidence (androgen-mediated ENaC maturation delay)
- **Macrosomia**: associated (often with maternal DM)
- **ENaC gene mutations (SCNN1A/B/G)**: rare cause of more severe neonatal respiratory distress

---

## 13. Context / Board Framing

- **"C/S without labor causes what?"** → TTN — no catecholamine surge → ENaC dormant → FLF retained
- **"CXR of TTN vs RDS?"** → TTN: perihilar streaking + fissure fluid + hyperinflation + NO air bronchograms. RDS: ground-glass + air bronchograms + low volumes
- **"Why can't furosemide treat TTN?"** → Furosemide acts at loop of Henle, not alveolar ENaC — wrong channel, wrong organ compartment
- **"What does betamethasone do?"** → Primes BOTH surfactant AND ENaC simultaneously — one drug, two targets
- **"Resolution timeline?"** → <48–72h as endogenous cortisol rises → ENaC transcription activates
- **"Maternal DM connection?"** → Insulin antagonizes cortisol maturational signal → delays both surfactant AND ENaC
- **"Same channel where?"** → Kidney collecting duct — aldosterone controls ENaC there

---

## 14. Edge Pearl

- **The ENaC unification**: TTN and the recovery phase of RDS are the same ENaC story told at different gestational ages. In TTN (term), the alveoli are open (surfactant present) but wet (ENaC dormant). In RDS (preterm), the alveoli are collapsed (surfactant absent) AND wet (ENaC immature). The recovery diuresis at ~72h in RDS — when the baby "turns the corner" — is actually ENaC finally activating as endogenous cortisol rises postnatally. Betamethasone primes both surfactant production and ENaC transcription simultaneously, which is why antenatal steroids reduce BOTH RDS and TTN. The furosemide trap: boards love asking whether diuretics help TTN. The answer is no — furosemide inhibits NKCC2 at the loop of Henle, not ENaC at the alveolar surface. It's the wrong channel in the wrong organ.
