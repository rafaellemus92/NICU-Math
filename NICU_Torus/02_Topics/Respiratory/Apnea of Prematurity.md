## 1. Identity

- **Apnea of Prematurity (AOP)** is cessation of breathing ≥20 seconds OR shorter pause accompanied by bradycardia (<100 bpm) and/or oxygen desaturation in a preterm infant
- Reflects brainstem respiratory center immaturity
- Incidence inversely proportional to gestational age — nearly universal in <28 weeks
- Resolves as infant matures, typically ~36–37 weeks PMA
- Caffeine is first-line treatment and also reduces [[BPD]] and improves neurodevelopmental outcomes

---

## 2. Taxonomy / Developmental Placement

- **Gestational axis**: Preterm — a disorder of prematurity by definition
- **Phase axis**: Postnatal — manifests in first days of life, persists for weeks
- **Three types**:
  - **Central** (40%): brainstem immaturity → no respiratory effort
  - **Obstructive** (10%): pharyngeal collapse/flexion → effort but no airflow
  - **Mixed** (50%): **most common** — central pause followed by obstructive component
- Diagnosis of exclusion: must rule out underlying triggers before attributing to prematurity

---

## 3. Structure

**Brainstem Respiratory Centers:**
- Pre-Bötzinger complex (ventral medulla): primary respiratory rhythm generator
- Immature in preterm infants → irregular respiratory drive, exaggerated inhibitory responses
- Chemoreceptor immaturity: blunted CO₂ response, paradoxical hypoxic ventilatory depression (initial hyperventilation followed by apnea)
- Laryngeal chemoreceptor reflex: exaggerated in preterm → liquid in larynx triggers prolonged apnea (feeds, GER)

**Upper Airway:**
- Pharyngeal muscles immature → airway collapse during sleep
- Neck flexion worsens obstruction

---

## 4. Function

- Normal preterm respiratory pattern: periodic breathing (rhythmic pauses of 5–10 sec followed by rapid breathing, without desaturation) — this is NORMAL, not apnea
- **AOP vs periodic breathing**: periodic breathing = no desat/brady; AOP = with desat/brady
- Caffeine mechanism: adenosine receptor antagonist → ↑ respiratory drive, ↑ chemoreceptor sensitivity to CO₂, ↑ diaphragm contractility, anti-inflammatory properties → reduces BPD

---

## 5. Dysfunction / Pathophysiology

**Primary: brainstem immaturity**
- Immature pre-Bötzinger complex → irregular respiratory rhythm
- Exaggerated inhibitory reflexes (Hering-Breuer, laryngeal chemoreceptor)
- Blunted CO₂ ventilatory response → apnea threshold close to resting PCO₂

**Secondary triggers (must exclude):**
- **Infection/sepsis**: new-onset apnea in previously stable preterm = sepsis workup
- **IVH**: acute IVH can present as apnea
- **Anemia**: ↓ O₂ carrying capacity → brainstem hypoxia → apnea
- **GER**: laryngeal chemoreceptor reflex → apnea (controversial relationship)
- **Hypothermia/hyperthermia**
- **Metabolic**: hypoglycemia, electrolyte disturbances
- **Medications**: opioids, sedatives, magnesium
- **Airway obstruction**: neck flexion, secretions
- **Seizures**: apnea can be sole seizure manifestation in preterm (EEG)
- **NEC**: systemic inflammation trigger
- **PDA**: may exacerbate apnea (pulm edema → impaired gas exchange)

---

## 6. Clinical Phenotype

- **Timing**: first days of life → peak incidence early → persists weeks
- **Episode**: breathing pause → bradycardia → desaturation → color change
- Mild: self-resolves with stimulation
- Moderate: requires stimulation, may need brief increase in support
- Severe: requires bag-mask ventilation or reintubation
- Clustering: multiple episodes suggest underlying trigger (sepsis, IVH)
- **Resolution**: typically ~36–37 weeks PMA

---

## 7. Differential / Distinctions

| Feature | AOP | Periodic breathing | Seizure-related apnea |
|---|---|---|---|
| Desat/brady | Yes | No | Yes |
| Pattern | Irregular | Rhythmic cycling | Stereotyped |
| EEG | Normal | Normal | Epileptiform |
| Treatment | Caffeine | None | Antiepileptics |

- **New-onset apnea in previously stable preterm** = sepsis until proven otherwise
- **Apnea in term infant** = NOT AOP; evaluate for central cause (seizure, brainstem lesion, metabolic)
- **AOP vs obstructive sleep apnea**: AOP = prematurity; OSA = anatomic in older infants

---

## 8. Diagnosis

- **Clinical definition**: ≥20 sec cessation OR shorter with brady/desat in preterm
- **Cardiorespiratory monitoring**: continuous HR, SpO₂, impedance pneumography
- **Rule out secondary causes**: CBC, CRP, blood culture (sepsis); cranial US (IVH); blood gas (acidosis); glucose, electrolytes; consider EEG
- **Pneumogram/polysomnography**: if persistent or atypical; characterizes central vs obstructive
- **Feeding-related pattern**: suggests GER-associated or laryngeal chemoreceptor trigger

---

## 9. Management

**Pharmacologic:**
- **Caffeine citrate**: first-line
  - Loading dose: **20 mg/kg** (of caffeine citrate = 10 mg/kg caffeine base)
  - Maintenance: **5–10 mg/kg/day**
  - Start early (day 1–3 of life in <32 weeks) regardless of apnea status → BPD prevention
  - Continue until apnea-free AND ~33–35 weeks PMA (some continue longer)
  - CAP trial: caffeine reduces BPD (36% vs 47%), reduces PDA ligation, reduces MV duration, **improves neurodevelopmental outcomes at 18 months**
- **Theophylline**: obsolete — narrow therapeutic window, more side effects, no advantage

**Non-pharmacologic:**
- Position: slight head elevation, neutral neck position (avoid flexion)
- [[Non-Invasive Ventilation|CPAP/NIPPV]]: provides continuous distending pressure, splints upper airway
- **Nasal CPAP 5–6 cmH₂O**: effective for obstructive and mixed apnea
- **NIPPV**: may reduce apnea more than CPAP alone
- Reintubation: for severe refractory apnea despite caffeine + CPAP

**Home monitoring:**
- Controversial — no evidence that home monitors prevent SIDS
- Consider for infants with persistent apnea approaching discharge
- Resolution typically by 36–37 weeks PMA

---

## 10. Therapeutics

**Caffeine Pharmacology:**
- **Mechanism**: non-selective adenosine receptor antagonist (A₁ and A₂ₐ)
- Adenosine is endogenous respiratory depressant → caffeine blocks this inhibition
- Additional effects: ↑ CO₂ chemosensitivity, ↑ diaphragm contractility, ↑ minute ventilation
- **Anti-inflammatory**: reduces IL-6, TNF-α → may explain BPD reduction (beyond respiratory stimulation alone)
- **Half-life**: very long in neonates (~72–96h in first week, ~40h by 2 months) due to immature hepatic metabolism
- Therapeutic range: 5–20 mcg/mL (rarely need to check levels with standard dosing)

---

## 11. Complications / Outcomes

- **BPD**: caffeine reduces BPD (CAP trial — NNT ~6 in <1250g)
- **Neurodevelopmental**: caffeine improves neurodevelopmental outcomes at 18 months and 5 years (CAP trial)
- **No long-term pulmonary sequelae** from AOP itself
- **IVH risk**: apnea-associated hemodynamic instability may contribute to IVH
- **Feeding delays**: persistent apnea delays oral feeding advancement and discharge

---

## 12. Associations / Genetics / Maternal-Fetal Links

- **GA inversely proportional**: <28 wk ≈ universal; 34–35 wk ≈ uncommon
- **Maternal magnesium**: can depress neonatal respiratory drive (transient)
- **Anemia of prematurity**: ↓ O₂ delivery → brainstem hypoxia → apnea (transfusion may reduce)
- **PDA**: pulmonary edema from left-to-right shunt → impaired gas exchange → apnea
- **No specific genetic predisposition** — purely a maturational phenomenon

---

## 13. Context / Board Framing

- **"≥20 sec OR shorter with?"** → Brady/desat — this is the definition
- **"Most common type?"** → Mixed (50%) — central pause then obstructive
- **"Caffeine dose?"** → Load 20 mg/kg citrate → maintenance 5–10 mg/kg/day
- **"CAP trial?"** → Caffeine reduces BPD + improves neuro outcomes at 18 months
- **"New-onset apnea in stable preterm?"** → Sepsis workup
- **"Resolution?"** → ~36–37 weeks PMA
- **"Home monitors prevent SIDS?"** → No evidence
- **"Periodic breathing vs AOP?"** → Periodic breathing = no desat/brady (normal); AOP = with desat/brady

---

## 14. Edge Pearl

- **Caffeine is a triple-threat drug**: the CAP trial showed caffeine doesn't just reduce apnea — it reduces BPD (NNT ~6), reduces PDA ligation, and improves neurodevelopmental outcomes at 18 months and 5 years. The anti-inflammatory mechanism (adenosine receptor blockade reduces IL-6, TNF-α) may explain the BPD benefit more than the respiratory stimulant effect. This makes caffeine one of the few NICU interventions that simultaneously improves both pulmonary and neurologic outcomes. The board loves testing: "what single drug reduces both BPD and neurodevelopmental impairment?" Answer: caffeine.
